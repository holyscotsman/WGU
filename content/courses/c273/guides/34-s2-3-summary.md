# S2 3 Summary

## Lesson 3: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson.

## Socialization Across the Lifespan

Socialization is a lifelong process through which people learn society's rules and expectations, shaping how they see themselves and their place in the social world.

Biological factors may influence early tendencies, but social experiences play the central role in forming identity and the self.

Families, peers, schools, institutions, and media all act as agents of socialization, teaching norms and roles in different but connected ways.

According to the life course theory, expectations for behavior change as people move through childhood, adolescence, adulthood, and old age, reflecting society's age-based norms. Socialization unfolds over time and is shaped by timing, social relationships, and historical conditions.

## Gender and Socialization

Sex and gender are distinct concepts: sex refers to biological traits, while gender reflects social expectations about masculinity and femininity.

Gender roles are learned through socialization, with agents of socialization such as family, schools, peers, and media reinforcing ideas about how men and women "should" behave.

Gender identity refers to a person's internal sense of gender, while gender expression reflects how gender is displayed outwardly in everyday life.

Gender does not fit neatly into a man/woman binary; instead, it includes a range of identities shaped by social and cultural contexts.

## Sexual Orientation and Socialization

Sexual orientation describes patterns of attraction, not behavior. Social and cultural environments influence these patterns.

Gender and sexual identities can be fluid, changing over time and across different life experiences and social settings.

LGBTQIA+ terms provide language for diverse identities, and using respectful, evolving terminology supports understanding and inclusion in a changing society.
