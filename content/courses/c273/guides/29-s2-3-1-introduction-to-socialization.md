## Socialization and Self

Children are predisposed to different behavioral traits. For example, one child may be very active and quick to react emotionally, while another may be calmer and easier to soothe. When a child starts school, they must learn when to raise a hand, how to take turns, and how to behave in class. Teachers and classmates respond to these behaviors in different ways, offering praise, correction, or labels. Over time, the child may begin to see themselves as a good student, a troublemaker, or someone shy or outgoing.

This story illustrates socialization, the process of learning society's rules and expectations. Through socialization, people develop the self, meaning their understanding of who they are in relation to others. Social experiences play a significant role in shaping identity. People may be predisposed to behaviors such as impulsivity, but nurture will teach you that rather than shouting out an answer in a classroom setting, you need to raise your hand.

Key takeaway: Socialization is the process of learning society's rules and expectations and how people develop an understanding of who they are in relation to others. Who we are is influenced by both nature (biology) and nurture (social experiences).

## Agents of Socialization

If socialization explains how people learn society's rules and behaviors, who and what are responsible for teaching those rules? Sociologists answer this question by pointing to agents of socialization. Agents of socialization are the people, groups, technology, and institutions that teach individuals how to behave and what is expected of them in society. According to sociologists, there are four main categories of agents of socialization:

family

peer groups

institutions

mass/social media

## Family as Agents of Socialization

The family is usually the first agent of socialization an individual encounters in their life. For this reason, the family is called the primary agent of socialization. From birth, family members teach children how to communicate, behave around others, and understand right and wrong within their culture. Families shape early beliefs, values, and expectations, such as how to express emotions in a culturally acceptable way.

## Peer Groups as Agents of Socialization

Peer groups include friends and classmates who are similar in age and social status. They are especially influential during childhood and adolescence, when children begin to spend more time with peers than family. Peers influence behavior by reinforcing what is considered acceptable, popular, or normal within a group. Through peer interactions, people learn how to fit in, form relationships, and develop independence from family.

## Institutions as Agents of Socialization

An institution is a large formal organization within a society that teaches formal rules, roles, and expectations. Institutions are present in almost every society in the world. They may look different from place to place, but most societies have a system of education, family units, and religion.

Examples of institutions include education systems, workplace organizations, religious organizations, and government. For example, schools socialize children by setting expectations for behavior, achievement, and discipline, while workplaces teach professional norms and responsibilities. Teachers, supervisors, managers, and leaders act on behalf of these institutions to reinforce rules and expectations.

## Mass/Social Media as Agents of Socialization

Mass/social media is any form of communication that reaches a large population and includes television, movies, and online content such as social media and websites. The media can expose people to ideas about success, relationships, gender roles, social class, and identity. It can reinforce expectations or challenge them by presenting new perspectives. Because the media is widely accessible, it plays a powerful role in shaping beliefs and expectations.

However, increases in communication technology and greater access to sharing have led to growing issues around misinformation, inaccurate AI content, and misleading or deceptive portrayals of others.

Key takeaway: Family, peers, institutions, and mass/social media teach the rules and expectations in society.
