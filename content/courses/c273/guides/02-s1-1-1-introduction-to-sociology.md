What Is Sociology? Have you ever wondered how some of your colleagues and peers have built extensive personal and professional networks that help them get ahead, while others struggle to navigate school or workplace environments? Have you ever wondered why people in India often wear white to funerals, while Americans tend to wear black? These questions and many others can be answered through the study of sociology.

This graphic presents a definition of sociology. It includes the term sociology followed by a description explaining that it is the study of groups, group interactions, societies, and social interactions. An icon of three connected figures visually represents relationships and social connections.

Because societies are a central focus of sociology, sociologists define a society as a group of people who live in a defined geographic area, interact with one another, and share common practices, beliefs, and values. Key takeaway: Sociology is the scientific and systematic study of groups and group interactions, societies, and social interactions.

Why Learn About Sociology? Sociology can be exciting because it teaches people ways to recognize how they fit into the world and how others perceive them. Looking at themselves and society from a sociological perspective helps people see where they connect to different groups based on the many different ways they classify themselves and how society classifies them in turn. For example, a person may choose not to go to the doctor because they "do not like hospitals," but using the sociological perspective, a sociologist may point out how lack of insurance, transportation, paid time off, or trust in medical institutions influences that decision. Key takeaway: The sociological perspective is the lens sociologists (and those studying sociology) use to examine social life and society.

## Sociology As a Science

Instead of relying on opinion or common sense, sociologists approach the study of society using systematic research methods to collect and analyze data. They then use that data to identify patterns in social life. This scientific approach allows sociologists to explain how social forces (societal patterns and structures) influence behavior in consistent ways. For example, if many students struggle in school, sociology looks beyond individual effort to examine factors such as school funding, neighborhood resources, and access to support services.

How Does Sociology Differ from Other Social Sciences? Sociology is closely linked with other social sciences, such as psychology (the study of the mind and behavior) and anthropology (the study of humans and human cultures, past and present), but its focus is different. Sociology focuses on society and groups by examining the lives and work of everyday people and how people are influenced by factors outside themselves, such as family, school, work, laws, and cultural expectations. Psychology focuses on the individual. It looks at how people think, feel, and behave on an individual level. Anthropology focuses on culture. It studies how people live in different societies, often comparing traditions, beliefs, and daily practices across cultures.
