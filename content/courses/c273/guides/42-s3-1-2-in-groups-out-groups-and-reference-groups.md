## In-Groups and Out-Groups

Groups influence how individuals see themselves and others, as well as how they behave in social situations. Sociologists focus on group membership—the inclusion or exclusion from a social group —to understand how inclusion and exclusion shape identity, interaction, and behavior.

Think about a time when you felt like you really belonged; perhaps on a work team, in a friend group, or as a fan of a favorite sports team. That sense of "us" often becomes clearer when there is also a sense of "them." Sociologists use the concepts of in-group (groups we feel we belong to) and out- group (a group we feel we do not belong to or even are in opposition to) to explain how group membership shapes identity and behavior.

## The Role of In-Groups and Out-Groups in Socialization

In-groups and out-groups shape how people see themselves, how they relate to others, and how they behave in social settings. Both in-groups and out-groups play an important role in socialization.

Individuals want to align with their in-groups for guidance on behavior, language, and even values.

Individuals may use their out-groups with opposing viewpoints to know which behaviors, language, and beliefs not to follow.

Key takeaway: In-groups and out-groups describe how people define who belongs and who does not, shaping identity, norms, and social behavior. Understanding these concepts helps you understand how belonging, identity, and exclusion shape your everyday life. It helps illustrate where divisive thinking stems and how stereotypes and bias can form. Recognizing how group bias can influence your ideas, decisions, or opinions can help you prevent treating others unfairly and avoid conflicts.

## Reference Groups

Imagine starting a new job and paying close attention to how coworkers dress, speak, or interact with supervisors. Even before anyone corrects you, you begin adjusting your behavior to fit what seems "normal." Sociologists use the concept of reference groups to explain this process. A reference group is a group that individuals use as a standard for evaluating themselves, their behavior, attitudes, or beliefs.

A reference group does not require direct membership. People may compare themselves to friends, coworkers, classmates, group fitness members, celebrities, or professional groups, even if they are not formally part of those groups.

## The Function of Reference Groups

Reference groups are important because they reinforce social norms and expectations and help individuals decide what is acceptable, desirable, or successful in a particular social context.

Key takeaway: Reference groups, even without direct membership, provide the benchmarks and contrasts needed for comparison and evaluation of group and personal characteristics. Recognizing reference groups helps you analyze how social expectations influence behavior and identify why individuals conform to certain norms in everyday situations and assessments.

## Key

group membership: belonging to a social group based on shared characteristics, roles, identities, or relationships

in-group: a group that a person identifies with and feels a sense of belonging to

out-group: a group that a person does not identify with and does not feel a sense of belonging to

reference group: a group an individual uses as a reference point for themselves
