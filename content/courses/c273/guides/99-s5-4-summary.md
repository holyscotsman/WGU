# S5 4 Summary

## Lesson 4: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson. Collective behavior refers to voluntary, noninstitutionalized group activity that emerges outside formal social structures. Crowds are a form of collective behavior characterized by physical proximity, temporary interaction, shared focus, and shifting norms that guide behavior. Emergent norm theory explains that crowd behavior develops through new, situation-specific norms rather than irrational "mob mentality." Social movements can be categorized as alternative, reform, religious/redemptive, resistance, or revolutionary based on the scope and target of change. Social movement theories explain how movements form, gain support, and achieve change. value-added (structural strain) theory declining privilege theory resource mobilization theory frame analysis (framing theory) political opportunity theory new social movement theory relative deprivation theory Social change occurs not only through collective behavior and social movements but also through factors such as technology, population shifts, diffusion of ideas, and environmental or economic pressures.
