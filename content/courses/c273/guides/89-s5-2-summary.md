# S5 2 Summary

## Lesson 2: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson. Religion is a social institution defined by shared beliefs, values, and practices centered on what is considered sacred. Major world religions developed in different regions and continue to shape social life, cultural traditions, and moral expectations. Religious beliefs influence social values, social norms, and individual behavior within societies. Religious organizations vary in structure, size, and social acceptance, ranging from cults and sects to churches and denominations. Religious belief systems such as polytheism, monotheism, animism, and totemism differ in what is considered divine and shape social organization and worldview. Sociological perspectives on the institution of religion: The functionalist perspective views religion as contributing to social cohesion, moral guidance, and stability, while also recognizing potential dysfunctions. The conflict perspective examines how religion can reinforce power structures and inequality, while also serving as a potential force for social change. The symbolic interactionist perspective focuses on how religious symbols, rituals, and shared meanings are created and interpreted in everyday life. Educational systems around the world reflect cultural values, economic conditions, and societal priorities. Education in the United States is a decentralized social institution that structures opportunity, mobility, and life chances. Sociological perspectives on the institution of education: The functionalist perspective views education as promoting socialization, skill development, and social stability. The conflict perspective views education as contributing to social inequality and reinforcing stratification. The symbolic interactionist perspective examines how everyday interactions, labeling, and expectations shape educational experiences and outcomes.
