## Gender Identity and Gender Expression

While gender roles describe social norms, individuals also experience gender on a personal level. Gender identity refers to an individual's deeply held internal perception of their gender. A person's gender identity may or may not align with the sex they were assigned at birth, and it is not determined by outward appearance or behavior. Gender identity is distinct from gender expression, which refers to how individuals present their gender to others through clothing, hairstyle, mannerisms, and speech. Cultural norms shape gender expression and vary widely across societies and historical periods. Because expectations for masculinity and femininity differ, the same behavior may be interpreted differently depending on the social context.

Having Gender or Doing Gender?

Sociologists who study symbolic interactionism emphasize that gender is not simply something people have; it is something they perform in everyday life. This idea is captured in the concept of doing gender. As sociologists West and Zimmerman explain, individuals are constantly "doing gender" through routine interactions, such as how they dress, speak, or use gender-marked spaces. Gender, like culture, is a social production and relies on societal participation. Importantly, gender identity, gender expression, and gender roles do not always align. A person may identify as one gender while expressing themselves in ways that challenge traditional expectations. This separation reinforces the sociological understanding that gender is socially constructed and maintained through interaction rather than dictated by biology. Key takeaway: Gender identity is a person's internal perception of their gender. Gender expression refers to a person's outward presentation. Gender is not something that individuals "have"; it is something that they "do."

## Gender Diversity

In addition to variation in biological sex, sociologists recognize significant gender diversity, or differences in how people understand, identify, and express gender. While many individuals identify as men or women, others experience gender in ways that do not fit neatly within these categories. These experiences highlight that gender is not limited to a single, universal pattern. Some individuals identify as nonbinary, meaning their gender identity does not align exclusively with the categories of man or woman. Others identify as transgender, describing people whose gender identity does not correspond with the sex they were assigned at birth. Transgender identities reflect a wide range of lived experiences and are not defined by appearance, behavior, or sexual orientation. Gender nonconformance occurs when a person's gender expression, such as clothing, behavior, or mannerisms, does not align with societal expectations associated with their perceived sex. Together, nonbinary identities, transgender identities, and gender nonconformance demonstrate that gender diversity is a normal part of human social life and varies across cultures and historical contexts.
