## Lesson 1 Introduction

Every day, you follow rules and expectations, often without even thinking about them. For example, when someone asks, "How are you?" you probably respond with a short, polite answer rather than sharing every detail of your day. These shared beliefs and values are part of the concept called culture. In this lesson, you will learn what culture is and how it shapes the way people think, communicate, and behave. You will explore how culture is created and shared within groups and how it can change over time. You will also examine how both biology and the social environment influence behavior.

## Learning Objectives

Consider the following learning objectives as you move through the lesson. Identify the cultural outcomes of processes and groups. Explain biological and sociological approaches to behavior.

## Defining Culture

Shared expectations and patterns of behavior are components of culture. Culture is when the following are shared in a society:

- beliefs (ideas people accept as true)
- values (what a society sees as right or wrong)

norms (the visible and invisible rules of conduct through which societies are structured) language (a structured system of symbols that enables a society to communicate) symbols (anything, such as a gesture, object, or image, that carries shared meaning among members of a society) patterns of behavior (practices of a group) Culture is shared by members of society and passed from one generation to the next. For example, in U.S culture, as stated above, the question "How are you?" is often used as a friendly greeting, not a request for a full update, unless, of course, you are communicating with your friends.
