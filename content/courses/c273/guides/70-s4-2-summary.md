# S4 2 Summary

## Lesson 2: Summary

Race is a socially constructed category used to classify people based on perceived physical traits (such as skin color).

Racial fluidity refers to how definitions and classifications of race shift across historical periods, social contexts, and geographic locations.

Ethnicity refers to shared cultural characteristics, such as language, heritage, religion, traditions, or nationality, that shape a group's identity.

A dominant group holds greater power, privilege, and social influence in a society, while a minority group has less power and may experience unequal treatment regardless of its population size.

Stereotypes, prejudice, and discrimination lead to inequality.

Stereotypes are oversimplified beliefs about a group.

Prejudice is a negative attitude or judgment toward a group.

Discrimination is behavior or actions that unfairly treat people differently, often producing social inequality.

Sociologists explain prejudice in different ways, including conflict perspective (competition over resources), scapegoat theory (blaming a group for problems), culture theory (learning prejudice through socialization), and intersectionality (how overlapping social identities shape experiences of prejudice).

Institutional racism refers to discriminatory policies or practices within organizations (such as schools or workplaces), while systemic racism describes broader patterns of racial inequality embedded across multiple social institutions.

Sociologists describe four main patterns of group relations:

assimilation (minority groups adopt the dominant culture)

segregation (groups are kept separate)

genocide (intentional destruction of a group)

pluralism (groups maintain cultural differences while coexisting in society)
