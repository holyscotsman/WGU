# S4 3 Introduction

## Lesson 3 Introduction

The United States views gender in a rigid binary system, meaning what is labeled "boys or men" is distinctly different than what is labeled "girls or women," with little to no acknowledgment of overlap between the two. Children are gendered before they are born, with gender reveal celebrations and nursery decorations signifying the gender they "should" assume.

In this lesson, you will examine how societies understand sex, gender, and sexuality and how these ideas influence education, work, family life, and social expectations. You will also explore how differences in power and opportunity can emerge from these social patterns.

## Learning Objectives

Consider the following learning objectives as you move through the lesson.

Identify outcomes tied to differences across gender and sexuality.

Distinguish various sociological perspectives of gender stratification.
