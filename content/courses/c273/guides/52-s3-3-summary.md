# S3 3 Summary

## Lesson 3: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson. Deviance refers to behaviors, beliefs, or characteristics that violate social norms, and what is considered deviant is socially constructed based on culture, time, situation, audience, and power. Crime is a specific type of deviance that involves violating formal laws, and what is defined as criminal depends on how societies create and enforce laws. Societies use sanctions as a form of social control, with positive and negative sanctions encouraging conformity and discouraging deviance through formal and informal means. From the functionalist perspective, deviance is a normal part of society that helps define boundaries, reinforce norms, and promote social change. The symbolic interactionist perspective explains deviance as socially constructed, and labeling theory shows how social reactions can shape identity and lead to continued deviant behavior. The conflict perspective explains deviance and crime as outcomes of unequal access to resources and power, with laws and enforcement reflecting the interests of powerful groups.
