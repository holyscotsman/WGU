## Sociological Perspectives and Theories

Sociologists do not all study society in the same way. Instead, they use sociological perspectives, which are lenses or ways of looking at society. Perspectives represent different vantage points for studying the social world. Sociologists use multiple perspectives but recognize that neither perspective is correct or incorrect. Sociologists build theories within these perspectives to help explain what they observe. A theory is an explanation for how and why social life works the way it does, and it can be used to create a testable idea. In other words, theories help sociologists move beyond broad perspectives and use evidence to support their explanations.

Macro- and Micro-Level Perspectives and Theories Sociological perspectives operate at different levels of analysis. Each perspective is usually associated with either a macro-level (big picture) or micro-level (small-scale) analysis.

## Example

The big picture, including how large systems such as education, government, family, and the economy shape society and influence people's lives

Studying how the education system affects students' opportunities based on school funding, neighborhood resources, and graduation rates across entire communities

## Micro Level

Small-scale, everyday interactions, such as conversations, relationships, and how people communicate in social settings

Studying how two students interpret an emoji in a text message (e.g., thumbs-up) and how that interpretation changes their conversation (whether it feels supportive or rude)

Key takeaway: Sociological perspectives are broad frameworks, and each perspective includes multiple theories to explain using evidence. Perspectives and theories can be applied at either the macro level (large systems shaping society) or the micro level (everyday interactions and communication). Using perspectives can help you understand beliefs you disagree with and find common ground.

## Sociological Perspectives in This Course

In this course, you will explore three sociological perspectives: the functionalist perspective, the conflict perspective, and the symbolic interactionist perspective.

This infographic presents three major sociological perspectives and their core ideas. The functionalist perspective

This infographic presents three major sociological perspectives and their core ideas. The functionalist perspective explains society as a system of parts working together to maintain stability. The conflict perspective highlights inequality and competition for power and resources. The symbolic interactionist perspective focuses on how society is created through everyday interactions and shared meaning.

Why Use Multiple Perspectives? Different perspectives often lead sociologists to ask different questions about the same topic. For example, one perspective might focus on how schools help society stay stable, while another might focus on how schools contribute to inequality. Using multiple perspectives helps provide a richer understanding of society because it shows that social life can be interpreted in more than one way. Key takeaway: Sociologists explain how society influences outcomes using three major perspectives: the functionalist perspective, the conflict perspective, and the symbolic interactionist perspective. Using multiple perspectives broadens the understanding of society.

## Key Terms

theory: an evidence-based explanation of how and why social life works that can be tested micro-level perspective: a way of studying society by focusing on small-scale, everyday interactions between people macro-level perspective: a way of studying society by focusing on large social systems and structures that shape people's lives functionalist perspective: a view that society is made of interdependent parts that work together to maintain stability and order conflict perspective: a view that society is shaped by inequality and ongoing competition for power and resources symbolic interactionalist perspective: a view that society is created through daily interactions and the shared meanings people give to symbols and behavior
