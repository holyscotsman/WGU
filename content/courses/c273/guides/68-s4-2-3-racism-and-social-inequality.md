## Individual Racism and Bias

Racism is a belief system used to justify inequalities against individuals by maintaining that one racial category is superior or inferior to others. It is a set of practices used by a dominant racial group to maximize advantages for itself by disadvantaging minority racial groups.

Individual racism refers to prejudice and discrimination carried out by individuals, consciously and unconsciously, that occurs between individuals. Examples include telling a racist joke and believing in the superiority of White people.

## Individual Racism Shaped by Bias

Individual racism is often shaped by bias, which can operate either consciously or unconsciously.

Explicit bias is a conscious belief or attitude about a group of people. Individuals knowingly judge or treat others differently based on characteristics such as race, gender, religion, or age. For example, a person openly expressing prejudice toward a racial group or intentionally treating someone differently because of their identity.

Implicit bias refers to unconscious attitudes or stereotypes about groups of people that influence decisions and behavior without a person realizing it. For example, a teacher may call on boys more often in class, or a doctor may assume that a woman is exaggerating her pain.

## Key Characteristics Implicit Bias

The following points summarize key characteristics of implicit bias and help you identify how it operates and why it matters:

Everyone has implicit biases, including individuals who aim to be fair and impartial.

Implicit biases differ from explicit biases; implicit biases operate unconsciously, while explicit biases are conscious and stated.

Implicit and explicit biases can influence and reinforce each other.

Implicit biases are often directed toward out-groups, but they can also apply to in-groups.

Implicit biases can shape behavior, even when a person is not aware of them.

Implicit biases can change over time, meaning learned associations can be unlearned and replaced with new ones through intentional effort.

Implicit bias can be unlearned and reduced through self-reflection and increased awareness, which support the development of new associations.

## Institutional and Systemic Racism

Racism can also operate at levels beyond individual attitudes or actions. Sociologists distinguish between institutional racism and systemic racism to explain how racial inequality can be built into social systems.

Institutional racism: These are policies or practices within an organization that create unequal outcomes for racial groups. Public schools in the United States are often funded by local property taxes, which means schools in wealthier areas receive more funding than schools in poorer communities, where students are more likely to be racial minorities.

Systemic racism: The broader, large-scale, long-term patterns that produce racial inequality stemming from policies implemented across many interconnected institutions. For example, historical policies such as redlining limited where Black families could buy homes, which affected wealth, school quality, and neighborhood opportunities across generations.

## Example: Race and the Law

Institutional and systemic racism often operate through major institutions, including the legal system. Throughout history, laws in the United States have shaped racial inequality by deciding who could vote, where people could live, and who had full rights as citizens. These laws sometimes enforced racial hierarchy and allowed discrimination to continue across generations. Even when laws aim to promote equality, how they are enforced can still lead to unequal outcomes, such as differences in policing, sentencing, or access to legal resources. Policies like affirmative action were created to address these inequalities and continue to shape debates about fairness and opportunity.

## Key

racism: prejudice or discrimination used to justify inequalities against individuals by maintaining that one racial group is superior

individual racism: prejudice or discrimination from one person to another

explicit bias: a conscious belief that someone holds about a group of people

implicit bias: an unconscious belief that someone holds about a group of people

systemic racism: the belief systems that support racist policies and practices

institutional racism: policies or practices enacted by institutions toward entire groupings of people

affirmative action: policies created to counteract society's past effects of discrimination in institutions and increase the representation of historically discriminated groups
