# S4 3 Summary

## Lesson 3: Summary

## Gender Inequality

Gender inequality develops through social processes that assign different expectations, roles, and opportunities based on perceived gender from an early age. Family interactions and educational environments reinforce gender norms by shaping behaviors, expectations, and beliefs about appropriate roles. Gender stratification results in measurable inequalities in work, income, leadership, and political representation, including patterns such as the second shift, pay gaps, and barriers to advancement. Sexual harassment reflects and reinforces gender inequality by maintaining power imbalances and limiting equitable participation in social and professional settings.

## Sexual Inequality

Sexual inequality occurs when heteronormative beliefs position heterosexuality as the standard, which can marginalize individuals with other sexual orientations and lead to stigma, exclusion, and unequal treatment. Sexual inequality can negatively affect mental health and self-concept, as repeated exposure to stigma, discrimination, and social rejection is associated with higher rates of depression, anxiety, and suicide risk.

## Sociological Perspectives on Gender Inequality

The functionalist perspective explains gender inequality as a result of traditional role division that was seen as necessary for maintaining family stability, even though these roles may no longer reflect current social conditions. The conflict perspective explains gender inequality as an outcome of power differences, where dominant groups maintain control over resources and opportunities, leading to persistent inequality between men and women. The symbolic interactionist perspective explains gender inequality as something created and reinforced through everyday interactions, where individuals actively perform and reproduce gender roles through behavior and social expectations.
