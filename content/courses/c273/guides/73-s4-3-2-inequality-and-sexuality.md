## Inequality and Sexuality

Recall that the definition of a person's sexual orientation is their physical, mental, emotional, and sexual attraction to a particular sex (male and/or female). Sexual orientation is typically divided into several categories: heterosexuality, homosexuality, queer, etc. The United States and many other countries are heteronormative societies, meaning heterosexuality is considered normal, and other orientations are often regarded as less legitimate, invisible, and sometimes even deviant. Because culture operates as a whole (holism), heteronormativity is all around us in advertisements, marketing, and daily interactions. While awareness and acceptance of different sexual orientations and identities seem to be increasing (including the legalization of same-sex marriage in the United States in 2015), the influence of a heteronormative society can lead LGBTQIA+ people to be treated like "others," even by people who do not deliberately seek to cause them harm. This can lead to forms of sanctions, such as exclusion and inequality, as well as stigma.

## The Outcomes of Sexual Inequality

Differences in how sexual orientations are valued in society can lead to unequal treatment and outcomes for LGBTQIA+ individuals. The following are examples of how sexual inequality affects health and well-being.

## Stigma, Self-Concept, and Mental Health

Recall Cooley's looking-glass self, which suggests that the self develops due to our interpretation and evaluation of the responses of others. Constant exposure to derogatory labels, jokes, and pervasive homophobia would lead to a negative self-image, or worse, self-hate. Stigma and negative social response can shape how individuals view themselves, affecting mental and emotional well-being.

## Social Rejection and Mental Health Outcomes

Social rejection tied to sexual orientation, such as homophobia, is associated with serious mental health risks, such as depression, particularly for youth. According to the CDC (2024), LGBTQIA+ youth experience significantly higher bullying rates than their heterosexual, cisgender peers. 29% of gay/lesbian youth and 31% of bisexual youth reported being bullied on school property, compared to 18% of cisgender youth. Roughly 40% to 43% of transgender youth report being bullied on school property. Black transgender and nonbinary young people report disproportionately higher rates of suicide risk. Current data indicate that 58% have seriously considered suicide and 25% have attempted suicide in the past year (The Trevor Project 2023). Key takeaway: Sexuality inequality occurs when certain sexual orientations are socially devalued, leading to stigma, discrimination, and social rejection that can negatively affect mental health and overall well-being, especially for LGBTQIA+ individuals.
