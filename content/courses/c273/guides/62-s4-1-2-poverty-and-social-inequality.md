## Poverty

While wealth shows long-term financial advantage, poverty shows a lack of financial resources. Poverty is a condition in which individuals or families do not have enough money to meet basic needs such as food, housing, clothing, healthcare, and transportation. In the United States, poverty is usually measured using the poverty line, a federal income level used to decide who qualifies for government assistance programs. Families whose income falls below this level are considered to be living in poverty. Sociologists describe two main types of poverty: Absolute poverty: A person or family cannot afford basic necessities, such as food, shelter, or clean water. In this situation, daily survival may be at risk. Relative poverty: A person or family has fewer resources than others in their society.

## Example of Relative Poverty

People in relative poverty may be able to meet basic needs but cannot afford the typical standard of living in their country. For example, they may not have access to the technology needed for safety

(such as a phone to call 911), may wear basic clothing instead of name brands, or may not have extra food beyond regular meals. Key takeaway: In the United States, poverty is a condition in which individuals or families lack the financial resources to meet basic needs and is measured using the poverty line. Sociologists distinguish between absolute poverty, where survival necessities are unmet, and relative poverty, where people have basic needs but lack the standard of living typical in their society.

Who Is in Poverty in the United States?

In the United States, poverty and economic disadvantage are very closely entwined with social factors. The likelihood of experiencing poverty varies across social groups. The following are examples of the key findings: Women disproportionately experience poverty. They are 51% of the population, but 55.9% of those in poverty. Single mothers and older persons particularly feel the experience of poverty among women. Minorities are overrepresented in poverty. Although they make up 40% of the total population, nearly 59% of those are living in poverty. Children under 18 have the highest rate of poverty, and they are more racially and ethnically diverse than adults who were in poverty. ## Feminization of Poverty

Due to many social factors, such as women being more likely to head single-parent households and typically earning less than their male counterparts, women make up the majority of people living in poverty in the United States. This worldwide phenomenon, known as the feminization of poverty, refers to the fact that women are more likely than men to experience poverty. Coined in 1978 and still

relevant today, the term highlights structural inequality, including the gender wage gap, increasing female-headed households, primary caregiving responsibilities, and limited access to resources that place a greater burden on women.

## Explanations for the Feminization of Poverty

In the United States, women make up approximately 56% of those living in poverty. One reason is that single mothers often provide for their children alone, and one in four unmarried mothers lives in poverty (Bleiweis, 2020). Another factor is the gender wage gap, where men earn more than women on average over their working lives. Because of patriarchal structures, women are more likely to take on unpaid caregiving for aging family members, often with fewer financial resources to support themselves and their dependents. Key takeaway: The feminization of poverty refers to the phenomenon in which women experience poverty at higher rates than men due to structural inequalities such as the gender wage gap, single parenthood, caregiving responsibilities, and unequal access to economic resources.

Poverty both reflects and reinforces social inequality in the United States by limiting access to stable housing, healthcare, education, and economic opportunities, which reduces social mobility and increases the likelihood that disadvantage persists across generations.

## Homelessness in the United States

In the United States, 1.25 million people experienced sheltered homelessness at some point. And 23% of homeless people are families with children, the fastest growing segment of the homeless. Homelessness, which is defined as the lack of a regular, adequate residence, is a social problem due in large part to structural inequality and the maldistribution of resources. Those at a higher risk of becoming homeless include veterans, people suffering from substance abuse or mental disorders, and people who are unemployed. Social factors that contribute to homelessness include the following:

- economic downturns
- deinstitutionalization
- lack of family support
- lack of affordable housing

## The Impact of Affordable Housing and Gentrification

A decrease in affordable housing contributes to higher rates of homelessness, particularly in urban areas. One factor that increases this challenge is gentrification, which is a process in which a lowerincome neighborhood experiences an influx of higher-income residents, leading to rising property values and the displacement of existing residents who may no longer be able to afford to live there. As housing costs rise, many people may find it more difficult to access stable and affordable living options.
