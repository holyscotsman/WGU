# S2 4 Summary

## Lesson 4: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson.

Socialization is the process through which people develop a sense of self through ongoing social interaction.

George Herbert Mead explained that the self develops over time through the preparatory stage, play stage, and game stage, as individuals learn to take the roles of others.

The game stage allows individuals to understand shared rules and the expectations of the generalized other, making a social sense of self possible.

Mead described the self as having two interacting parts: the "Me," shaped by social norms and expectations, and the "I," which represents individual response and creativity.

Charles Horton Cooley's looking-glass self explains how people form self-understandings by imagining how others see and judge them and reacting to those imagined judgments.

Self-image reflects how people see themselves in specific situations and can change, while self- concept is a more stable understanding of who they are over time.
