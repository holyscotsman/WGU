## Applied Sociology

Sociology is useful because it helps people understand how society works and why patterns exist in everyday life. However, sociology is not only used to explain society, but it can also be used to improve it. When sociologists take what they know about society and use it to address real-world issues, they are practicing applied sociology. Outcomes of applied sociology include the following: solving problems in areas such as education, healthcare, work, family life, and communities identifying practical strategies that can make life better for individuals and groups identifying barriers that affect people's opportunities and using research to recommend changes

## Example of Applied Sociology

To see how applied sociology works, consider the scenario of a student who is attending a traditional university while working full time. If the student's work schedule changes suddenly, they may miss deadlines, fall behind, and feel overwhelmed. From a sociological perspective, this situation is not just about one student's time management. It may also be connected to larger social systems such as workplace expectations, education policies, access to technology, and family responsibilities. An

applied sociologist might use the conflict perspective to examine how unequal access to resources, such as time, reliable internet, and flexible work schedules, creates barriers for some students but not others. Based on this analysis, applied sociology might recommend solutions such as creating online educational environments that provide affordable, high-quality education that better supports working students. WGU was founded when several state governors realized the traditional model of education was not working for their constituents and designed a new model of education to serve a more diverse and underserved student population. These types of changes focus on reducing barriers in the system rather than blaming the individual, helping more students succeed.

## Clinical Sociology

Clinical sociology is a type of applied sociology that focuses on improving social situations through analysis and intervention. Clinical sociologists study social problems and then work directly with individuals, groups, or organizations to create change by identifying solutions and supporting improvement through interventions. Clinical sociology can be used in settings such as schools, workplaces, healthcare organizations, and social service agencies.

## Clinical Sociology Example

A community clinic notices that many patients delay treatment because they cannot afford doctor visits or medications. A sociologist would study the situation to determine the barriers that prevent people from getting care. Then, they would work with the clinic and community partners to create an intervention, such as connecting patients to financial assistance programs, improving access to lowcost medication options, and helping patients schedule appointments outside of standard work hours.

## Comparing Applied and Clinical Sociology

Applied sociology uses sociological research to identify and reduce real-world social barriers through practical solutions, while clinical sociology is a hands-on form of applied sociology that directly intervenes with individuals, groups, or organizations to improve social conditions.
