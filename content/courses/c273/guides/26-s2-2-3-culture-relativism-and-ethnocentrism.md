## Culture Shock and Cultural Relativism

When people interact with a culture different from their own, they may notice behaviors that feel unfamiliar or uncomfortable. In a new cultural setting, this reaction can lead to confusion or frustration. Sociologists call this experience culture shock. To make sense of behaviors that feel unfamiliar, sociologists use cultural relativism. Cultural relativism means examining a behavior within the culture where it occurs instead of judging it by outside cultural standards. For example, in some cultures, standing close during a conversation shows warmth or engagement, while in others, more personal space is expected. Rather than labeling one approach as right or wrong, cultural relativism explains how behaviors reflect different cultural expectations shaped by social settings.

Key takeaway: Cultural relativism is the practice of understanding beliefs and behaviors within their cultural context instead of judging them by your own standards. Cultural relativism helps you avoid biased assumptions that can damage relationships, communication, and decision-making when working with people from different backgrounds.

## Ethnocentrism

People often use their own cultural experiences as a reference point when interpreting others' behavior. When individuals judge another culture by the standards and values of their own, this perspective is called ethnocentrism. (The word ethnocentrism comes from the roots ethno (people, culture) + centr (center) + -ism (perspective).) Ethnocentrism shapes perceptions by leading people to view their own culture as normal, correct, or superior, while viewing other cultures as strange, inferior, and even wrong. This mindset can contribute to misunderstanding, stereotyping, or conflict when people do not consider cultural differences in context. Even individuals, government, non-government, private, and religious institutions with good intentions may travel to another society to "help" its people or label them as uneducated, backward, or even inferior.

## Cultural Imperialism

Cultural imperialism is the deliberate imposition of one's own cultural values on another culture. A modern example includes the work of international aid agencies introducing agricultural methods and plant species from developed countries into regions where indigenous crops and local farming practices are better suited.

## Internal Variations within a Culture

Additionally, each culture also has internal variations. At times, differences within a culture can be greater than differences between cultures. Sociologist Pierre Bourdieu described this as cultural capital, which includes material goods, non-

material attitudes, and knowledge associated with a particular economic class. Key takeaway: Ethnocentrism refers to judging other cultures using your own culture as the standard and viewing your way as normal or correct. Avoid ethnocentrism because it can lead you to misinterpret people, reinforce stereotypes, and create conflict that harms teamwork, leadership, and your ability to work effectively in diverse environments.

## Cultural Change

Recall that culture is a process; it is not static. It changes over time as people respond to new ideas, technologies, and social conditions. As culture changes, people may respond in different ways. Some may react with ethnocentrism by judging new cultural influences based on their own cultural standards. In contrast, others may use cultural relativism to understand cultural change within its social and historical context. Cultural change occurs through innovation, diffusion, and globalization.
