# S3 4 Summary

## Lesson 4: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson. Crime is an action that violates a formal law, and punishment refers to the formal sanctions used by the legal system in response to crime. Crimes fall into major categories, including violent, property, victimless, white-collar, hate, and organized crime. Patterns of crime and victimization differ by gender, with men more likely to commit violent offenses and women more often experiencing higher rates of sexual victimization. Social inequality shapes patterns of crime and victimization by influencing access to resources, exposure to risk, and how laws are enforced across social groups. The U.S. criminal justice system includes law enforcement, courts, and corrections. The correctional system uses incarceration, probation, and parole as forms of punishment, with prisons and jails serving different functions. Sociological perspectives explain incarceration differently. The functionalist perspective emphasizes social order. The conflict perspective highlights power and inequality. The symbolic interactionist perspective focuses on labeling and identity.
