## Introduction to Social Stratification

Do you ever notice how some groups of people have more or less than you do? Have you noticed that some groups live in fancy houses with gated neighborhoods and attend well-funded high-tech schools, and some groups live in small, crowded neighborhoods and attend schools with outdated textbooks? Or maybe you are not in either group.

This is an example of social stratification, which is a system by which society ranks and categorizes groups of people based on access to valued resources such as wealth, income, education, power, and prestige. It is a characteristic of society and not a matter of individual differences.

Key takeaway: Social stratification forms a social hierarchy (ranking) within society and influences access to opportunities, which leads to inequality. For example, families with greater wealth and education may have greater access to opportunities, while families with fewer resources face more limitations.

## Example: Status as an Influence on Ranking within Society

Status is one factor that influences social stratification because it can affect access to resources, opportunities, and influence. There are two types of status:

Ascribed status is a social position assigned at birth or involuntarily later in life, based on characteristics like race, sex, family background, or ethnicity.

Achieved status is a social position acquired through personal effort, talent, choices, or actions, such as gender, position at work, chess skills, etc. For example, getting married, being a college graduate, and getting a job all represent achieved statuses since these require effort to attain, while being a female is an ascribed status since it is assigned to you at birth.

Ascribed status is a social position assigned at birth or involuntarily later in life, based on characteristics like race, sex, family background, or ethnicity.

Achieved status is a social position acquired through personal effort, talent, choices, or actions, such as gender, position at work, chess skills, etc. For example, getting married, being a college graduate, and getting a job all represent achieved statuses since these require effort to attain, while being a female is an ascribed status since it is assigned to you at birth.

Achieved status is a social position acquired through personal effort, talent, choices, or actions, such as gender, position at work, chess skills, etc. For example, getting married, being a college graduate, and getting a job all represent achieved statuses since these require effort to attain, while being a female is an ascribed status since it is assigned to you at birth.
