## What Is the Symbolic Interactionist

Perspective? The symbolic interactionist perspective (also called symbolic interactionism) is a micro-level way of looking at society. This means it focuses on small-scale, everyday interactions between individuals, such as conversations, relationships, and communication through texting or social media. Symbolic interactionists study how people use symbols (e.g., words, gestures, emojis) to create shared meaning in daily life. For example, consider a short text message reply that simply says, "K." One person might interpret it as neutral, while another might see it as rude or annoyed. A symbolic interactionist would focus on how the meaning of the message depends on the relationship between the people, the situation, and the shared expectations about what that symbol (the message) means. ## Key Contributors to the Symbolic

## Interactionist Perspective

One important contributor to symbolic interactionism is George Herbert Mead, who is considered a founder of the symbolic interactionist perspective. Mead focused on how people create society through social interaction and shared meaning, specifically through the development of the self. Another key contributor is Herbert Blumer, a student of Mead. Blumer coined the term symbolic interactionism and explained observable interactions, or how people interact based on the meanings things have for them, and those meanings come from social interaction. Key takeaway: The symbolic interactionist perspective is a micro-level approach that explains social life by examining how people use and interpret symbols (like words, gestures, and emojis) to create meaning in everyday interactions.

## Critique of the Symbolic Interactionist

## Perspective

Research using this perspective is often questioned because it can be hard for researchers to stay completely objective. Others argue that the focus on symbolic interaction is too narrow and ignores how social structures affect human behavior and choices.

## Symbolic Interactionist Perspective: Social

## Exchange Theory

An important modern-day theory from the symbolic interactionist perspective is the social exchange theory. This theory argues that society is shaped by ongoing interactions in which individuals seek to maximize rewards and minimize costs. It assumes that people evaluate the options available to them and try to choose the one that provides the greatest benefit with the least loss.

## Social Exchange Theory Formula

Social exchange theory is often summarized with a simple formula: Rewards − Costs = Outcome In other words: "What I gain" − "What I lose" = "My decision."

## The Concept of Equity

Sometimes these decisions lead to positive outcomes, and sometimes they do not. A key concept in social exchange theory is equity, or the sense that an interaction is fair to everyone involved based on the rewards and costs exchanged. For example, in some dual-income households where both partners work full time, women may still perform more housework and childcare than men. Even if the arrangement appears unequal, it may be perceived as fair by the individuals involved due to expectations about roles, conflict avoidance, or beliefs about competence. In these cases, the relationship continues because both partners view the overall exchange as acceptable.
