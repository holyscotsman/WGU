## Steps in the Research Process

Sociologists use a structured research process to investigate patterns in social life and explain why patterns happen in society. This process is based on the scientific method, which Comte, the founder of sociology, originally proposed. The scientific method helps sociologists stay organized, objective, and consistent as they collect and analyze information about the social world. The scientific method typically includes six main steps, shown in the following graphic: "The Research Process."

This infographic outlines the six steps of the research process in a vertical sequence. The steps include asking a question, conducting background research, constructing a hypothesis, designing and conducting a study to collect data, analyzing the data, and reporting the results. Each step is paired with an icon and connected by arrows to show progression.

## Applying the Steps in the Research

## Process

The following is an example of how a sociologist applied the steps of the research process.

## Step 1: Ask a Question

Imagine a sociologist notices that crime rates are increasing in some communities and not others. Instead of assuming the cause, the sociologist begins the research process by asking a question: How do social and economic conditions affect differences in crime rates across neighborhoods and cities?

## Step 2: Conduct Background Research

Next, the sociologist reviews existing research and background information to see what other researchers have already discovered about crime rates and social and economic conditions. This includes reading studies, examining crime statistics, and looking at existing records from sources such as police databases or court systems.

## Step 3: Construct a Hypothesis

After reviewing what is already known, the sociologist forms a hypothesis, which is a prediction about what they expect to find. For example, the sociologist might predict: If unemployment increases, then the crime rate will increase.

Step 4: Design and Conduct a Study (Collect the Data) To test this hypothesis, the sociologist designs a study and collects data. This might include using existing data sources on unemployment and crime rates or gathering additional information through surveys or other research methods. (For additional information about research methods, see page 3.2.)

## Step 5: Analyze the Data

Once the data is collected, the sociologist analyzes it to look for patterns and determine whether the results support the hypothesis.

## Step 6: Report Results

Finally, the sociologist reports the findings by sharing the results in an article, a book, a report to the funding body of the research, or a professional conference. This allows other researchers and stakeholders to review the study so they can build on the research in the future and apply the knowledge to future policy development.

Key takeaway: Sociologists apply the six steps of the scientific method to systematically ask questions, test hypotheses, collect data, and report findings about patterns in social life.
