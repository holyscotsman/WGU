## Stages in a Life: Expectations

Sociologists divide the lifespan into stages based on age-related expectations. These stages are socially constructed, meaning societies define what behaviors and responsibilities are considered appropriate at different ages. Sociologists use the concept of the life course to explain how people move through socially defined stages of life, each shaped by age-related expectations.

The following table shows how social expectations change as people move through the lifespan. These expectations are shaped by society and culture, not just age or biology, and they help guide behavior and identity at each stage of life.

*Note: Adolescence may be referred to as a liminal state, which is a transitional period of "being-in- between," representing a threshold or waiting phase between one stage of life and the next.

## Life Course Theory (5 Principles)

Life course theory explains how people's lives are shaped by the timing of life events, social expectations, and the historical period in which they live. Rather than viewing socialization as something that happens only in childhood, this theory emphasizes that socialization is a lifelong process and aging and socialization are shaped by both structure and choice.

Sociologists often describe the life course theory using five key principles:

1. Socialization is a lifelong process. 2. Lives are shaped by the when and where. 3. Age and timing of life events matter. 4. Lives are connected to each other. 5. Lives are shaped by choices/actions within opportunities and constraints.

## The Life Course Theory in Action

The following is a story to help you understand how this theory is applied.

Story:

When Maria was growing up, her parents expected her to focus on school and help care for her younger siblings. In her community, children were taught to respect adults and avoid questioning authority. As a teenager, Maria began working part-time to contribute to her family's income, delaying extracurricular activities that many of her classmates enjoyed.

When Maria was making plans for graduating high school, many of her classmates were doing the same during a period of economic uncertainty that shaped how they viewed their options for the future.

After graduating from high school, Maria entered the workforce rather than attending college, reflecting both her family's economic situation and the expectations placed on her as the oldest child. Years later, after her own children were grown, Maria returned to school to earn a degree, supported by changing social norms that made adult education more accessible.

## Applying the Life Course Theory

This story illustrates life course theory, which explains how age-related expectations, social relationships, and historical circumstances over time shape people's lives.

## Principle: Lifelong Development

Life course theory emphasizes that socialization and development occur across the entire lifespan. Socialization is the process of learning one's culture and society, and since those are constantly changing, the learning process never ends.

## Socialization is a lifelong process because

one enters different stages of life and always has to learn what those entail (e.g., child to adolescent to adult), and culture and society are constantly changing (e.g., language/slang, fashion, expected age to marry, etc.).

In the story, Maria's life does not stop changing after childhood or adolescence. Her return to school later in life shows that development and learning continue into adulthood and even later stages, not just early life.

## Principle: Historical Time and Place

Maria's ability to return to school later in life reflects broader historical changes, such as increased access to adult education and shifting norms about lifelong learning. Her life course is shaped not only by personal choices but also by the historical period in which she lives.

## Principle: Timing

The principle of timing focuses on when life events occur. Maria entered the workforce right after high school rather than attending college, which affected her later opportunities. Returning to school after her children are grown shows how the same event (education) can have different meanings depending on when it occurs in the life course.

## Principle: Linked Lives

Life course theory stresses that lives are interconnected. Maria's decisions are closely tied to her family (first her parents and siblings and later her own children). They are also tied to her peers who were graduating from high school during an economic downturn. Because Maria and her classmates were all the same age during a period of economic uncertainty, they experienced linked lives, developing similar attitudes about work and financial responsibility.

## Principle: Human Agency

Maria's choices are shaped by her family structure, economic situation, and cultural expectations. She went to college at a later stage in life than is normative because she had expectations such as helping care for siblings and working as a teenager. Her choice to delay college reflects the social environment in which she was raised. These contexts influenced what options felt available or appropriate at different points in her life.

Key takeaway: Life course theory explains how a person's life is shaped over time by lifelong development, individual choices (agency), the timing of life events, interconnected relationships (linked lives), and the influence of historical time and place.
