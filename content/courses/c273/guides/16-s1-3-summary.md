# S1 3 Summary

## Lesson 3: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson. Sociologists use descriptive research to identify and describe social patterns, and they use explanatory research to explain why those patterns happen. Sociological research can be quantitative (focused on numbers and measurable data) or qualitative (focused on meanings, experiences, and detailed descriptions). The sociological research process follows these steps using the scientific method:

- 1. asking a question
- 2. reviewing existing research
- 3. forming a hypothesis
- 4. designing and conducting a study
- 5. analyzing results
- 6. reporting findings

Sociologists collect data using research methods such as experiments, surveys, field research, content analysis, and secondary data analysis. Research findings can be affected by data integrity issues, such as missing information, biased results, or limitations in how data are collected and interpreted. Ethical research requires protecting participants through practices such as informed consent, confidentiality, and avoiding exploitation, especially when working with vulnerable groups. Research integrity also depends on ethical professional behavior, including avoiding plagiarism, staying within areas of expertise, preventing conflicts of interest, and reporting results accurately.
