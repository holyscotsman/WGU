What Is the Conflict Perspective? The conflict perspective is a macro-level way of looking at society. From the conflict perspective, society is not always balanced or fair. This perspective is most closely associated with Karl Marx, who argued that society is shaped by conflict between groups competing for scarce resources, such as wealth, education, housing, healthcare, power, and political influence.

## Example of the Conflict Perspective

Look at an example of the conflict perspective. Conflict theorists might explain differences in opportunity by pointing to unequal access to resources such as education, housing, and high-paying jobs. Some groups have access to more resources; they are more likely to attend better-funded schools, live in safer neighborhoods, and gain access to stronger job networks. Over time, these advantages can continue across generations, even when individuals work equally hard.

What Is a Resource? In the conflict perspective, a resource is anything valuable that people or groups compete for because it helps them meet needs, gain advantages, or increase power. Resources can include the following: money and wealth; jobs and income; education and credentials; housing and safe neighborhoods; healthcare; political influence and decision-making power; time and leisure. Key takeaway: The conflict perspective is a macro-level viewpoint that explains society as shaped by unequal power and competition between groups for scarce resources, which creates and maintains unequal opportunities over time.

## Conflict Perspective's View of Society

From the conflict perspective, society is shaped by inequality between groups and power differences. They often describe society in terms of dominant groups, who have more power and resources, and subordinate groups, who have less power and fewer opportunities. Because resources are limited, groups compete for things like money, education, housing, and political influence. Conflict theorists also argue that social institutions can help maintain these unequal outcomes over time, but conflict can also lead to social change when groups challenge inequality.

## Example

For example, the conflict perspective might explain differences in educational success by focusing on how unequal access to resources creates unequal opportunities. If some schools receive more funding, that funding leads to better facilities, better learning resources, and more support staff. Students in these schools may have more advantages than students in schools with fewer resources.

## Critique of the Conflict Perspective

The conflict perspective is often criticized for focusing too heavily on conflict. Because it highlights disagreement and struggle so strongly, it can overlook the fact that many social structures remain stable over long periods of time or change slowly and gradually rather than suddenly, as conflict theory predicts.

## Conflict Perspective: Feminist Theory

One important modern theory that falls within the conflict perspective is the feminist theory. This theory focuses on gender inequality and how overcoming inequality requires social change because it is built into social structures. Feminist theorists examine how power and privilege are connected to gender and how these patterns can shape people's opportunities and experiences.

## Feminist Theorists' View of Society

Feminist theorists argue that gender inequality is not a personal issue or an individual problem, nor is it accidental. Rather, it is built into long-standing social power structures. For example, workplace leadership positions are often disproportionately held by men. This pattern is not simply the result of individual choices but reflects broader expectations, hiring practices, and promotion systems that advantage men over women. One key idea in feminist theory is patriarchy, a social system in which men, as a group, hold more power and authority than women. This unequal distribution of power shapes expectations, opportunities, and outcomes in areas such as work, family roles, education, and leadership. A key distinction between feminist theory and conflict perspective is that the former calls for action steps to better the lives of women through policies and institutional practices.
