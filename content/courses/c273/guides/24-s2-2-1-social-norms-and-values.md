## Social Norms

One way sociologists understand culture is by looking at the shared expectations that guide behavior in everyday life. Sociologists refer to these shared expectations as social norms, which are the rules or guidelines that a group or society uses to shape behavior.

Social norms tell people what is considered appropriate or inappropriate in specific settings, such as how to communicate with others, how to behave in public spaces, or how to respond in professional situations. Because social norms are learned through social interaction, they can vary across groups, environments, and cultures.

Throughout this course, the term norms is used as a shortened way to refer to social norms.

Key takeaway: Social norms are shared, learned rules that shape appropriate

behavior in different settings. Understanding them helps you adapt to

expectations locally, globally, and across teams and workplace cultures.

## Components of Culture: Norms and Values

Norms guide behavior by shaping everyday expectations and influence how people interact with others, make decisions, and respond in different social settings. Because norms are socially constructed (created and maintained through people and society) and learned through social interaction, they can differ across groups, environments, and cultures.

Closely related to norms are values, which are the ideas or standards that a culture considers important or desirable. Values shape what people believe is right or wrong, good or bad, and worth striving for. For example, many cultures value education, fairness, or independence. While values represent what society believes should matter, norms provide guidance on how people are expected to behave based on those values.

## Norms, Values, and Behaviors

Norms and values work together to shape behavior. For example, a society that values education may develop norms such as attending school regularly, completing assignments, and respecting teachers. When people follow these expectations, school interactions run more smoothly. When expectations are unclear or violated, confusion or conflict can occur. One of the ways societies strive to maintain their values is through rewards and punishments (sanctions). Sanctions are forms of social control, or ways to encourage conformity to cultural norms or rules.

## Types of Norms

Sociologists divide norms into three types: folkways, mores, and taboos. Understanding these helps explain why some behaviors lead to minor social reactions while others result in serious consequences.
