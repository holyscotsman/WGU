What Is the Functionalist Perspective?

The functionalist perspective (also called structural functionalism) is a macro-level way of looking at society. The functionalist perspective looks at society as a system where different parts or institutions work together to keep society running smoothly. For example, schools (which are part of the social institution of education) teach students math, reading, and skills that a healthy labor force needs. They also teach rules, teamwork, and responsibility. From a functionalist perspective, it is in these ways that schools ensure society has what it needs to maintain stability: an educated and prepared workforce and citizenry who share a common culture.

What Do Functionalists Study?

From a functionalist perspective, sociologists study how social institutions contribute to society. Functionalists are especially interested in questions like these:

How does a social institution help society meet important needs?

How do institutions work together to maintain order and stability?

What happens when an institution stops working effectively?

## Unintended and Intended Outcomes

Why do people go to college? What else happens in college that may not be part of the official goal? These are questions that a sociologist would ask from the functionalist perspective.

Functionalists recognize that social institutions, such as educational systems, often produce more than one outcome. Some outcomes are planned and expected or intended (referred to as manifest functions), while others happen unexpectedly and are unintended (referred to as latent functions). ## Critique of the Functionalist Perspective

One major criticism of the functionalist perspective is that it does not do a good job explaining how or why societies change over time. Another issue is that the theory can be circular; it assumes that repeated behaviors must have a purpose, but it only identifies their purpose because they are repeated.

In addition, some behaviors or institutions continue even when they no longer serve a helpful role, which goes against the theory's basic idea that everything exists because it has a function. Because of these problems, many sociologists think functionalism is not very useful as a big picture (macro- level) explanation anymore, though it can still be helpful when studying society at a more specific, midlevel scale.
