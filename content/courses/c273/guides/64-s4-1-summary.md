# S4 1 Summary

## Lesson 1: Summary

## Social Stratification and Social Class

Social stratification is a structured system of inequality, and societies organize people into hierarchies based on access to resources and power.

Societies use different systems of stratification (e.g., slavery, caste, class) that vary in how rigid or flexible they are.

Social mobility refers to movement within a stratification system and can occur upward, downward, or across and within generations.

In the United States, social class is structured largely around income, wealth, education, and occupation.

Wealth in the United States is unevenly distributed, contributing to significant economic inequality.

Sociologists explain stratification using different perspectives.

Functionalist: Stratification is necessary as it motivates people to pursue roles and jobs that require more training and educational attainment.

Conflict: Stratification exists because those with control of resources rely on exploitation to maintain power over others.

Symbolic interaction: Stratification is reinforced through status symbols, language, and social expectations.

## Poverty in the United States

Wealth and poverty are indicators of economic stratification in the United States.

Certain social groups, including women, racial and ethnic minorities, children, older adults, and individuals with lower levels of education, face a higher risk of poverty.

Poverty reinforces social inequality by limiting access to education, healthcare, housing, and employment opportunities.

Homelessness is influenced by structural factors such as housing costs, employment instability, and access to social services.

## Global Stratification

Global stratification describes inequality between nations.

Economic inequality between nations is measured using indicators such as gross national income (GNI) per capita, gross domestic product (GDP), wealth, life expectancy, and Gini index.
