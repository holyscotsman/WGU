# S1 2 Summary

## Lesson 2: Summary

Take a moment to think about what you learned in this lesson. Sociological perspectives and theories help sociologists understand society by using different "lenses" to interpret patterns in social life. The functionalist perspective explains how social institutions work together to create stability, and it highlights the functions and dysfunctions of social systems. The conflict perspective focuses on how inequality and competition for scarce resources shape power differences, create conflict, and drive social change. The feminist theory, which comes from the conflict perspective, examines how patriarchy and gender inequality shape society, and intersectional feminism expands this by showing how overlapping identities influence inequality. Symbolic interactionism explains how people create shared meaning through everyday social interactions, symbols, and communication. The social exchange theory, which comes from the symbolic interactionist perspective, argues that society is shaped by ongoing interactions in which individuals seek to maximize rewards and minimize costs. Applied sociology uses sociological knowledge to address real-world problems and better understand outcomes in areas like education, work, healthcare, and communities. Clinical sociology uses sociological analysis and direct intervention to improve social situations and create meaningful change in organizations and communities.
