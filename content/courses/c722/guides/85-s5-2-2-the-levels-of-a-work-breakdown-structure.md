# S5 2.2 The Levels of a Work Breakdown Structure

## Learning Objectives

Describe how a project's work breakdown structure is created.

## Work Breakdown Structure

The work breakdown structure (WBS) follows the same levels recognized in the defining and planning phases of the project or program:

programs

projects within programs

deliverables within projects

activities within deliverables

tasks and subtasks within activities

When you complete the defining phase of the project, you will have a charter, customer agreement, or contract for the project. Essential to the charter is defining how the customer will determine if the project deliverable meets expectations. It is the definition of a project according to the stakeholders. It has a beginning, work steps, and a clear and defined end. The end is defined not only with an end date but also with deliverables that must be completed for it to be considered complete. These deliverables must be provided to and accepted by the customer and must meet the scope, budget, schedule, and quality agreed to in the charter and called for by the triple constraint.

The project management methodologies are shown with related icons next to them. They are, in order, plan the project, gather the team, team starts project, meet about the project, manage deadlines, complete tasks, and finish the project. The team uses the WBS to break the deliverables into the activities and then generate the task level. This is the key area of project planning that is done by the team and is used to transform the team into a cohesive workforce. It will identify tasks and set the stage to assign work. The people who are responsible begin to understand how their tasks fit into the overall project.

It is recommended that the team members who will execute the plan be the people who determine the tasks to be completed. This allows them to take ownership of the project, tasks, and deliverables. The integration of skills and functions will be clear. This is also an opportunity to identify any missing capabilities and skills or concerns about achieving what is being defined.

Ultimately you want to get to tasks with the following characteristics:

It can be realistically estimated and completed.

It is at its best and most realistic size.

It can be completed in a realistic time frame to standards the team embraces.

Each group of tasks and activities produces a piece of a required deliverable.

Each task is a unique package of work that can be managed and performed by a given resource.

## The 8/80 Rule

An excellent rule to follow in breaking down tasks is the 8/80 rule. You do not assign anything that takes less than eight hours, so you allow resources sufficient work to occupy them and you do not micromanage.

This is essentially one business day. You do not want to assign a task that takes more than 80 hours or two work weeks to complete. This way, you keep your communications strategy at its best and make certain you are controlling an optimum amount of work at a given time.

The 8 by 80 rule is followed when breaking down tasks. This helps to break down 80 hours by 8 working hours each day.

## Formats of a Work Breakdown Structure

As you can see from the previous examples, a simple hierarchical format can be used for developing the WBS. With all the technology and software in the world, one of the most effective techniques for creating a WBS is using a whiteboard with sticky notes. The group will sit around a room with the list of deliverables and begin to deconstruct them into activities and then tasks and then assignment recommendations of the tasks to individuals or roles. This is an excellent team-building exercise because you give everyone an opportunity to speak to the work that has to be done and their piece of it and to understand other people and their roles. The team will be able to identify time and integration issues. By using sticky notes, you can move tasks between activities until you have this well organized. You can quickly identify which tasks can only be done once other tasks are completed. These become your contingencies, and people will begin to understand how they will need to work together. The goals of breaking down the work are visually clear, and the team is able to understand the magnitude of the effort.

The hierarchy can be captured using the drawing and shapes functions in PowerPoint or a similar software package. It is easy to do it using the sticky note process and then enter the resulting WBS into the software. The choice belongs to the project manager.

When you have completed the planning format, you can take the information and begin to build a resource plan using many available tools. One is a WBS number as a coding scheme used to associate tasks to activities, to deliverables, and finally to the overall project and program.
