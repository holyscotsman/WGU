# S3 1.5 Financial Concepts for Project Selection

## Learning Objectives

Describe how financial concepts are used for project selection purposes.

## The Time Value of Money

Financial concepts can be helpful in evaluating which projects should be undertaken. The underlying theme of financial models is that an organization incurs a cost by making money available to execute projects. In its simplest form, the organization may need to borrow money from a bank to fund a project. The loan would require the paying of interest. Another financial perspective would recognize that if the organization spends money on a project, then other possible uses of the money would need to wait. In finance, this concept is defined as an opportunity cost. The opportunity cost of executing a specific project is the benefit that the next best alternative use of the money would have provided. In this case, benefit could refer to revenue generated, another project funded, or any other value the organization could have gained. The concept of time value of money suggests that money is worth more to an organization now than in the future. It makes sense if you consider that if someone gave you $1,000 today and you could invest the money such that the investment would return additional value to you in the future.

In project management, you look at it in the reverse, saying that if completing a project will bring in revenue next year of $1,000, then the future $1,000 is worth less than $1,000 to you today. So, would you spend $5,000 on a project today that will return $5,000 to you next year? Hopefully, you said, “No way!” because you could take the $5,000 today, put it in the bank, and have more than $5,000 a year from now. That is the concept of time value of money: sooner is better. Money in your possession today is worth more than money in the future.
