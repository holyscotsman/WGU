# S5 3 Introduction

## Learning Objectives

Consider the following learning objectives as you move through the lesson:

- Define risk and risk management.
- Describe how to construct a risk register.

## Risk Management

Every activity of a project has some element of risk. During risk planning, you acknowledge there are risks, clearly define them, estimate through knowledge and experience what the probabilities and impact are of the risks, and decide how you will manage and, if you choose, how to respond to the risk throughout the life of the project. Communication is a key element of recognizing, planning for, and managing risks.

This lesson will introduce you to the process and performance domains—Uncertainty, Stakeholder, Team, Development Approach and Life Cycle, Planning, Delivery, and Measurement—used by project managers (PMs) for risk management.

You will understand the process of developing a risk management plan that consists of identifying, assessing, and responding to risk, which are the components of the risk register. These methods are not exclusive, so the PM will need to combine team knowledge, experience, and known methods depending on the situation. The goal is to develop an awareness of possible risks or opportunities and set a plan for reacting to these risks or opportunities should they occur. This will become a key component in team communications for monitoring these possibilities and the project’s progress and if you choose to respond to an outcome of a risk.
