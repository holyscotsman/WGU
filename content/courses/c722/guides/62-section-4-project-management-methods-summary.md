# Section 4: Project Management Methods Summary

## Section 4: Project Management Methods Summary

Expanding on the project life cycle introduced earlier in this course, this section framed the extensive activities required to manage a project using five process groups: initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing. The five process groups align closely with the project life cycle phases but are distinct in that the process groups represent management activities and clearly identify the iterative nature of planning, executing, and monitoring and controlling. This section provided a comparison of traditional, agile, and extreme project management approaches. Traditional approaches follow a linear development often driven by chronological life cycle stages: define, plan, execute, and close. Agile project management allows the project outcomes to be delivered as a series of functional pieces. This iterative approach has its own special terms derived from rugby. Each short sprint is led by a scrum master who works closely with the customer to select and deliver functionality from the project backlog. Computer hardware and software projects often make use of agile project management approaches. Extreme project management approaches are for project environments with extensive unknowns. In this approach, the project team must be highly skilled and able to integrate new knowledge to achieve the customer’s desired outcomes. Extreme project management works with virtually no plan, compared to agile methods that plan for the short sprint and traditional project management, which uses extensive planning.
