## ProjectPlanning

Welcome to the Project Management concepts course. There are two facts about project management that most people are unaware of: First, project management has been around in some

form since the dawn of civilization. Second, many of us engage in some form of project management without even realizing it. What is a project? A project is any temporary endeavor with a specified start and end date that results in a unique product, service, or result. Project management involves managing everything about the project to get the desired result. Consider the oldest relics of ancient civilizations. Most people might think of the pyramids of Egypt, the Great Wall of China, the aqueducts of Rome, or the temples in the jungles of India. The one thing these all have in common is that they were once projects that had to be managed. Although the term project management is relatively new, the concept goes back millennia. Now consider your everyday life. Have you ever taken a family vacation? Most people have. Is this a project? There is a specific start date and end date. There is a unique result that you expect—quality time with your family, visiting new or favorite places. You plan. You have a budget in mind. These are all attributes of a project. No one expects you to do a formal project plan for your next vacation. However, is it not interesting to know you have participated in projects without even being aware of it? Every product and service you use started out as a project. Project management touches everyone in some way every day. As the world and the products and services you use become more complex, good project management becomes more and more important. This section will explore the fundamental project management concepts that must be understood to deliver a project successfully and ultimately produce business value for an organization. We will start by defining and identifying project characteristics and explain how projects are a system for value delivery. Next, we will introduce the 12 principles of project management, review ethical and professional conduct in project management, and continue with the role of the project manager and organizational structures.
