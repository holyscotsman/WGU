# Section 1: Project Management Concepts Summary

## Summary

In this section, you were introduced to some of the basic foundational topics in project management. First you learned some of the characteristics of projects. You saw that projects and project management have been around from the dawn of civilization. You were informed on 12 project management principles to guide the behaviors and actions expected to be present in project managers, teams, and project stakeholders, as well as the code of ethics and professional conduct, according to the PMBOK 7 guide. You also looked at the difference between a project and an operation. Projects are temporary endeavors with a specified start and end date that result in a unique product or service. Operations are ongoing activities. Then the section illustrated the products of a project and how projects can help realize organizational strategies and long-term goals and objectives delivering benefits and value to the organization. You were next introduced to the project manager's roles, knowledge, skills and abilities, how they communicate, and some professional project management organizations. You saw the impact that a project manager has on the success of a project and the importance of the 12 project management principles and 8 performance domains in managing a project. You explored the meaning of triple constraint and how time, scope, and cost must be balanced for a project to succeed. A change in any one constraint will affect the other two. You were introduced to the use of portfolios, programs, and projects within organizations to accomplish strategic goals. Also, an illustration of portfolios, programs, and projects, along with operations, becomes a value delivery system through continuous feedback.

You were introduced to project management as a career and know the importance of having refined people skills. Project managers must be adept at communicating with various stakeholders and be able to resolve conflicts. Communications may be done using a variety of methods, depending on the nature of the project. It is important to have a solid communication plan. Communications may be through reports, meetings, or other means. Working in virtual teams makes good communication skills even more important. Stakeholders must be kept engaged and apprised of the project status. The section introduced various project management standards and discussed professional organizations and the types of professional certifications available. Finally, you looked at some organizational structures. You saw that there are three basic types of organizational structures:

- functional
- project-based
- matrix

This section ended with a discussion of the project management office (PMO). The PMO ascertains that best practices are being followed and that all personnel obtain the necessary training to properly carry out project activities. These PMO functions help the organization obtain higher project management maturity levels, which assures customers and other stakeholders that the organization can manage the project efficiently and effectively.
