# S1 1.7 Defining and Characterizing Project Management

## Learning Objectives

Define project management.

What Is Project Management? The Project Management Institute (PMI) in its PMBOK Guide (7th edition, 2021) defines project management as "the application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities." To clarify further, it is the management and execution by an organization’s resources to move the tasks and assignments of development and other continual improvement endeavors toward successful completion.

## Phases of the Project Life Cycle

The graphic illustrates the phases of a project. At the left is defining, followed in a linear direction by planning, executing, and closing.

The project life cycle illustrates the unique nature of project work. It recognizes that projects have a limited lifespan and that there are predictable changes in the level of effort and focus over the life of a project. We will examine each of these phases in turn.

## Defining

Once senior management approves a project, it enters the initiation or defining phase. Immediate activities that take place in this phase are the following:

- Determining the project’s sponsor and the project’s manager

Creating a project charter. The charter is a document that, like a contract, is agreed upon by the sponsor and key stakeholders. It defines the project and authorizes the resources, roles, responsibilities, authorities, and scope for the project.

## Planning

Project teams that do not comprehensively plan their project significantly lower their probability of achieving success. There are many parts of a plan that need to be considered during this phase, among them are the following:

- technology
- business process
- staffing
- training
- testing
- communications
- budget constraints
- integration of the project with operations and other projects

## Executing

Executing involves overseeing and managing all necessary work and assignments by project team members required over the project's life. This includes overseeing the project deliverables' successful completion in accordance with the approved implementation schedule. This can be a challenging and

volatile phase, due to the following: unexpected changes that may present themselves in the competitive environment new organizational priorities loss of key project resources previously unidentified risks occurring that put the project plans in jeopardy Planning tasks and then ensuring sound communications about progress and associated issues will help the success of this phase. As the project is executed, the project manager must monitor the actual activities relative to planned activities. As tasks are completed and evaluated against the planned schedule, changes may become necessary, which means that monitoring and controlling the project activities becomes crucial. The project manager must know what is going right, what is going wrong, and what is in jeopardy before problems occur. This information must be made available to everyone involved to keep the project on course.

## Closing

The closing phase of the project occurs when the project is completed to the customer's satisfaction. The closing of a project signifies that the project deliverables have been successfully launched and the resulting project efforts are complete. The closing phase includes not only the launch but also a review and transfer of lessons learned. Occasionally, events may occur that necessitate closing a project before it is completed. This may be due to a number of issues, including the following: unexpected changes in the business climate that render the project unnecessary changes in company strategy a realization that the project triple constraints cannot be met It is just as important to formally document the close in these circumstances as there are still lessons to be learned.
