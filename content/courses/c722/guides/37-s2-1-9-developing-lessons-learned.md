# S2 1.9 Developing Lessons Learned

## Learning Objectives

Explain the purpose of developing lessons learned.

## Project Lessons Learned

During the closing phase of a project, the project manager documents the lessons learned. The lessons learned have great value to the project organization because they provide guidance for future projects. Project managers often comment that the lessons learned from prior projects provide valuable insights into what went well and what went poorly on similar projects. It is always better to learn from someone else’s experience and mistakes rather than having to always learn from your own. It is especially valuable to learn steps taken and to see actual time and procurement costs.

## Capturing and Documenting Lessons Learned

Project managers gather information for the lessons learned from their own experiences by reviewing status reports prepared during the project and by soliciting input from stakeholders. The lessons learned are normally stored centrally as a knowledge base by an organization so that they are readily available for use during the initiation and planning of future projects. By speaking to people or reviewing lessons learned, your own planning will get better.

## When Lessons Learned Should Be Applied

The library of lessons learned from prior projects should be a primary source for use in the defining and planning phases of a project. Imagine how valuable it would be to have information from a prior failed project to prevent the same mistakes from occurring again, or to know that certain types of procurement contracts are more effective than others when buying certain raw materials. The value of lessons learned cannot be overstated. This requires that the closing project manager gathers the information and the future project managers use the information.
