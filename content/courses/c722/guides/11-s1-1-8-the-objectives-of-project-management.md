# S1 1.8 The Objectives of Project Management

## Learning Objectives

Define project management.

The purpose of any project is to achieve some specified objective. This objective may be a product, a service, or some other specified result. Earlier, you saw that the objective of a project may be a new or an improved product. Examples that you considered included a passenger car with improved fuel economy and a new vaccine. You also saw that an improved service may be the objective of a project when a new bus route was created to serve a portion of the population. And you saw that the objective may be to gather information to determine if new construction may be required, like when there was a study of traffic patterns for an interstate highway expansion.

Goals and specifications should be SMART. The acronym SMART stands for specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and timed. Measurable goals and specifications keep the project team focused on the project outcomes.

In each case, there were specific objectives, unique to that project (addressing the triple constraints of scope, cost, and time). In all cases, the objective(s) met certain criteria. These criteria are often referred to by the acronym SMART:

- Specific
- Exactly what are you attempting to achieve?

## Measurable

Can you assign a number? Is the result quantifiable? Attainable (or Achievable) Is this possible with the resources (including financial) and technology available?

## Relevant

Does it make sense? Does it fit with the organization’s strategic goals? Time-based (or Timely) Is there a date when it needs to be completed? If any of these are not in place, the project does not have identifiable objectives to meet. Each criteria can be applied to a specific project from the examples:

- Specific

A specified fuel economy objective was identified.

## Measurable

A number (miles per gallon or mpg) is identified for fuel economy.

## Achievable

The desired mpg is achievable with technology that is, or will be, available.

## Relevant

Customers desire more fuel economy.

## Time-based

A date for manufacture of new vehicles was identified. These same SMART criteria may be developed for each of the projects in the examples. Each of these SMART criteria are called out in the scope statement. Sometimes, the term quality and performance criteria is used in place of the term scope. Since quality and performance are key characteristics of the scope, this is viewed as an acceptable practice. Remember also that the triple constraint of cost (budget), time (schedule), and scope is part of the SMART criteria.

## The Challenges of Project Management

Managers and other personnel involved in the project management discipline may face several potential challenges. The challenge that all project managers face is termed the triple constraint. This refers to the fact that all projects are constrained by cost (budget), schedule (time), and scope

(deliverables). A change in any one of these three will impact the other two. This will be further addressed in an upcoming section. In addition to the triple constraint, there are additional challenges facing the project manager and the project team. Some of the most serious and most encountered are the following:

- lack of clearly defined project scope, objectives, and charter

lack of clear and direct strategic alignment with the specific project underway customer needs and requirements not properly understood or defined project change management process not well defined and/or followed, resulting in expanding scope of the project potentially jeopardizing the successful achievement of the project objectives lack of project integration inadequate project management training, skills, and experience shifting management and organizational priorities adversely impacting ongoing projects changes to the organization’s long-term strategic plans lack of comprehensive project risk management and mitigation company culture and organizational structure that does not properly support project management poorly defined and/or implemented communications management plan Every project sponsor and project manager should be aware of these and other potential challenges. They should anticipate how they will avoid these and manage around them in order to ensure the success of their projects.
