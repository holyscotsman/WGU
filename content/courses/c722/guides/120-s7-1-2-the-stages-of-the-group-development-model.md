# S7 1.2 The Stages of the Group Development Model

## Learning Objectives

Describe the characteristics of an effective project team.

## Overview

The PMBOK Guide 7th edition directs its readers to principles and performance domains of teams. High-performing project teams deliver the best quality. High-performing project teams use open communication, shared understanding of project purpose, shared ownership of project performance, trust, collaboration, adaptability, resilience, empowerment, and recognition. Effective project leaders establish and maintain the project vision, use critical thinking, and strive to understand and improve the motivation of their team members.

## Creating an Effective Team

So how do you create an effective team? You cannot just put a group of strangers and different SMEs together and have an effective and productive team. Even with the best team leader or builder, every team goes through a process from the first time they meet until they become effective and eventually disband. But is the process the same for all teams? Does the composition of the team make a difference? Does the skill, experience, or education level of team members make any difference?

In 1965, Dr. Bruce Tuckman introduced the "Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing" model of group development. These stages are the same for every team, regardless of the size or the purpose. Each stage may have different durations from team to team, but all teams go through each and every stage. In addition, any team can and will occasionally regress back to an earlier stage in team development over the life of the project.

## Forming

In this stage, team members may be meeting for the first time. Often, no one really knows much about anyone else on the team. It may be premature to refer to this group of individuals as a team. It is a time of introduction and forming relationships and understanding from the exchange of information.

## Storming

Team members are beginning to learn about each other, but they do not yet understand how to work together. Members may "jockey for position" within the team. The dynamics of working together beyond any written statement of roles and responsibilities are being established. Personalities surface, showing the strengths, weaknesses, and personal needs of each individual on the team. Integration into a team may come with some struggle and conflict.

## Norming

Team members have figured out how they will interact with each other. Working relationships are beginning to form. Trust and understanding is beginning to form among team members. They are beginning to feel comfortable working together and openly and willingly sharing information.

## Performing

Team members are fully comfortable working together. Trust has been developed. Working relationships have jelled. Work is being conducted, and project progress is occurring.

## Adjourning

This only occurs when all the team’s work has been completed and the team is no longer required. This may occur at any time in the project's life cycle.

Any team can—and probably will—regress back to an earlier stage at some time. This can occur at any time in the team’s existence and for many different reasons. Essentially, anything that may impact how any team member interacts will potentially regress the team to an earlier stage. If a new member joins, if a member is replaced by another, if a member leaves, if the team objectives change, etc.—all of these can cause the team to regress.

The transition from one stage to another may not be readily identifiable, and these transitions cannot happen on any predefined schedule. Even the best team leader cannot force transition from one stage to the next. A good team leader will have some tools and techniques he or she has learned to help the process transition smoothly.

There are many team-building techniques, and there are many companies that conduct team- building experiences. The team-building technique to use is at the discretion of management. Each team-building technique has its place and its own value. The team leader will need to make their own decision. Regardless of what is done, the team-building process will take its normal course. It depends on the leadership, personalities, timing, information collection and change, and internal and external evolution.
