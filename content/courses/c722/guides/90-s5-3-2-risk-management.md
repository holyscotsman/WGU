# S5 3.2 Risk Management

## Learning Objectives

Define risk and risk management.

## Risk Management

Risk management is the proactive planning for risk and opportunities that occur at most steps of the project management process, from the time you define the project and create the charter to all the project management planning through execution of the project. Often a specific plan— called the project risk management plan—is created. It is important that you

identify risks,

analyze risk for both quantitative and qualitative impact, and

generate risk responses for all risks that are identified.

Consider the organizational and environmental factors to ensure you know the possible outcomes and costs of the risks (or positive opportunities) you are facing.

The team responsibility in risk management is to review deliverables, activities, and tasks for risks, analyze them for impact, and define your best plan for responses based on your current knowledge and experience. Inputs to this activity are a recognition of both the organizational and stakeholder appetites for risk and project-specific knowledge of what risks and rewards are possible. You also look at the internal and external environmental conditions for impact on your planning. Look to experts for guidance.

Healthy risk management is the identification of risks and the plan for how you will respond both as a team and as part of an organization. It is being conscious of the reality of risk and preparing to respond appropriately.

Look at your planned tasks from the work breakdown structure (WBS) using available information, informed judgment, and experience to generate a list of risks you may encounter. Use all available expertise to analyze the risks, including senior management judgment, stakeholder knowledge, lessons learned from past projects, and external expertise. You want to know the odds of the risk occurring and the impact of the risk should it occur. Possible sources of risk that can happen to your tasks include the following:

technical impacts

cost changes

schedule slippage

the client adding information or requirements

contractual requirements that add tasks, responsibility, time, and cost

weather impacts on schedules and costs financial changes in timing and availability of funds

political impacts on the ability to perform or changes in laws

changing environmental conditions

people (They are always the most unpredictable resource.)

It is your desire to identify all possible risks that are uncovered during creative thinking by your team. You must understand the impact and prepare accordingly.

Watch " Project Risk Identification Methods " (3:30).

The ultimate objective of risk management is to minimize the negative impact of risk and maximize the positive outcomes. In summary, this requires

careful analysis,

prioritization and monitoring of the risks identified at every step for emergencies, and

understanding the impact of the risk through assessment.

Like all things in project management, you identify, plan for, and monitor to recognize if the risk occurs. You measure the impact and control it where possible. You communicate with everyone to understand the impact and to gain resources when required. You do everything to minimize costs and maximize benefits.
