# S5 2.1 The Purpose of a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

## Learning Objectives

Describe how a project's work breakdown structure is created.

## The Purpose of a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

This course has discussed managing resources in several places because it is key to any project’s success. Often the most important and expensive resource is the people who constitute the time and, subsequently, much of the cost dimension of the triple constraint. You can never have too much time or too much expertise, but can your budget afford it?

After the defining phase, the project manager will review the deliverables of the project, begin to draw a picture of the skills needed, and start to assemble the team for the planning phase. The team may come from different disciplines within the organization or from outside sources. People are added when their skills are required. As a project manager, you will frequently add and release people throughout the project life cycle.

Team members need clear direction on what they must do, when it must be done, and where it fits into the big picture. Without this clarity, productivity and quality are affected. Team members will regularly say that they hate working on teams, which is often because they do not know what the expectations are, how they will be held accountable, and how they fit into the team. They will need training and coaching to transform them from individual contributors into a team.

The secret weapon that organizes the team and brings it fully together is the work breakdown structure (WBS). The WBS is a methodical deconstruction of deliverables into activities and then tasks to be performed. It begins to bring clarity to the team and organization for completing the planning phase and preparing for execution. It is a picture of the agreed-upon deliverables and what activities and tasks need to be completed to ensure delivery happens, milestones are met, and that you complete the project to expectations. Often the WBS is used for the following:

effectively organizing the resources to complete the project’s deliverables

assigning responsibility to resources for tasks and activities for both project-specific actions and project management controls

identifying the interaction and contingencies of tasks and how to assign them to meet schedule requirements showing control points and milestones for communicating the team’s progress—both expected and discovered as you move through the project

setting the foundation for more accurate estimating of time and cost for budget purposes

confirming one more time to the customer/sponsor and other stakeholders that the deliverables are understood and the plan to create them is clear

providing a communications platform for the team and stakeholders to use to understand and ask questions about their responsibilities and the project as a whole

validating everyone’s understanding of the project scope

A deliverable is a specific product or functionality that the project will provide. Deliverables can be the final outcome of a project or intermediate outcomes that are integrated into the final project outcome. An activity is a portion of work that will be executed as part of the project to create the specified deliverables. The term task is often used to describe specific actions taken to complete an activity. Creating the WBS will require that the team decompose the deliverables into the activities and tasks that represent the work needed to produce the deliverables. Activities are connected to two types of deliverables:

project management—communications, planning, execution, etc.

project specific to the desired end result of this project

Anything that will require resources to complete the project should be accounted for in the WBS.

## The WBS is not

an exhaustive list of all tasks

It is a comprehensive classification of project activities and tasks based on the deliverables in the scope. Microtasks are assumed within the tasks described. Try not to overmanage the resources.

a project plan, a schedule, or a chronological listing

The WBS specifies what is to be done within the current scope. Add things like "who" and "when" later.

an organizational structure—but it will be used later for help assigning responsibilities to team members

An organization chart for the project may be created separately.
