# S1 1 Summary

## Summary

People have been creating and managing projects since they first began trying to control their own environments, but the concept of project management is relatively new. This lesson defined what a project is and discussed the importance of projects for organizations. It distinguished between a project and a routine business operation and illustrated types of products a project creates. You also read business scenarios and learned about the role of project activity. This lesson introduced the Project Management Institute's PMBOK Guide (7th edition), 12 principles, 8 performance domains, and Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct. These inform the actions of project managers in leading or managing projects that will deliver value and benefits to the client. Projects must be tied to an organization’s business strategy, and there are many determinants of success. The lesson briefly explained the triple constraints and how each must be met in order to ensure project success. It also discussed the relationship between a project, a program, and a portfolio. Projects are generally done in logical steps or phases. You were introduced to those phases (initiating, planning, executing, and closing) and learned how to make project goals specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-based in order to avoid miscommunication among stakeholders and team members. Finally, you took a look at some of the challenges of project management, such as lack of project scope clarity, lack of strategic alignment with organizational goals, project changes, and insufficient communication.
