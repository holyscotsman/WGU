This section will focus on two important aspects of project management. The first is a high-level view of project management based on the four major phases of the project life cycle. You will use the define-plan-execute-close model of project life cycle phases to explain the activities that typically occur. The second aspect you will address in this section is the triple constraint of project management.

## Project Life Cycle

The graph explains the changes occurring at each resource level across the project life cycle. The levels are shown on the y-axis, and the time is shown on the x-axis. In the defining phase, there are fewer people. In the planning phase, SMEs are added to the team. In the execution phase, the maximum number of people are added to the project, and in the closing phase, the major portion of the work is completed, so a smaller team may remain to turn the results over to the customer. Starting with the defining phase, you will discuss the project proposal, which is used to drive the project selection process that you learned about in an earlier section. The defining phase ends with the decision to accept or reject the project proposal and, if approved, the project sponsor will approve the project charter.

You will learn how in the planning phase the project requirements are gathered from the stakeholders and the project team identifies the work activities necessary to produce the project deliverables. The planning phase ends when the approval of the project scope, budget, and schedule are approved.

You will learn the "real work" of creating the project outcomes. The execution phase is when the team uses the plan to produce the project outcomes. You will discuss how the linear process is interrupted by an iterative process. The iterative process occurs when monitoring and controlling processes are used to ensure that the project execution is going as planned. If the monitoring and controlling identify problems, then the team may need to return to planning in order to correct the situation. Once the new plan is approved, the team returns to execution.

## Triple Constraints

This section will conclude by discussing the triple constraints of scope, budget, and schedule, with quality management being key to the monitoring. You will see how the project manager must balance the trade-offs between the three constraints and how to communicate about issues that arise as you monitor the triple constraints.
