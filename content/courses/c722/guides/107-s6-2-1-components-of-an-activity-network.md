# S6 2.1 Components of an Activity Network

## Learning Objectives

Describe the components of an activity network including their sequencing.

## Label Nodes

The critical path method (CPM) was developed for the DuPont Corporation to help with projects in their manufacturing plants. CPM is also used for estimating project activity durations, but CPM assumes that the estimates do not vary, and time can be reliably estimated. The program evaluation and review technique (PERT) as discussed in the previous lesson, and the critical path method (CPM) are two project scheduling methods. Both CPM and PERT were created based on the concepts of identifying activities, determining their logical order, and estimating their duration. The project manager and team can develop networks representing the sequencing of activities and perform schedule calculations. Let’s look at how to begin sequencing project tasks to develop your network/PERT diagram. As you move through the planning process—including requirements, work breakdown, budgeting, etc.—you will identify and add detail requirements for activities and tasks to be completed. You will further define and break down the activities as your knowledge grows. The project manager is responsible for documenting this detail as the planning steps are completed. ## Serial Activities

Serial activities are done in a single stream of tasks. If you look at the previous chart, you recognize that you must do the design, get approval, and then purchase materials before you can do the work. These are serial activities and flow one after the other. You have decided to paint before installing flooring so that the painter and flooring person do not get in each other’s ADA Accommodation way and you do not get paint on the new floor. These are serial activities, so the nodes follow each other.

## Concurrent Activities

If you have the opportunity and the resources to do so, you do tasks concurrently. In looking at the WBS, you have the following activities for the front of the house:

- staffing
- order entry
- dining room design and setup
- bar

Each of these sets of activities can be done at the same time, because they involve different resources. You choose to have a hiring manager function for the team who will do all the recruiting and hiring of the staff for the dining room, kitchen, bar, and administration. This frees up the line managers to get the restaurant ready by participating in activities like setting up the bar or the dining room or dealing with the software vendor for the order entry system, where their expertise is critical. If the team has limited human resources, these activities cannot be done concurrently and must be done serially. Decisions on when something is done concurrently or serially also depend on task dependencies. These are defined as follows: discretionary, where the project manager can choose based on resource availability, including people, money and budget, and other materials mandatory or logical dependencies of one activity or task to another or legal requirements internal dependencies, such as impact on milestones as defined by the project requirements or under management control external dependencies that rely on external resources or forces physical dependencies where primer must be applied before the final paint or where the plumber and electrician cannot work in the same utility closet at the same time

## Sequencing Diagrams

A project schedule network diagram is a graphical representation of the logical relationships or dependencies the team has recognized during the planning phase. You can produce this diagram from the spreadsheet you build using routine software tools or project management software. The picture is easy to produce with the correct tools. The harder job is identifying the tasks and making correct assumptions about what activities and tasks must be done, by when, by whom, and with what relationship to each other and to the given milestones. Resource availability and skill set and level will need to be taken into consideration.

The network diagram can be created at a detail or summary level. Its primary function is to provide everyone involved on the project with a clear representation of the schedule and the critical path(s) so the assumptions made during the planning process can be validated. It is not unusual for the chart to be published with a detailed list of assumptions and analysis.
