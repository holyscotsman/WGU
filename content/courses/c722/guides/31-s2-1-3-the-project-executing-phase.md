# S2 1.3 The Project Executing Phase

## Learning Objectives

Identify each phase of a project.

The phases occurring in the project are explained in the flowchart. The four phases are defining, planning, executing, and closing. Executing is emphasized. The project executing phase is when the bulk of the work to complete the project outcomes occurs. Based on the plans created during the project planning phase, the project team completes the required work activities.

The project outcomes are tangible products (buildings, textbooks, gaming systems, or training manuals) or intangible products (software programs, websites, or events). The intermediate outcomes from work activities are often referred to as deliverables.

If your project is to create a restaurant, one of the important activities would be to select the location. An intermediate outcome that will move you toward opening the restaurant would be the deliverable of obtaining the location for the restaurant. Even more specifically, the deliverable may be identified as the signed contract for use of the facility.

While the team is completing the work activities of the project that are established in the work breakdown structure, progress is monitored to ensure that the budget, schedule, and scope expectations are being satisfied. If any of those expectations are not being met, the project manager can take corrective action to get the project back on track. These will often be reported in status reports to the team and to the customer.

During the execution phase, the team may discover that the planned activities are not sufficient to complete the project’s objectives. If this occurs, they may need to return to the planning phase to adjust the budget, schedule, or scope. Additionally, the customer may identify changes to their desired outcomes, which might require the team to replan parts of the project. In either case, once the revised plan is approved, the team will return to execution and work toward completing the project. Often a formal change management system is used to track and approve changes. Changes impact time and budget and need to be identified and integrated into those estimates.

When managing the execution phase, it is critical to be careful of project scope creep. Scope creep is when you add functionality without caring about the impact on the rest of the project. Sometimes a team member will say, "It is a small thing," or "It would be great if . . ." You will discuss scope creep in more detail later, but it is important to be very careful about creating and delivering what you have contracted for on budget and on time. Scope creep can quietly sneak in and sap money, resources, and time. When changes are made, they should follow the normal change management procedure and be approved by the appropriate parties.

The project execution phase is completed when all the project work activities that produce the promised final deliverables are ready to be delivered to the customer or sponsor.
