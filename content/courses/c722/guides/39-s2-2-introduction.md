# S2 2 Introduction

## Learning Objectives

Consider the following learning objectives as you move through the lesson:

Define the project term triple constraint.

Describe how triple constraint impacts project quality.

## Constraints

According to Merriam-Webster, a constraint is "something that limits or restricts someone or something." So why a triple constraint? What is a triple constraint? Perhaps the most easily understood example comes from a sign sometimes seen in old country stores.

A sign displays a tagline. It has engaging words like fast, cheap, and good, with a reminder of quality. Customers can pick only two options. Essentially this sign advises that fast and cheap often will not be good, cheap and good will not be fast, fast and good will not be cheap. In other words: If you want it fast, you either pay more or get lower quality; if price is the major issue, then it takes longer or the quality is lower; and if you want it good (best quality), then you pay more, or it takes longer.

The triple constraint concept is often shown as a triangle to illustrate that if you change any one side of the triangle, at least one of the other two sides is affected. It is not possible to change any one side of a triangle without affecting the other two characteristics.

Triple constraints triangle with Cost on the bottom left, Time on the bottom right, Scope at the top, and Quality in the center of the triangle. This really makes sense. Whenever you order any product or service, there are three questions you ask:

Time: How long will it take to get? This is both hours of work and calendar time.

You pay more for rush orders or delivery.

Cost: How much will it cost? You want to know how the item fits in your budget.

Can you afford it? Often this includes time and the cost of quality.

Scope: What is the scope? (Or what features/functions does it have? Or how good is it?)

## Quality

And through all of this, you must think about quality. Quality is described as follows:

an aspect of the scope that defines to what degree the characteristics meet the customer’s expectation

a result and impact of the triple constraint (If any one of the triple constraints changes, without changing either of the other two, quality can be impacted.)

paying more for more scope or function and higher perceived quality

## Scope

Quality is sometimes inappropriately used interchangeably with scope. This is not correct. Scope is the number and type of features or functions. Quality is how well the feature meets the requirements of a scope or function. Does it work flawlessly, or feel extra silky, taste better, or shine more? Quality is subjective and often costly, so it does impact the triple constraint.

In managing a project, this can be a big concern. The project sponsor always wants the project completed faster and cheaper but also wants the scope requirements met with quality. There is a customer-defined minimum quality (scope or requirements), maximum budget (cost), and a minimum time (schedule) required to complete the project. Occasionally, there may be government or corporate standards expressing a required level of quality. This is the case where public safety is concerned, as with the design and construction of commercial buildings, roadways, automobile braking requirements, etc.

## Time

It is important to understand that there are two usages of time when thinking about a project. First, there is the calendar time when something will be available. This refers to a date or time of day. Then there is work time required to produce or procure the item. This translates into hours (time used) of labor or hours of equipment and facilities used. This time used is converted into dollars spent and directly impacts the cost side of the triple constraint.

For example:

## Case A: one person working 40 hours

## Case B: two people working 20 hours each

## Case C: five people working 8 hours each

In Case A, it takes 5 calendar days of 8 hours to complete.

In Case B, it takes 2 1/2 calendar days of 8 hours to complete.

In Case C, it takes 1 calendar day of 8 hours to complete.

All three cases take a total of 40 hours of time charged. But each take a different number of calendar days.

## Cost

Cost also goes back to the scope, which defines functions, and each function has costs associated with it. In the case of the triple constraint, cost is the dollar expression of scope and time with an addition for money to be spent on meeting quality expectations. The triple constraint often speaks to schedule but must include time used (hours billed) to understand budget and cost. So in terms of the triple constraint, both usages of time are important.

In your project of starting a restaurant, you want the restaurant built quickly because you cannot begin generating revenue until you have an operating business with customers. There is a maximum amount of money you have available. And you know that to keep customers happy and coming back, you have to serve good food (function), prepare the food well (quality), serve food in a reasonable amount of time (time), and provide a venue with comfortable seating in a pleasant atmosphere—all function and quality issues that could cost money and time. How do you balance these three constraints?

As the owner of the restaurant, you want a high-end Mexican eatery, not a taco stand. This is a scope or functional requirement. When you choose higher-end food, costs will go up and perhaps preparation time will increase, so all three of the triple constraints will be impacted.

This lesson defines each of the triple constraints and gives examples of how a change in one can impact the others.
