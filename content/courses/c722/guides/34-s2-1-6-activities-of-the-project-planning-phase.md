# S2 1.6 Activities of the Project Planning Phase

## Learning Objectives

Describe the activities of each phase of a project.

When the parameters of the project proposal have been clearly developed and the project charter has been approved, the project organization will develop a detailed plan that identifies the activities that will need to occur to complete the project. At this point, the project team begins to form, as key individuals are brought on board. The planning process often requires these additional resources because expert knowledge is required to identify activities, estimate costs, and estimate activity completion times.

While the project manager may or may not be assigned during the defining phase, that person must be named so that they can take the lead in the planning process. There will be costs associated with the development of the project plan as people, office space, supplies, equipment, and other resources will be required to do the planning. The degree of planning varies depending on the project. The goal of planning is to develop a plan that is comprehensive enough that the schedule can be managed, the budget is accurate, and the activities are defined clearly so that team members understand what work needs to be completed. Only then can the team meet the organization's expectations. A project to install new menu boards at your restaurant may require the interaction of only a few organizations and have a duration of only a few weeks. In this case, there may only be a few work activities, which will be executed by experts in each area. The electrician may only need a simple activity explanation such as "Install 115 volt electrical circuit to location X." The electrician would likely have extensive experience in that type of activity and be able to provide a reliable estimate of the cost and time required based on the distance from the electrical panel to the location. Similarly, a procurement professional could identify a supplier of menu boards and arrange the purchasing agreement. The menu board company would provide a standard catalog item that an experienced contractor would install upon delivery. This may be overly simplified, but given the participation of experienced personnel and the relatively straightforward activities, a reliable schedule, budget, and activity list could be created without great detail. Conversely, if the project outcomes are more unique and if the required work is less routine, a significantly more comprehensive plan will be required. The important concept is that the project plan is tailored to meet the needs of the project and the project organization.

Many Integrated Plans? When people who are new to project management think about the project plan, they often focus on a single tool like the Gantt chart, work breakdown structure, or network diagram. These are important tools of project management that will be discussed in other modules, but they are individual tools, not the plan itself. These tools help to create aspects of the plan. The project plan is a robust document that addresses all the activities necessary to complete the project. You may want to think of the project plan as a set of subsidiary plans that address all aspects of project management. PMBOK suggests that planning a project requires several distinct plans addressing diverse functions.

## Management Plan

## Management Plan

## Stakeholder

## Management Plan

Explains how stakeholders will be identified along with their level of interest in the project and influence over the project; this is closely tied to the communications plan since stakeholders will need varying levels and frequency of information

Quality Management Documents how the quality of the project deliverables will be measured Plan and what steps will be taken to monitor and ensure that the quality meets the customer requirement Cost Management Plan

## Management Plan

Change Management Documents how changes to the project outcomes will be managed, Plan approved, implemented, and monitored Process Improvement Plan

Describes how the processes and procedures used on the project will be evaluated to ensure that the most efficient methods are implemented

## Requirements

## Management Plan

Documents how the customer and stakeholder requirements will be identified, integrated into the project scope, tracked throughout the project, and verified at closure

If you were thinking that project planning just meant creating a sequenced list of activities, then the list of functional plans provided in the table may seem overwhelming. Not to worry—these planning areas need to be tailored to match the needs of the project and the project organization. The takeaway from this list is that project managers who properly address all areas of the project increase their likelihood of successfully completing the project. Depending on the complexity of your project, each of these plans will vary in depth. Some may only require a brief narrative.
