# S3 1.6 The Payback Period

## Learning Objectives

Describe how financial concepts are used for project selection purposes.

## Payback Period

The first financial selection mode is the payback period. Payback period calculates the amount of time required to earn back the cost of doing the project. To calculate the payback period, you need to know the cost of the project and the amount of revenue the project will generate in future periods.

If purchasing a new piece of equipment will cost the company $250,000 but will save the company $25,000 per month in labor expenses due to increased efficiencies, the payback period can be easily calculated in months. In this example, the initial project investment of $250,000 will be repaid in 10 months.

Assuming the company has several projects from which to choose, calculating the payback period for each project would allow for a comparison. Remembering that there is a time value of money, earning the invested cost of the project back sooner is better than later, and the lower payback period project would be preferred. Using a payback period model would encourage the decision maker to select a project with a payback period of two years over a project with a payback period of three years. Although this method is very simple to calculate and the information can be easily gathered, it does not take into consideration the additional returns that a project might generate after it reaches its payback. If a company compared two projects and both had the same payback period of two years, how would they choose? Payback period alone will not help because the payback model ignores how much benefit the company gets from the project beyond the payback of the initial investment.

In addition, the traditional payback period model does not take into consideration the time value of money. This means that the cash inflows received in future months are not discounted, so the payback period is actually underestimated. Here is one more example. A company in the business of delivering construction materials to customer work locations uses tractor and trailer combination units for their deliveries. Because the work sites are often unmanned, the company uses a truck-tractor with a crane mounted behind the cab in order to unload the materials. When the truck arrives at the construction site, the driver operates the crane from the trailer while his helper stands on the ground to unhook the loads from the crane. If the helper was not present, then the driver would need to climb back and forth from the trailer to connect and disconnect each bundle or pallet. One of the truck-tractors has reached the end of its useful life, so an operational necessity project has been undertaken to select and purchase a new truck-tractor. One manufacturer has proposed his vehicle with a new crane that can be unhooked automatically. Purchasing this vehicle would eliminate the need to have a helper accompany the driver on every delivery. The crane with the automated uncoupling device will cost an extra $10,000 to purchase but will save the company the cost of having a helper accompany every delivery ($22,000 annually). If all other costs associated with other truck-tractor options are the same, the company could use the payback period model to evaluate how long it would take them to recover the additional purchase cost of $10,000.

So in this example, the company achieves labor savings that pay back the initial additional cost of $10,000 in 5.45 months. This use of the payback period model is a good example of a model that is easy to construct and interpret but overlooks other factors. An obvious factor that is not included is how long either vehicle will last. If the vehicle without the uncoupling device has a significantly shorter useful life, then the company will need to replace it sooner, making it a less attractive alternative. There may be other important externalities that affect the decision such as the vehicle with the uncoupling device reducing the likelihood of work-related injuries. The decision maker may need to use a combination of models to evaluate all the factors that are important to the organization.
