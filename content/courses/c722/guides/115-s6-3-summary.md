# S6 3 Summary

## Summary

The critical path answers the most important question: "How long will the project take to complete?" From this critical information, you can recognize float, or extra time in the schedule. At all points, you must recognize that there are activity estimates, or "best guesses," and you can use this to analyze what you can do with the schedule. You estimate the start and end dates of each task by doing a forward pass or starting from today and going forward to find out your estimated end date. Then you can do a backward pass where you start from the expected end date and work back to the beginning to find out what the latest date is so that you can start each task and the overall project. Schedule compression can be done several times after you get a look at the critical path. It is important to understand dates and their flexibility and resource availability to make this change to the schedule. You need to fully understand all the assumptions and risks around the schedule and be prepared to react to issues or problems that may occur. One activity that can be done is to "crash" the schedule to see how much you can modify it to meet the stakeholders’ needs and desires. Once thought to be totally negative, crashing the schedule has become a respected methodology for planning risk reaction and problem response. The majority of what you have done here is to pay close attention to the schedule and meet the project demands. Essential to this is the estimates for how long a resource takes to complete a task. These are estimates or "best guesses." They will have variances and can cause an overload of a resource. Resource leveling is another project manager task that will be done by the project manager and the team as they view each resources assignment and estimate. The project manager uses many soft skills to become fluent on what needs to be done and how to monitor and manage the schedule through clear communication and resource management.
