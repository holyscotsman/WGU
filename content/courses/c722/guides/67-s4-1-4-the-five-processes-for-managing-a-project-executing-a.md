# S4 1.4 The Five Processes for Managing a Project: Executing and Monitoring and Controlling Processes

## Learning Objectives

Identify the five processes for managing a project.

## The Processes of Executing

The executing processes are what the project team does to create the project outcomes. The project manager and project team are executing the project plan. During execution, the project manager will add team members when needed, and some team members will complete their work activities so that they can move to other projects.

Project execution can be a very dynamic time with people joining and exiting the project team and activities starting and being completed. That can create challenges, as new team members need to be quickly assimilated to the team and receive any required orientation and training so they can efficiently complete their activities and tasks.

While the project team members are completing the project work, the project manager manages resources, communicating with stakeholders, engaging with vendors, and ensuring that the team can complete their work.

It is not unusual for a company to have several projects running concurrently. In that case, the project manager may be negotiating with other project managers to get resources for the project. When project managers cannot reach agreement on resource allocations, the program manager, project sponsor, or the project management office may need to intercede to ensure that the right resources flow to the right projects. The most powerful stakeholder is the one who has control or influence over resources.

The project management processes completed during executing include the following:

acquiring project team members

training and coaching team members

communicating with stakeholders

managing stakeholders

implementing the project plan

documenting change requests assessing team member performance

recognizing and rewarding performance

## The Processes of Monitoring and Controlling

Monitoring and controlling did not appear as a specific phase of the project life cycle. Instead, monitoring and controlling is positioned as an iterative activity that occurs while the project work is being executed.

Monitoring can be separated from controlling (although they work hand in hand). Monitoring involves assessing whether the project plan is being implemented as it was designed and whether the resulting outcomes and deliverables are as expected.

Controlling is the action to get the project team back on track with the plan or making adjustments to the plan to achieve the desired outcomes and deliverables. As you can see, you would not have controlling unless during monitoring you detected some change that needed to occur. However, if your monitoring did not detect any issues that need to be addressed, then there would be no need to make any changes; no controlling needs to occur.

Project management processes that occur during monitoring and controlling are as follows:

keeping the project activities in sync (integration control)

verifying that the deliverables meet the required scope

documenting any requested changes to scope

monitoring the schedule

monitoring the budget

assessing project quality

resolving issues about scope or activities or interpersonal issues

reporting project performance

monitoring project risks

monitoring vendor/provider performance

determining when replanning is needed
