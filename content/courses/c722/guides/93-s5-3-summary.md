# S5 3 Summary

## Summary

In this lesson, you learned that everything you do has some risk to it. Your goal here is to establish a recognition and develop a methodology for recognizing the risks, prioritizing the risks, and planning for how you will respond to the risks. Knowledge and understanding of the performance domains—Uncertainty, Team, Stakeholder, Development Approach, and Measurement—will guide all facets of the risk management process. Should you try to avoid every risk you recognize? The answer is probably no. Avoiding risks can be expensive, and you need to decide if it is worth the investment. The goal of a project manager is to deliver a full-functioning quality product on time to budget. The project manager is a business practitioner with a clear eye to the bottom line of the organization. Risks can add costs and break schedules and even stop a project from being completed. The best plan is to recognize risks, build a budget for them, choose how to react to each risk, and be certain the plan is easily executed by the team. The project manager will either accept a risk, mitigate or avoid it with up-front investment, or transfer the risk. The plan must be clear on what is at risk and the recommended action to manage it. Ultimately, a good project manager will ensure everyone understands the risks and the suggested ways to mitigate them.
