# S6 1 Summary

## Summary

In this lesson, you used the activities identified by the work breakdown structure (WBS) as your starting point for estimating activity durations. You recognized the risk register, budget, and WBS would be developed together with the schedule because each provided information to support the other. Activity durations are best developed by the team members who will be involved in the later execution of the activities. These estimates can be single-point estimates, which are often called deterministic estimates. If there is uncertainty about how long the activity may take to complete, probabilistic estimation methods can be used to integrate a three-point estimate. The optimistic, pessimistic, and most-likely estimates can be combined using the triangular distribution, which is a simple average. They can also be combined using the beta distribution, which places additional emphasis on the most-likely estimate. The lesson focused primarily on the labor component of activity time estimation; however, other time considerations include the time necessary for the delivery of products (ordering/vendor lead time), the time necessary for products to cure (drying time for paint and concrete), and the time equipment will need to be available for use by the project team (machine time or equipment rental). The concept of learning curves was introduced as a way to improve the estimation accuracy of activities, which will be repeated throughout the project. Finally, you drew on the restaurant example to develop a single-point and three-point beta distribution estimate of an activity’s duration.
