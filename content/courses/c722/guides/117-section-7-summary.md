# Section 7: Summary

## Section 7: Summary

This section provided a foundational perspective on managing team dynamics and the unique challenges faced by virtual teams. Change management processes ensure that potential changes to the project plan are identified and properly evaluated before being implemented. If the team arbitrarily changes the project plan without proper evaluation and approval, scope creep occurs. Change management also drives the communication and implementation of approved changes. Integrated change control integrates the change control process within the project to utilize established elements within the project plan, such as the original scope statement, established baseline, identified risks, and predetermined contingency plans. A structured change management approach, consisting of four essential phases—prepare for the change, plan, manage, and reinforce and sustain the change—linked with the project processes, is necessary for successful project management. Monitoring and controlling were discussed as iterative processes that occur during project execution. Monitoring compares the current project status to the approved baselines. Monitoring areas include scope, schedule, budget, risk, and quality. Project quality was further refined by exploring the specific aspects of quality control, quality assurance, and quality management. Together, the three aspects work to keep the project on track to deliver outcomes that meet the customer requirements. Earned value management was presented as a specific monitoring method used to determine project status and make predictions of how the project will end with respect to the baseline budget. The results of the earned value management analysis support the control processes,

which may prompt the project manager to take corrective action, replan parts of the project, or negotiate with the customer to revise the baselines. The final lesson covered the project closure processes and knowledge of integrating the PMBOK 7 performance domains of Stakeholder, Team, Development Approach and Life Cycle, and Delivery. Project closure is a critical process that should be implemented in a structured and disciplined way. It involves specific steps that lead to the closure. Project closure elements included handing off the completed project to the customer in order to receive formal acceptance. The important role of documenting lessons learned was included as part of the process of completing all project documentation. Generally, successful project outcomes create benefits that, in turn, generate value for the sponsor or the organization. To assist the customer in realizing these project benefits.
