# S2 1.2 The Project Planning Phase

## Learning Objectives

Identify each phase of a project.

The phases occurring in the project are explained in the flowchart. The four phases are defining, planning, executing, and closing. The planning phase is when the detailed planning of the project is done. The project planning phase is where detailed planning occurs. The level of detail can be tailored to align with the complexity of the project, but the planning must be sufficient so that the stakeholders are satisfied that the project outcomes will be achieved, and the project team understands the work that needs to be completed. This is often detailed in a work breakdown structure. The project planning phase will require additional resources since people with specific knowledge about the work will need to provide input to the plan. The planning will document information about each activity that will need to be completed in order for the project to achieve its intended outcomes.

Depending on the complexity of the project, the planning phase may require a significant amount of time. Consider the example of a project to build a multistory office building. The defining phase would clarify the size of the building, general cost, expected revenues, and other high-level details. But at the planning phase, specific blueprints would be drawn up and approved, materials and labor costs would be estimated, prototypes may be created, and work activities and tasks would be identified. Together, the planning will result in a reliable scope, budget, and schedule that can be approved and then executed. Quality expectations, measurements, and costs are defined. You officially leave the planning phase when the scope, budget (cost), and schedule (time) are approved by the customer or sponsor and the project team. The approved scope, budget, and schedule become the baselines used to measure project performance throughout execution. The creation of the baselines marks the conclusion of the planning phase.
