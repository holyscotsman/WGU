What Is a Histogram? A histogram is a graph of a frequency distribution in which rectangles with bases on the horizontal axis are given widths equal to the class intervals and heights equal to the corresponding frequencies. Histograms should be familiar to almost everyone. They are often used to show distributions of common items. The following histogram, for instance, displays the heights of black cherry trees in a particular forest. Note, you should not care about the height of each individual tree. You should instead be more concerned with a height range. For example, eight trees are between 70 and 75 feet and 10 trees between 75 and 80 feet. This survey only cares about a height range, possibly because it is very difficult, and not very important, to determine the exact height of each tree.

The resource histogram is a commonly used pictorial representation of resources required at a specific time in the project life. Often used to display specific types of personnel (such as SMEs), it may also be used for allocation of other resources as well. Resources on a project can include things such as people, equipment, machine time, and office space. Project management programs are capable of generating these charts from data input by the project manager. If the project manager knows the hourly rate for each resource, as well as how many hours per day the resource is used (billed to the project), the costs for each resource may be quickly determined and added to the project budget document. You previously saw that project scheduling software allows many options for how information can be formatted and displayed. Project scheduling software allows you to combine the activity schedule and the resource requirements histogram into a single document. This permits you to visually see how much of each resource you will need at any particular time. This information allows you to do resource leveling. As the name implies, resource leveling is the act of leveling the amount of resources needed to be constant over a period of time. Leveling is used in project management to minimize the confusion on the project (resources being constantly added and subtracted) and to rectify situations where resources are being assigned to more work in a period than they can realistically complete. In the above figure, you see a need for one programmer in January, February, and June, and four programmers in March, April, and May. You could look at your schedule and analyze the activities to see if you could level the number of programmers to be constant from the January through June period. For a relatively simple project such as what is shown, this may not seem like a major concern. But what if this was a much larger project, and the numbers of people shown were in the hundreds? You would try to avoid going from 100 to 400 and back down to 100 programmers. What if you could level the number of programmers at a steady 200 during this time period? In addition to reducing the administrative overhead of adding and removing people, there would be less disruption to the project activities as there would not be a constant stream of people joining and leaving the program. As you will see when you get to the discussion on team building, every time there is a change in the project team members, productivity will be negatively impacted. When the project manager levels the use of resources, he first moves noncritical path activities within their slack. This has no effect on the duration of the project, but it can alleviate leveling problems. If this is not effective in correcting the leveling, then resources will need to be added or the completion date will need to be extended by adjusting the schedule of critical path activities. Now consider a project with multiple activity paths. You have a path that you wish to shorten. The resource histogram, in combination with the project schedule, allows you to see where there are other activities which may have resources that they can spare, for a short time. This can allow you to reallocate some resources for one activity to another without negatively impacting the project schedule or the budget. The resource histogram is applicable to facilities and equipment as well as personnel. Therefore, the resource histogram can identify bottlenecks that may occur with limited facilities or equipment. This information is fed back into the scheduling and budgeting to determine if any activities can or should be rescheduled or if there is a need to lease or purchase additional facilities or equipment.
