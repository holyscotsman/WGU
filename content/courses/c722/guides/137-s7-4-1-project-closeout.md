# S7 4.1 Project Closeout

## Learning Objectives

Describe how to close a project.

## Overview

You saw earlier that there is a formal process for initiating a project. This begins with a project charter. When you come to the end of the project life cycle, you have a formal process for this as well. The process is termed a project closeout and has certain documentation that goes along with it. Closing a project consists of finalizing all activities needed to finish the project. It ensures completion of all phases according to the scope and the customer’s expectation and requirements. At this point, the customers are asked to accept the project deliverables and provide feedback. At project completion, lessons learned are collected and shared. Contracts are closed. Participants are recognized, rewarded, and reassigned. Reports are created and archived. Success is celebrated, and the project team ensures that customers receive the ongoing support they need to use the project deliverables successfully.

## Receiving Formal Acceptance of Project Deliverables

Closeout project documents indicate that the project has been completed and that the project sponsor or customer agrees with and approves of the outcomes. The closeout document provides formal acceptance of project deliverables by the customer or project sponsor. Formal acceptance of the project deliverables is of extreme importance for project completion because it indicates that the customer is satisfied with what the project produced. It is the last time that the customer can take exception to any of the project deliverables (the products of the project). Once the customer or sponsor accepts deliverables, it is notified that the project has been successfully completed. Some closeout documents may be relatively brief or extensive. The extent of the document is dependent on the nature of the project, the organization’s policies, and any government documentation requirements that may be required for the nature of the project. Some projects will not have any government-required documents, while others will. These include any projects that impact government regulations, public safety, and public infrastructure. Some examples include the following:

- bridges
- roadways
- commercial/industrial buildings

residential buildings power generation facilities new medical treatments These projects usually have periodic safety checks required, and the closeout will document that these checks have all been performed and passed. Regardless of the need for government-required documentation, care must always be taken to ensure that all project activities are documented. Failure to document all activities could cause issues if there are ever any liability claims against the product of the project after the closeout. It is also key to be sure to understand the processes and procedures of the organizations involved in the project and comply with their needs.

## Creating a Vendor-Closing Checklist

For the vast majority of projects, there will be at least one of three types of procurement. The first, and simplest, is for commodity items. These are items that are often described as "off the shelf," meaning that they are standard items that can be purchased based on cost alone. Second are products specifically designed and built (specialty items) for the project. This may include items such as specialty test equipment, additional (working) facilities, manufacturing equipment (for the product of the project), manufacturing facilities (for the product of the project), or any other items built to specification for the needs of the project. Third are procurements of personnel. This is for personnel hired temporarily on a contract basis for the specific project. The type of personnel may range from unskilled labor to highly skilled contributors and professionals in the field. The key is that they are hired specifically for the project. Checklists can be very useful in verifying that products and services ordered were delivered on time and as ordered. A checklist, as previously discussed, is simply a list of items, attributes, and activities. A specified responsible person checks each item on the list to verify that the items, attributes, and activities are present and were completed. This is often done as items are completed throughout the project, and the lists are finalized at closeout. Commodities and personnel are fairly simple to accept delivery on. Commodities are either what was ordered or not. However, sometimes orders are for multiple items, and checklists to ensure that each item ordered was delivered are required. In regard to personnel, if people are not able to do the task they are contracted for, the contract will have a clause that allows replacement. Checklists can play an important part in hiring temporary personnel as well. Checklists can ensure that every open position has been filled and that each position was filled by a person with the required skills and experiences.

For the third item, the specialty items, the acceptance (checklist) is somewhat more involved. These items will typically have complex technical specifications and performance criteria. There will typically be performance testing, durability testing, quality checks, and other validation to ensure that the product delivered meets all technical specifications and performance criteria and is suitable for the purpose it was designed for. Because of this, there will be a formal acceptance and vendor closeout checklist at the time of delivery. Additionally, specific specialty items may be required at different times during the project life cycle. The checklist for the specialty items will also ask if the item was delivered at the required point in the schedule.

While the cartoon provides a humorous vision of teams adjourning, releasing team members should be taken seriously, whether a single team member is no longer required or the entire team is disbanded. This is an opportunity to assure team members that both their individual and combined contributions to the team’s success were recognized and valued. If individuals do not feel as if their work is valued, they are less likely to generate the same level of commitment in the future. For employees of the organization or outside personnel, the need to clearly end the project involvement is important. Often, organizations require formal reviews or paperwork to ensure clarity that the project has been completed.

## Reasons for Project Termination

It may occasionally be necessary to terminate a project that has not been completed. There are numerous reasons for this, including but not limited to the following: The project may no longer align with company strategy. The project proved to be technically unfeasible. Competitive pressures negate the need for the project. The project is no longer considered to be economically feasible or practical. If any of these (or other) reasons for project termination exist, it is equally important to provide a formal project closeout. The difference, in this case, is that the customer or project sponsor will indicate acceptance for early project termination. The closeout documentation will describe why the project was terminated and will detail the duties and responsibilities of all parties. The amount of payment due for the portion of the project completed is also defined in the closeout documents. Lessons learned are especially important if a project has been terminated early. It can be extremely valuable to learn what not to do in a project. It can also provide early warnings on future projects that may help avert the need to terminate other projects.
