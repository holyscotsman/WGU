## Business Scenarios That Are Projects

Projects bring value to an organization and its stakeholders by adding or improving products or services and sometimes satisfying regulatory or other legal requirements. It is essential to know that successful projects drive business value and customer satisfaction. Value creation is the driving force in achieving successful project outcomes. It involves a strategic process that delivers outcomes that contribute significantly to organizational objectives, create customer value, and meet stakeholder expectations. It is important to understand what the term business scenario means when it is used to describe a specific situation encountered in your professional life. This situation is important in the project management context because there is the potential to use project management approaches to improve the situation. A scenario is "an imagined or projected sequence of events, especially any of several detailed plans or possibilities" (dictionary.com). A business scenario "is representative of a significant business need or problem and enables vendors to understand the value to the customer organization of a developed solution" (opengroup.com). Or, more simply, a business scenario is a description of a business situation or problem that describes the following:

- past events that are relevant
- current key information
- expected or desired outcomes

The more complete the information, the better a decision can be made about action to take for the scenario. Projects are more than just outcomes, deliverables, or performance results. They focus on performance targets necessary to achieve business objectives. They are structured and staffed to overcome challenges or unnecessary work associated with the change and continual improvement necessary to the organization’s survival. Projects are important to all organizations as a means of providing much of the methodology and processes for making an effective change to achieve the mission and goals of the enterprise. Some of the more important benefits include the following: achieving specific objectives and strategies that support the mission of an organization or enterprise meeting customer expectations continually improving the organization’s cost-effectiveness and productivity managing and minimizing the associated risks that have the potential to impact the organization

improving competitiveness pertaining to a business’s products or services within participating markets improving quality and reliability of products and services researching and developing efforts necessary to further the technological capability and reach of the firm meeting government requirements fixing something that is broken

## Projects (Temporary Activity; Specified Life

Span) Versus Operations (Continuous, Ongoing Activity; Indefinite Life Span) Most organizations’ operations are composed of ongoing and permanent activities that sustain dayto-day activities and support the mission of the enterprise. For example, a product-oriented business must have activities that support their business: sales, marketing, manufacturing, field service, order delivery, accounting, etc. Projects support the operations but are targeted temporary efforts that have a specific start date and end date. The personnel for the projects are temporarily assigned and, upon completion of their assigned tasks, return to their normal ongoing organizational role in the enterprise or are assigned to another project. A project has a distinct beginning and end and is an effort to produce a tangible or intangible output upon project completion. The deliverables are integrated into ongoing business operations so the values and benefits of the project work can be incorporated into the organization. This can be permanent or until another change to that operation is required. The success of this transition could mean a change to operational processes or employment training. Imagine completing a healthcare company's internal project to develop a new electronic medical records (EMR) system. The launch of this new system is part of the project. End users will need training on using the new system and adjusting their ways of working to incorporate the EMR system into their daily tasks to realize the benefits. While a project may develop a product or service used in operational work, the need for change to operational work may have prompted the project's initiation. Take a look at how this could happen with the EMR project example:

The need for a new EMR system may have arisen from problems occurring in the organization's business operations. Suppose the EMR system has transitioned to operations, and users have started working with it and found programming bugs. Fixing the bugs would likely be addressed as part of the system's operational maintenance, such as updates and patches, instead of a new project. After it is in operation, the organization decides to add new features to the EMR system. The addition of these new features would prompt a new project. The true value of a project, of course, is the resulting output. Its success or failure is judged by the measurement of the intended objectives against how successfully the project results achieved those objectives. Did the project achieve the quality, features, and capabilities intended on time and within the allotted project budget?
