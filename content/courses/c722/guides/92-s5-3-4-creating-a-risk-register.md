# S5 3.4 Creating a Risk Register

## Learning Objectives

Describe how to construct a risk register.

## Creating a Risk Register

Once you identify and document the risks, create a prioritized chart called a risk register. The risk register lists all risks (external, technical, or organizational) and assigns scores for probability and severity. The combined probability-severity (P*S) score helps you prioritize the risks appropriately. In addition, the risk register lists action plans or risk management options for each risk, as well as the person responsible for responding to the risk.

Use the risk register to document your findings during planning, and add them on a chart for monitoring and control. Your goal is for the register to tell you which risks to watch for and how to respond. The highest score of P*S is the highest priority for risk monitoring.

Another way to represent these items would be on a chart such as the one that follows. Use the same prioritization numbers as in the spreadsheet to place the items on the chart.

The alternative risk register process is in a graph where the x-axis measures the impact and the y-axis measures the likelihood. Moving clockwise, the first grid shows the low impact and high likelihood, the second grid shows the high impact and high likelihood, the third grid shows low impact with low likelihood, and forth grid shows the high impact and low likelihood. The factors that affect the graph are the kitchen staff's availability, the customers not liking the menu, late delivery of appliances, and the inability of suppliers to deliver food on time.

When you look at the charts, you see all types of risks that are real possibilities, and you set a priority based on probability and severity. These are best estimates based on experience and knowledge of the project. The project manager must then monitor these items to be sure you recognize them should they occur and react as planned.
