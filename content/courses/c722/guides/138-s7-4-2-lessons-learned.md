# S7 4.2 Lessons Learned

## Learning Objectives

Describe the steps in performing a lessons learned review, as part of project closeout.

## The Lessons Learned Review

Sometimes called an after-action review (AAR)—especially by the military, first responders, and other uniformed services—the lessons learned review teaches current or future project managers, project teams, and others what can work well and what may not work well. This can help avoid unnecessary expenditure of resources on future projects.

While every project can, and should, be an opportunity for the project team members to improve their skills and broaden their knowledge base, there are subsets of information that can be obtained:

- What did we do right?
- What can we do better?
- What went wrong?
- How can we prevent what went wrong then from going wrong next time?
- What Went Right?

Reviewing what went right and recognizing team members who made extraordinary contributions can be a great learning experience and a great motivator. Reviewing and understanding what worked well is a good foundation for future projects, which can help avert problems down the line. This, however, is not a benchmark to meet. It is a baseline from which to improve. Recognizing what went right is a good motivator in at least two respects. First, people tend to get more energized when discussing what went right then they do going over what went wrong. Having the project team recall the good job done gives positive energy that can be carried over to the next project. Secondly, recognition for good work motivates people to continue. People want to know that their efforts are appreciated. Documenting all of this creates a database of information and experience that will serve the organization in the future if properly used. A great example for this is understanding the actual time or schedule, resources, and budget that was utilized. Building a future project plan based on past success will reduce risks. An approach of "we did a good job, and let’s see what we learned to do even better in the future" works much better. It acknowledges that everyone put in an effort and that everyone learned. It gives the opportunity for the "grunt on the ground" to offer suggestions. It engages the team members. Engaging people in decisions is one of the best ways to motivate and educate them. What Went Wrong? There is no denying that sometimes things go wrong. These cannot, and should not, be ignored. This is an opportunity to review and understand issues and anticipate corrective action for the future. It is not a time to place blame. Remember, the project manager is the one who guides all the activities of the project. By extension, the project manager is responsible for evaluating all that went right and all that went wrong. This is a time for learning to avoid the same mistakes, not a time to blame people. Things that go wrong are often the result of the following:

- overpromising and underachieving
- wrong or insufficient resources assigned
- poor communication
