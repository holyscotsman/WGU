# S1 2.6 Project Management Organizations and Their Standards

## Learning Objectives

Identify globally recognized project management organizations and their standards.

## The Purpose of Using Project Standards

As project management organizations grew in size, professional standards were developed as a way to capture, codify, and allow for the systematic growth of the knowledge base of the practitioners. The first editions of the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide) and Projects in Controlled Environments, Version 2 (PRINCE2) methodologies were published in 1996 and in the late 1980s, respectively. These standards have been revised based on feedback from the practitioners who use them in different industries around the world. This helps to ensure that they are relevant and reflect the best practices of the time. Both the PMBOK and PRINCE2 standards outline best practices but may not apply to every project in every company in every industry. It is up to the project manager and enterprise to make those decisions. Many companies have their own project management processes and standards that a project manager needs to understand and adhere to. The standards offer the practitioner a "standard" that they can compare their company's processes against and identify areas for current and future improvement.

The components of the model are planning, starting up a project, initiating the project, managing project delivery, control of stage, managing stage boundaries, project mandate, directing a project, corporate and program management and closing a project.

## Contributing Organizations and Their Standards

There are three major project management organizations—the Project Management Institute (PMI), Association for Project Management (APM), and International Project Management Association (IPMA)—and one project management joint venture between a government office and a private company (AXELOS). Each provides

invaluable information to project managers globally. All were started in the late 1960s and early 1970s in conjunction with the development and maturation of tools and methodologies in industries.

Project Management Institute (PMI) With more than 700,000 members, the Project Management Institute (PMI) is the largest in the world. Founded in 1969, it is headquartered in Newton, PA, and has member chapters on every continent (except Antarctica). PMI publishes two types of standards: foundational and practice. The foundational standards provide frameworks and processes in the areas of project, program, and portfolio management and the Organizational Project Management Maturity Model (OPM3). The practice standards focus on specific knowledge areas or topics such as risk management, scheduling, and estimating.

Association for Project Management (APM) The Association for Project Management (APM) is a UK-based charter organization. The association is a registered charity with more than 21,150 individual and 550 corporate members, making it the largest professional body of its kind in Europe. Formed in 1972, its membership is primarily limited to the United Kingdom.

International Project Management Association (IPMA) The next largest project management organization is the International Project Management Association (IPMA). IPMA was the world’s first project management association, founded in 1965. Currently, IPMA is composed of more than 55 Member Associations (MAs) that develop project management competencies in their geographic areas of influence. IPMA is the leading authority on competent project, program, and portfolio management (PPPM).

AXELOS Ltd. AXELOS Ltd. is a joint venture between the government of the United Kingdom and Capita PLC. It was formed to manage and grow a number of standards and associated certifications developed by the Cabinet Office, including ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure Library), PRINCE2, and Managing Successful Programs

The goals of these organizations are to put structure around the work done and to create a standard level of professionalism for managing projects. PMI is the largest organization with international standards for setting rules and methodologies. It also has provided a professional certification so that companies or organizations using project managers can identify serious commitment to the discipline. The other organizations are more limited in their commitment to geography or discipline. APM is primarily European, and IPMA and AXELOS are specific in their interest in standards and are more like aggregates of organizations that seek larger distribution of their work. PMI is aimed at being the go-to international organization for project management standards and professionals.

## Project Management Certifications

Using the standards described above as a basis, for those choosing to make project management part of their career plans and to validate their skills, a number of project management–related certifications are available to the practitioner. The PMP (Project Management Professional) certification has the largest number of practitioners registered globally. In some geographic regions such as Europe, PRINCE2, APM, and IAPM certifications may be required for project management professionals. The growth in PMI membership and individuals holding accreditation attests to the value and relevance provided by the certifications. Recent growth has stabilized in North America and Europe and spread in South America and Asia (blog.sukad.com). PMI membership has grown to over 700,000, with over 1,448,439 active holders of the PMP certification. This reflects the global nature of both project management and the certifications.
