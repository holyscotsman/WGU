# S6 3.2 Schedule Compression

## Learning Objectives

Describe schedule compression techniques.

## Schedule Compression

Schedule compression is the reduction of the overall project timeline to the least possible length for the least cost to the project. Those are measured in cost to the project or change of budget, time increase or reduction, or loss of functionality, all from the triple constraints. The goal is to get the project delivered faster. Generally, this is done because the owner or sponsor of the project has a fast and hard deadline for the deliverables of the project. You can use tools like crashing to compress the schedule.

## Schedule Compression Techniques

To compress the schedule, the project leadership and team must find ways to do the following: Do the work faster—using more or better resources often results in more costs or reducing quality. Do less work—finish the tasks with less functionality. Reduce the quality of the work, accepting the potential consequences. This requires the team to review every requirement and the associated tasks and work plan. The team must question every assumption and look for reduction of effort and elapsed time. It is critical that if recommendations for change are created that this be widely communicated to all stakeholders so that no surprises occur and objections have a platform before there are issues. A well-designed change control system should be used.

Advantages and Disadvantages Between Schedule Compression Techniques Schedule compression techniques can cause increased costs or reduced functionality or quality. The best outcome for most stakeholders is no loss of functionality. This, however, is most frequently achieved by moving functions to a future phase that is completed later to fulfill the original requirement. Stakeholders lose faith in the project if functionality is not delivered. The addition of resources generally results in meeting the functionality desired on delivery but increases costs. Often it is hard to get more money to meet the stakeholders' demands. The team must be careful to evaluate each scenario for compression and choose the one that most meets the real-world expectations and resource availability.
