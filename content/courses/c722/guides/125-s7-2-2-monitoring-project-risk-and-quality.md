# S7 2.2 Monitoring Project Risk and Quality

## Learning Objectives

Describe how managers monitor against project baselines.

## Monitoring Project Risk

During project planning, project risks are identified and documented in the risk register. Recall that the risk register is a list of potential risks, how the risks will be monitored, and what action will be taken should the risk event occur.

Throughout the execution of the project, the risk register is monitored to identify the status of each risk item. First, the project manager monitors to determine if a risk event has occurred that will prompt the team to execute the planned risk response.

An example would be if the team identified a potential risk that low weather temperatures could delay the installation of concrete floors. If during monitoring it is determined that temperatures are reaching critically low levels and the concrete pouring task is reaching its scheduled start date, the risk response will need to be enacted. The response may include rescheduling, purchasing an antifreezing additive, or the acquisition of heaters.

The risk register is also monitored to remove risks that are no longer a threat to the project. If the "pour concrete" task has already been completed prior to the arrival of low temperatures, the risk of low temperatures is no longer a threat, and that risk no longer needs to be monitored.

Monitoring of the risk register is an ongoing effort to ensure that status of potential risks are known and that risks that are no longer a threat to project performance are removed from active monitoring.

## Monitoring Project Quality

Remember that project quality is the degree to which the project outcomes meet customer requirements. During project execution, the project deliverables are monitored to ensure that they are on track to create outcomes that will meet the customer requirements.

A character is examining the word “quality” with a magnifying glass. Verifying the quality of the deliverable at the time it is created can save time and cost related to verification and correction before the deliverable is integrated into other activities or concealed by subsequent work.

A corrective action is a process to document quality failures that have occurred and how they will be corrected. The deliverable itself may need to be reworked, and the project plan may need to be revised to ensure that future deliverables do not include the same error. It is important to address the root cause, not just the symptoms, to avoid a recurring problem.

Consider the electrical and plumbing work required for the restaurant project. The jurisdiction inspectors will verify the work before the walls are enclosed so that they can clearly see any errors, and corrections can be made before additional expenses are incurred.

The specific activities involved in quality control, quality assurance, and quality management will be addressed later in this section.
