# S7 2.5 Quality Management

## Learning Objectives

Describe the principles of quality management.

## The Aspects of Quality Management

This approach to project quality can be summarized as the three quality aspects: quality management, quality assurance, and quality control.

There are three quality aspects for determining project quality. The first aspect, quality management, is the process of identifying the customer's requirements and how they will be measured. The second aspect, quality assurance, is a review of the fully defined project requirements to validate and ensure they are feasible and suitable. The third aspect, quality control, is the set of steps taken to ensure you are meeting the standards set during quality management and assurance. You will see the elements of the PMBOK 7 performance domains of Stakeholder, Team, Project Work, Delivery, Measurement and Uncertainty intertwined to uphold quality management, quality assurance, and quality control.

## Stakeholder

Stakeholder satisfaction is one facet of quality. Building collaborative and working relationships with stakeholders will ensure satisfaction.

## Team

Engaged and effective (high-performing) project teams deliver the best quality.

## Work

Quality must be built into the project and project deliverables to meet requirement measures and acceptance criteria. These project deliverables are created using work processes. Processes should be reviewed to ensure they are efficient and effective. Project managers need to balance competing constraints and the workload of their teams to consistently create deliverables that will deliver the desired quality and value. Project teams should develop and share knowledge to improve both current and future projects.

## Delivery

Delivery quality focuses on value as determined by required performance levels. This value may be delivered incrementally in adaptive projects and more likely at the end of predictive projects.

## Measurement

Project teams and their stakeholders want a reliable understanding of project progress— actionable data for decision-making to keep performance acceptable and to ultimately deliver outcomes that generate business value. Effective metrics enable these. Metrics directly related to quality include defects, performance measures, work in progress, and stakeholder satisfaction. Each metric should include the desired baseline and the threshold beyond which corrective action is taken.

## Uncertainty

Project quality can be adversely impacted by uncertainty, ambiguity, complexity, and volatility. To respond to uncertainty, you must gather information, prepare for multiple outcomes, investigate alternative designs, and build in resilience. To deal with conceptual ambiguity, use clear definitions; and for situational ambiguity, use progressive elaboration, experiments, and prototypes. Deal with complexity by decoupling, simulating, viewing from diverse perspectives, balancing data used, iterating, engaging, and building in redundancy. Deal with volatility by analyzing alternatives and using reserves.

## PMBOK 7, 2021

Quality management is the process of identifying the customer’s requirements and how they will be measured. Quality assurance is the process of reviewing and validating that the requirements and measurements are feasible and suitable (i.e., appropriate for the project). Quality control is the process of monitoring and changing project execution to ensure that activities are being executed as planned and will result in meeting the customer requirements. Quality management is emphasized during the planning phase of the project to ensure that the project team understands the customer’s requirements and how the project outcomes will be assessed upon delivery. This effort helps prevent miscommunications that can result in costly rework to the project deliverables. It is also a time when the team and the customer set reasonable expectations about quality and budget for quality. Quality assurance is a review of the fully defined project requirements to validate and ensure that they are feasible and suitable. People often disagree on how to define and measure quality. From a project management perspective, quality is the degree of perfection set for the project outcomes that meet customer requirements. As an example, look at the restaurant project. One of the project’s major deliverables is the business plan, which includes a training guide as part of the operations plan. Quality control is the set of steps taken to ensure you are meeting the standards set during quality management and assurance. It is what you do to ensure that quality is met during project execution. If done correctly, you will meet the standards and pass any quality audit with flying colors.

A work breakdown structure is created for setting up a new restaurant. This partial WBS provides details of the three activities required for the operations plan section of the business plan deliverable. There are six major deliverables procurement systems and procedures, menus, front of house, kitchen, and business plan. Further into the business plan, the deliverables identify six major activities groupings. Drill down to the Operations Plan, three specific activities have been identified: design procedures, create training guide, and write job descriptions Because the training guide is clearly identified in the requirements and it appears in the work breakdown structure (WBS), you can expect that the outcomes of the project will result in having a training guide. However, it is not clear whether the training guide that the project team produces will meet the quality expectations of the customer; that is where quality comes into play. If the training guide does not meet the needs of the customer, the team will need to spend time reworking the activity to produce a training guide that does meet the customer’s requirements. That can result in both schedule and cost overruns, and the expectation of overruns should be managed through change control.

The project team could just create the training guide that they think the customer should have rather than understand what the customer wants. That is risky and may result in costly rework and a negative interaction with the customer. A better alternative is to define the quality of the outcome during the planning process and then develop plans that will achieve the expected quality, meet the available budget, and monitor the execution to ensure that the resulting guide will meet the customer requirement.

## Quality Requirements and Standards

Quality of project outcomes must be determined at the beginning of the project based on the customer requirements. The form that these requirements take will vary depending on the type of project. In some cases, project outcome quality can be determined based on an industry standard. Standards are requirements that are generally accepted by a group of firms that produce similar products or services. These can be formal, such as in the case of the American National Standards Institute (ANSI), which provides written technical standards for many applications. ANSI publishes standards for hardware strength and thread sizes, computer programming languages, pumps, and even eye protection. Each standard is assigned a reference number and includes details on the characteristics a product must have in order to be in compliance with the standard. When a published standard exists, the customer may require that the project outcome meets that standard rather than creating their own standard (e.g., "Product must meet ANSI Z87.1"). If there is not a published industry standard that can be applied to the outcome, the project team must work with the stakeholder to develop an appropriate requirement. Requirements are what the customer needs to achieve from the completed project. Requirements can include references to industry standards or can be written specifically to define the results of the project. For the requirement to fit the project quality framework, it needs two parts: the description and how it will be measured. The description should explain the requirement in clear and complete terms. To simply note that the restaurant project requires a training guide is not sufficient. In the restaurant example, the training guide would need an additional description that clarifies the format of the guide (e.g.,

electronic, online, hard copy); notes which procedures are to be included; identifies at what reading level it is to be written so that the user can comprehend the training; and details how text, diagrams, and pictures will be integrated. Based on the expanded description, specific measures and evaluation methods must be identified. For the training guide example, the customer may specify that each training topic would include 750–1,000 words of instructional text, at least two diagrams, and two pictures. The customer would also include a list of specific training topics and procedures. Additionally, specifying that the typical user of the training guide will have a high school education and no prior experience working in a restaurant would provide a target comprehension level. With specific measures in place, the team and customer must determine how they will be assessed. The number of words in the text, diagrams, and pictures could be reviewed by a representative of the customer who has experience with training materials. Additionally, the words of text can be counted using word processing software. Similarly, the coverage of the listed topics and procedures could be verified using a checklist. The most difficult requirement to measure is providing a training guide that is sufficient for a person with a high school education and no prior restaurant experience. These types of requirements often necessitate creative solutions on the part of the customer and project team. You may agree to have the training guide reviewed by an experienced restaurant supervisor or pilot test the guide with a high school graduate or a training expert. The key is to determine in advance how the quality of the result will be measured. The process of defining requirements to this level of detail is not always easy for the customer, but without it, the team runs the risk of not delivering what the customer wants by not meeting their measure of performance. If done right, initial investment in quality can be high, but money can be saved later by avoiding costly problems and rework.

## Quality Audits

Quality audits are rigorous reviews of the project performance. These reviews are often completed by groups of experts outside of the project team, such as a company’s quality assurance (QA) department or an outside consultant. If the company does not have access to these resources, the project manager may lead the audit. However, if the auditing resources are

provided, the time and cost of the audit must be included in the project plan to support these activities.

Having auditors visit a project team often has a negative connotation. Being audited can feel like you are not trusted or that the company is "checking up on you." That should not be the case in quality audits related to projects. While the audit team will verify that company policies are being followed, that the project plan is being executed as specified, and that quality controls are put in place, the purpose of the audit should provide positive outcomes. These positive outcomes include the following:

- identifying issues before you go into production

identifying best practices that can be adopted by future project teams identifying lessons learned that can improve performance on other projects identifying problems that can be corrected before additional costs are incurred

Quality audits can review specific portions of the project or can be general and review the overall project. An audit could focus on areas including budgeting, risk management, communications, procurement, or any specific area that could improve the performance of the focal project or future projects. Consider the benefits of lessons learned for future projects if an auditor could review a project plan to understand how the requirements were or were not supported. Audit results such as this could improve how the organization manages projects. Quality audits require project resources, so they must be included in the WBS, project schedule, and project budget. The audit team will often need data in the form of project reports, and they may need to meet with project team members. Scheduling the audit keeps the time focused and allows the team to plan for the audit as well as know when they will be able to return to producing the outputs required by the project customer.

## Quality Control

Quality control is the monitoring and controlling process that occurs during project execution. The project team monitors the creation of project deliverables to ensure that what is created achieves the level of quality needed to meet customer requirements. Earlier, we referred to this as an iterative process because during the execution of the project plan, the project team monitors performance (quality in this case) and will make adjustments to the project plan if the quality of the deliverables does not meet customer requirements. Because the project team is monitoring quality during project execution, problems can be identified and corrections made prior to delivering the completed project to the customer. If done properly, quality control guarantees that the customer will accept the completed project because the team has already confirmed and documented their achievement of the customer’s requirement. It is also true that catching a problem early in a project will be less costly than reworking later. The measurement of the quality of a project deliverable can be completed by the project manager, the project team, or a group external to the team such as a quality assurance department or an external consultant. If shortcomings are identified, the project team will need to determine if the project plan was insufficient to meet the quality requirement or if the plan was not executed properly. Based on that assessment, corrective actions are taken to rework the deliverable or replan future activities to ensure that the customer requirement is met.
