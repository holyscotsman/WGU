# S2 2.7 Scenario: The Impact of the Project Schedule

## Learning Objectives

Describe how triple constraint impacts project quality.

## Impact on the Project Schedule

Earlier, you saw a possible situation where you have a winter that is more severe and longer lasting than initially forecast. This postpones the start of construction since you have to wait for the ground to thaw enough to dig the foundation and for the mud to dry so that an access road can be prepared and drivable, and this shortens your schedule. You do not wish to compromise quality. Therefore, you must have some combination of increased cost or decreased scope.

There is a limit to cash available. And there is a maximum you are willing to borrow. So there is a limit on additional resources (labor) that can be added to the project to speed completion. In addition, there is a limit on how quickly materials can be delivered to the site due to restricted road access. There is an option of air delivery, by helicopter, but that is cost prohibitive. Materials could be delivered by boat, over the lake, but this is also too costly. Some portions of the project can be subassembled off-site. Although this may increase shipping costs, this is a viable option when weighed against time saved.

For any real-time savings, the scope must be reduced. Some features of the project may be postponed or eliminated.

The following must be completed:

the house itself

health and sanitation concerns

environmental and safety regulations

the access road

The first thing you decide to postpone is the boathouse. If you do not have a place to live in, you do not need a boat.

An access road must be completed to be able to bring in materials and labor. A graded, packed dirt road is adequate for this and could serve as your permanent access road. You decide to go with the dirt road. It can always be covered by a gravel or asphalt top layer at a later date, if so desired.

Water supply and waste disposal will also need to be completed early on. The quickest way to do this is to siphon water from the lake and have a residential water treatment system installed and to build a septic system. You install both of these with the ability to connect with water and sewer mains at a later date, if desired.

Electrical lines have not been run to your site. You will need power for construction as well as for occupancy. You installed an electric generator and fuel storage tank.

A shed for snowmobiles can be postponed or eliminated. They can either be stored in the garage or covered by a tarp when not in use.

Furniture, rugs, utensils, and so on can be brought in over time as weather and road conditions permit. These do not all have to be in the house immediately.

As you review your plan, you note changes to the scope and schedule. You see the scope changes as a balance between delaying some features, possibly eliminating others, and replacing things in some cases. From your project management courses, you actually see that the postponement is really a cancelation from this project, and you will need to undertake a future project to add them back in.
