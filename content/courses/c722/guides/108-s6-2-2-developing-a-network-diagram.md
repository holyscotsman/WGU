# S6 2.2 Developing a Network Diagram

## Learning Objectives

Construct a project network diagram.

## Relationships Between Serial Versus Concurrent Activities

As you have seen, serial activities must be completed in order, one at a time. Concurrent activities run in parallel (at the same time) and compete for resources and project time. Most projects include both serial and parallel activities. By including concurrent activities, the time from project start to project completion can be reduced. The dependencies of the activities and the availability of skills and resources will impact what can be done concurrently or serially.

## Preparing Data in a Spreadsheet

One of the first steps you take in building a network diagram is to take the activities and tasks from the work breakdown structure (WBS) and build a chart showing them with some additional information developed along the planning cycle: Identify the tasks’ sequence in the process (which step occurs when), and assign node numbers to give each task and activity a unique identifier. Fill in time information—both in elapsed or calendar time—with start and finish estimates and estimated work hours or days. Identify resources. Provide predecessor or successor information on sequencing requirements as tasks may relate to each other. There will be tasks that are not related and therefore can have unrelated numbering. Move this list into a simple spreadsheet to begin to express it to your team and stakeholders. The team will modify and review this list as it develops during the planning process. ## Drawing Diagrams with the Text Box Feature

In Microsoft Office, you will find a SmartArt function in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, and it will create the network chart. As an example, using the list in the spreadsheet prepared earlier, lay out the information by showing the task dependencies by indenting:

## Start Front of Restaurant Setup 3.0

## Design 3.1-8 days

## Approval 3.2-1 day

## Purchase Furnishings 3.3-5 days

## Unpack Tableware 3.10-2 days

## Purchase Paint 3.4-1 day

## Paint 3.5-10 days

## Lay Floor 3.7-5 days

## Touch up Paint 3.75-1 day

## Arrange Furniture 3.8-3 days

## Set Tables 3.11-2 days

## Purchase Flooring 3.6-2 days

To represent a process using Smart Art, there are certain steps to be followed. The first step is to select the Insert button, then select the Smart Art option. This will lead to Choosing a Smart Art Graphic. The categories of the graphic are List Proses, Cycle, Hierarchy, Relationship, Matrix, Pyramid, and Picture. The next step is to select one of the hierarchies.

The Smart Art Chart opens to the selected hierarchy template, which has options for entering the text in a separate text entry window. A horizontal hierarchy is shown. Add the title to the text by double tapping the title text box. The text boxes for the individual blocks and the title box are connected with lines.

A customized horizontal Smart Art Chart is shown. The flow of activities begins on the far left with Design 3.1, 8 days, which leads to the second box, Approval 3.2, 1 day. The third box, Purchase Paint 3.4, 1 day, branches to an upper and lower level. The branch up leads to Purchase Furnishings 3.3, 5 days and Unpack Tableware 3.10, 2 days. The branch down leads to Purchase Flooring 3.6, 2 days. The fourth box is Paint 3.5, 10 days; fifth box is Lay Floor 3.7, 5 days; sixth box is Touch up Paint 3.75, 1 day; seventh is Arrange Furniture 3.8, 3 days; and the final box is Set Tables 3.11, 2 days.

A diagram is created without you having to be a graphic designer.
