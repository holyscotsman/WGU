# S1 1.6 The Relationship Between a Project, Program, and Portfolio

## Learning Objectives

Describe the role projects play in achieving strategic business initiatives.

A pyramid divided into the different sections, which are, from the top down: portfolio management, program management, and project management. Portfolio management includes: led by the business, business goal alignment, program selection, and portfolio optimization. It provides business value. Program management includes: sponsored by the business, ownership of benefits, encompasses multiple projects, alignment of protocols with overall program benefits, and compliance with project management standards. It provides stakeholder value. Project management includes: delivery of product/service; scope, cost, schedule; and responsible for quality of deliverables. It provides quality deliverables.

A system for valued delivery consists of portfolios, programs, projects, and operations. Operating collectively or individually, portfolios, projects, and operations create deliverables used to produce outcomes, where an outcome is a result of a process or project. The outcomes create benefits, which are returns realized by the organization. Benefits, in turn, create value, something of worth, importance, or usefulness. It is important to understand that projects' outputs (deliverables) drive outcomes (benefits), ultimately delivering value to the organization and its stakeholders.

A company will have a portfolio of the projects and programs it engages to improve its operations. A program is a group of projects that support the goals and objectives of the company; it may be established and is a group of related projects. For instance, an automobile manufacturer may have a program to develop a new full-size passenger car. It is known that many new components and systems will be required to produce the level of comfort, quality, and reliability that the market demands. Therefore, there will be a number of new projects within the program to develop a new full- size passenger car program, including

improved seating,

a more efficient engine,

new marketing and sales programs, and

improved car servicing procedures for ongoing service.

This new passenger car program might be a part of a portfolio of programs or new products and services to gain more market share in the passenger car market. Other programs could be new midsize cars and new compact cars. Within programs are organized logical groups of activities that make up a project. Similar activities are kept together and end up with several projects. In short, a program is a group of projects.

A portfolio is a group of related programs that support a long-term company goal or objective. The projects in a portfolio may individually address separate initiatives within the organization but collectively move the organization toward meeting a long-term goal or objective.

A program is a group of related projects.

A project is an activity or group of activities that generate a new, unique product, service, or result to support a program. However, it is possible that not all projects will be part of a larger program. There could, for example, be a project to develop a new battery that is more adaptable to more extreme temperature conditions. The product of this project could be applicable to all existing automobiles and not strictly a single program.

Viewing them as part of a system of value delivery becomes clear and more effective when the sharing of information and continuous feedback among all components will keep the system aligned with strategy. Here is an example of the information flow:

Senior Management shares strategic information with portfolios about new passenger cars. >> Portfolios share the desired outcomes, benefits, and value with programs and projects. >> Program and project deliverables are passed on to operations, along with information on support and maintenance for the deliverables.

If you examine information flow in reverse, from operations to programs and projects, this may suggest adjustments, fixes, and updates to deliverables.

Programs and projects provide performance information and progress on achieving the desired outcomes, benefits, and value for portfolios. Portfolios provide evaluations of portfolio performance with senior management.

Additionally, operations offer information on how well the organization's strategy is advancing.
