# Section 5: Introduction

## Section 5: Introduction

The six project management concepts shown in the chart are project selection criteria, project life cycle, project management methods, project planning, project scheduling, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing project work. This section includes four lessons that will take you deep into the mechanics of planning a project. You will begin with an overview of project planning and an overview of the integrated performance domains used to develop the project scope. While an initial scope is created as part of the defining phase of the project life cycle, a detailed scope must be developed based on a

solid understanding of the customer requirements. The rigorous collection of customer requirements and the creation of the approved project scope occurs during the planning phase of the project life cycle. The scope will clearly and completely define the project outcomes and how the customer will measure the outcomes to ensure that their requirements are satisfied. The team will use the approved project scope to identify specific deliverables that will need to be created to satisfy the project outcomes. The process of identifying the deliverables and the subsequent work activities that will need to be completed to generate the deliverables is called work breakdown. You will learn the process of breaking down the project into specific deliverables and work activities such that a budget and schedule can be created. The resulting work breakdown structure (WBS) is a key document that will be used for cost estimates, time estimates, risk identification, and resource assignment. You will be introduced to the concept of risk and how it applies in a project management context. A process will be discussed whereby the WBS is used to identify risks and create a risk register where the risks can be managed. With the WBS and risk register as a foundation, you will learn about methods for estimating the overall project cost, high level costs, and detailed costs. These approaches to cost estimation will be integrated to develop a simple overall project budget and a detailed calendarized budget that will help the organization ensure that cash is available when required to pay project expenses.
