# S1 2.1 Project Management and the Project Manager

## Learning Objectives

Describe the role of a project manager.

## Project Management and the Project

## Manager

The previous lesson defined a project as a temporary activity that creates a unique result. Project management is the act of managing a project and encompasses the use of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to ensure that the project meets its goals. The knowledge, skills, and tools that are used can come from general management skills or specific project management skills. General management skills would be communication skills and techniques, while schedule development, PERT charts, work breakdown, and risk management can be seen as project management skills and techniques.

The project manager should have knowledge of the project management principles and the eight project performance domains as depicted by the PMBOK 7 guide.

The eight project performance domains function as an integrated system, where each domain is interdependent on the others, ensuring the project's successful execution and the achievement of its desired results.

Each domain provides a set of activities and functions that are adaptable to an organization's specific needs and capabilities, among other variables.

## Project Manager’s Skills

In addition to these competencies, project managers must have a variety of skills that involve the ability to deal with people. These "people skills," or "soft skills," are vitally important since the project manager will have to interact with subject matter experts (SMEs) from many different functional areas. In some cases, the project manager will have little, if any, real authority and will need to negotiate for resources. Even if they do have real authority, the project manager will be dealing with a variety of stakeholders, each with a different priority. Some stakeholder priorities may even be in conflict, if not mutually exclusive. If any of these stakeholders are from outside the company, such as a customer, then whatever authority the project manager has will be irrelevant. The project manager will have to rely on people skills, including these skill sets:

## Key Skills

Two key skills worth mentioning are leadership and critical thinking.

## Leadership

Project managers demonstrate leadership behaviors throughout a project, including the ability to influence others, which will influence project results.

## Critical Thinking

The project manager is responsible for applying critical thinking skills to manage a project effectively. Critical thinking involves the following:

gathering unbiased information

responding logically and without bringing more emotions to the situation

using analytical skills to resolve issues

analyzing data that will inform decision-making

being aware of relationships and related patterns

## Emotional Intelligence

Emotional intelligence is a set of interpersonal skills associated with having tremendous leadership success. It describes the ability to perceive, evaluate, and control emotions in self and others.

For instance, an emotionally intelligent project manager can establish and maintain positive relationships by adjusting communications and anticipating the needs of others. An emotionally intelligent project manager understands how emotion can drive the behaviors of others and can use this understanding to deal with the issues and concerns of the team.

Emotional intelligence is a practice that can be learned and developed, centered around self- awareness, self-management, social awareness, and relationship management.

Emotional intelligence enables a project manager to bring out the best in coworkers and team members by making them feel valued and important.

## Project Manager Responsibilities

The project manager (PM) is responsible and accountable for managing the project to maximize its success. PM responsibilities include the following:

initiating the project with clear goals, sponsorship, and a business case that aligns with the goals of the organization

overseeing the development and acceptance of a realistic project plan that meets the requirements approved for the project

The PM should have complete knowledge of the project plan, which should include

approved requirements for the product, service, or process being produced;

a schedule for the project;

resource plans/requirements, including people (time), money (budget), material, equipment, and facilities;

a strong communications plan;

a risk plan identifying risks and actions; and

a statement of quality standards and directions.

Both the resource and cost plans are based on activities in the schedule and include the team members’ skills and assignments. All subcontracting activities should be included.

The final scope, schedule, and budget plans are captured as baselines for the project, which the sponsor approves before the project moves into the execution phase. Project performance is measured against the baselines in these three areas. This is often referred to as the triple constraints of a project (time, cost, and scope).

The project should be executed to the defined baselines: Team members come onto the project, understand their responsibilities, and execute to them; materials are purchased and used efficiently.

Change control process should be clearly defined: Doing this will drive discussions and control actions necessary to approve all changes. In many cases, the schedule, scope, or cost baselines will be modified with the approval of the sponsor or customer.

Lessons learned should be shared: When the project is complete or if it is terminated, the project manager is responsible for ensuring that all contractual requirements are closed and that the knowledge base of the organization is contributed to.

## Project Management as a Career

Project management has become a well-respected title and career. It is recognized across industries and throughout disciplines. In a recent article by PMI, Tim Wasserman, program director of the Stanford University Advanced Project Management program said, "The ability to convert strategy into action is the emerging skill of our time." When project managers can grasp the larger ecosystem in which the project lives, they are able to create more agile processes that lead to improved outcomes —not just for the organization but also for the economy.

And employers realize the demand for project managers are increasing. According to a PMI- commissioned study on talent gap analysis, completed by Anderson Economic Group (AEG), its predicted growth in job opportunities and career growth for project managers within the 11 countries studied greatly increased. The project management–oriented labor force in seven project-oriented sectors is expected to grow by 33%, or nearly 22 million new jobs.

## Managers

In some organizations, project management is not a distinctly separate role in the organization and can be the role of a functional manager for the duration of the project. An example would be if the product design manager was also responsible for the operational delivery of the product. This person is referred to as a working project manager as compared to a dedicated project manager whose role in life is to be a project manager regardless of the operational needs of the business.

There are some problems with the use of working project managers:

Management of the project is usually the second role that the person has. The functional role is usually where their expertise, emphasis, and passion lie.

Besides a lack of time to correctly manage the project, the working project manager may also not have project management skills or training.

People from other disciplines may not report directly to the working project manager. However, the working PM is responsible for the work they perform related to the project. This is sometimes referred to as a matrix organization.
