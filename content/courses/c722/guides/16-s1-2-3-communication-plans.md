# S1 2.3 Communication Plans

## Learning Objectives

Describe the communication methods used by the project manager.

## Communication Plans

Although the core tools that are used may be the same, each project needs its own stated communication plan. This sets the stage for what is required and guides individual expectations. Plans need to speak to what is expected, what is completed, what issues have been identified, what steps are needed to solve the issues, and what will be done in the next period between communications. They also need to address any changes and plan for resources and integration.

## The Purpose of Project Status Meetings

An important part of the communications plan is determining how project status meetings will be used to exchange information. An organization and the project manager will define at the beginning when project status meetings will occur and what level of reporting will be done. These decisions include defining the purpose of the various status meetings and project reports. Often, these decisions are influenced by stakeholder analysis. At the beginning of the project, the project manager conducts the analysis to define the relevant stakeholders, their interests, and their communication needs. A key factor is that all the status meetings and project communications need to be timely and regular. Information and communication requirements that are not met by the project communications plan can often lead to harmful assumptions by stakeholders. A different schedule and design of the status reports may be required for stakeholders depending on their direct involvement with the project.

It is through the status meetings that the project manager will express the following:

Current status summary of the project’s schedule, budget, and functional progress

"Red Light" issues: These are things that will immediately impact the success of the project and what decisions or resources are required. These often are potential change requests or emphasis on items planned for but not received.

"Yellow Light" issues: These may be impending issues arising for the future of the project, plus recommendations for how to prevent these issues from becoming problems.

## Resource usage and near-term requirements

In a weekly status meeting, the project team determines progress on scheduled tasks and deliverables and discusses issues and risks. Another weekly meeting focuses on problem-solving by functional teams. In weekly reports, the project manager communicates the data collected. The sponsor and senior manager attend a monthly multi-project review presentation that includes milestones/deliverables, significant risks, and issues.

This figure shows one example. The project team has a weekly status meeting in which they focus on determining the status of the project. They will also discuss the status of the issues and risks that they are actively managing. The project manager is responsible for turning this data into communicable information about the status of the project, which is then distributed in the form of a project report. This progress report is distributed to the stakeholder(s) identified in the stakeholder analysis to receive a weekly update.

On a monthly basis, this project may be part of a multiproject review (program review) in the business division. The audience is the sponsor, and other senior managers may or may not report directly to the sponsor or may be in a matrix to the sponsor. Because they are reviewing multiple projects that may be part of a portfolio, attention is focused on the status of the project in meeting major milestones and making key deliverables. The sponsor’s interest in risks and issues is generally confined to the ones with the most potential impact to the project. These higher-level audiences will also want to know if there are ways in which they can support the project.

Change requests (CRs) for changes in scope, schedule, or funding should be submitted to the appropriate change control board (CCB) for a decision. Although the CCB may have a significant overlap in audience, the purpose and preparation for the meetings are different and worth keeping separate. The CCB ensures that the change does not negatively impact the project triple constraints.

In addition to these meetings, a charter or contract may require regular meetings and reports with an internal or external customer. The type and frequency of these status reports and meetings are usually defined in the contract or agreed to early in the project.
