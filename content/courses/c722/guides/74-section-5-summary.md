# Section 5: Summary

## Section 5: Summary

In this section, you learned and applied skills to begin building the project plan. You were enlightened with how the performance domains (Stakeholder, Team, Development Approach, Planning, Delivery, Measurement, and Uncertainty) cut across the planning process.

The project charter developed in the prior lesson authorized the existence of the project and provided a high-level view of the project. The lessons in this section transitioned into the details of collecting customer requirements and developing an approved project scope. The project scope identified the customer’s expectations with regard to deliverables, cost, time, and quality. While the preliminary scope developed in the defining phase of the project life cycle provided an overview, the project scope developed during the planning phase is detailed enough to guide the team in identifying exactly what work will need to be done, how much it will cost, and how much time it will take.

You learned how to decompose the overall project into deliverables and work activities using work breakdown. The resulting work breakdown structure (WBS) provides activity-level details that identify the work to be done and allows the project manager to assign specific people to complete the work.

You were introduced to the concept of risk and how it applies in a project management context. Essentially, risk is the likelihood that some event will occur that will prevent the project team from meeting the approved scope, schedule, and budget. The process of identifying risks based on the activities in the WBS was used to create the risk register. The risk register was discussed as a document that will be used throughout the project life cycle to plan for risk responses, monitor risks, and close out risks that are no longer a threat to the project.

Top-down and bottom-up cost estimation techniques were also introduced. Top-down methods can provide reasonable estimates of high-level costs based on historical knowledge. Bottom-up methods rely on project team expertise to identify work activity level costs and then aggregate them up to the total project. It was proposed that the two techniques can be used together in order to negotiate an approved project budget that meets the cost expectations of the customer and the cost realities of completing the work. You then applied the cost estimation methods to create both a simple overall project budget and a more detailed calendarized budget that identified the time periods when the expenditures would occur.
