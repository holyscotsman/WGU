# Section 4: Introduction

## Section 4: Introduction

The image is a circular-shaped infographic illustrating six interconnected concepts that define the Project Planning within the broader scope of Project Management. At the center of the circular design is the title “Project Management,” surrounded by six evenly spaced nodes arranged in a closed loop, visually emphasizing the cyclical and continuous nature of project management processes. Starting at the top and moving clockwise, the first node is Project Selection Criteria, represented by a clipboard icon to indicate the evaluation and selection of projects based on strategic alignment. Next is the Project Life Cycle node, highlighted in green to signify its central role in tying all phases together. Following that is Project Management Methods, illustrated with a pencil and ruler icon, symbolizing structured frameworks like Agile or Waterfall. Continuing around the circle, the Project Planning node appears with a checklist icon, representing the organization of goals, timelines, and resources. This is followed by Project Scheduling, marked with a clock and calendar icon to reflect time coordination and milestone tracking. The final node is Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing Project Work, displayed with a gear and document icon, capturing the execution, supervision, and formal closing of the project. The circular shape, flowing structure, and consistent navy and green color scheme reinforce the continuous, interconnected nature of managing projects from initiation to completion. This section will build on the knowledge of how the project life cycle describes the doing of project work to the managing of the project itself. There are many processes required to manage a project, and they are organized into five process groups. The groups will be defined, and examples of activities included in each group will be provided. This section will also provide a comparison of traditional, agile, and extreme project management approaches. While this course focuses primarily on traditional approaches—which follow the chronological life cycle stages: define, plan, execute, close—there are other approaches you can use when the project outcomes or environment is less certain.
