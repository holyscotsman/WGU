# S5 2 Summary

## Summary

In this lesson, you looked at how to take the requirements of the project and translate them into concrete deliverables, breaking them into work packages that can be assigned to individuals who will be responsible for meeting the obligations associated with the project. This is a great time to bring the current project members together and begin team building. It is also the time to get buy-in by individuals as they begin to understand the project at a more detailed level. The work breakdown structure (WBS) takes deliverables and breaks them into activities to be done, breaking the activities further into tasks that can be assigned to individuals. You should understand exactly what has to be delivered and how you define how the work will be done. This gives you budget information as you get some estimate on what skills are required for the task and how many work hours are required—the "who" and the "how long." During this process, you build an understanding of what activities must be accomplished to produce the deliverables. For example, if you need the dining room ready for customers in October, your experts review the activities that go into preparing the restaurant design, painting, and installation of flooring, furniture, and fixtures. You talk to your painter and break down tasks for paint selection and purchase and labor for prepping and painting. This involves more tasks than the obvious one of "Let's paint the dining room." Now you have a work breakdown that can be assigned to the right people.
