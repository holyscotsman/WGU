# S6 2.3 Task Relationships and Dependencies

## Learning Objectives

Describe task relationships and dependencies in network diagrams.

## Constructing Task Relationships

Task relationships are critical for creating accurate and realistic project plans and schedules. You gain the beginnings of your understanding of relationships from reviewing the project scope, product scope, WBS, and any other relevant requirement documents. Relationships depend on the following:

- project deliverables
- project constraints like dates and competing projects or demands
- project assumptions from requirements
- milestones
- budgets

project environmental factors like standards, tools, work authorization systems, and government regulations To clearly understand task relationships, the activities and tasks must be reviewed by the team. Key stakeholders that are internal to the organization or external (including government, customers, suppliers, or strategic partners) can define requirements and constraints, and these must be included in the plan. While the SmartArt function can create a basic network diagram using the basic task relationships where the successor begins when the preceding task has been completed, there are other task relationships that can occur in a project. The most common relationship between tasks is the finish-to-start relationship, but it is important to understand all the possible relationships.

Finish-to-Start Relationships (FS)

There are two Approval boxes and a Purchasing box in the Finish-to-Start relationship. There is an arrow in blue directed from one box to the other. The process of finish and start is written below the boxes to indicate the same. Finish-to-start is a logical relationship in which a successor activity cannot start until a predecessor has finished. The purchase of materials—including paint, flooring, furniture, and decorations—cannot be done until the design is approved by the owner.

Finish-to-Finish Relationships (FF)

There is one Approval box and a Design box in the Finish-to-Finish relationship. There is an arrow in blue directed from one box to the other. The process of design and approval is indicated in the box. Once the owner gives approval for the decor after reviewing the design proposal, the process is complete.

A finish-to-finish relationship is a logical relationship where the successor task cannot be completed until the first task is completed. You cannot, for instance, finish the design until the owner gives you approval of the decor after reviewing the design proposal. The design will need to be refined as the owner reviews it, and the owner will not approve it until his feedback is integrated into the design. While the design task starts first, it will not be completed until the approval task has been completed. The approval task will begin after some initial progress on the design has occurred. Both tasks will finish at the same time.

Start-to-Start Relationships (SS)

Two boxes are labeled Install Pipe and Fill Trench. This is the process of the Start-to-Start relationship. There is an arrow in blue that is directed from the Install Pipe box down to the Fill Trench box. The process of design and approval is indicated in the box. The activity to be conducted by the successor cannot be started unless the predecessor begins the work. In a start-to-start relationship, you cannot start the successor activity until the predecessor activity has started. These relationships are less common and do not occur in the restaurant example. An example from gas pipeline construction would be that as soon as the crew begins placing the pipe into the trench, the process of filling in the trench can occur. That way, the tasks are occurring at the same time but recognize that filling the trench cannot begin until the pipe is placed into the trench.

Start-to-Finish Relationships (SF)

There are two boxes labeled Paint and Lay Floor. This is the process of the Start-to-Finish relationship, as the arrow moves from the Paint box at Start to the Lay Floor box at Finish. The Start-to-Finish process completes when the tasks are performed sequentially. A start-to-finish relationship is where you cannot finish the first task until the second task has started. This is often where there is task dependency. In the case of your restaurant, a cooking shift cannot leave until the second shift has arrived and started. This prevents orders from being delayed for customers in the dining room. In the case of the construction project, the painter does work first but cannot leave until the floor is done because the walls may need a touch up after the flooring is put down.
