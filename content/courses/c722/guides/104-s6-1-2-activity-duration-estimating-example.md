# S6 1.2 Activity Duration Estimating Example

## Learning Objectives

Construct an activity duration estimate.

## Activity Level Estimation

You will use the work breakdown structure (WBS) that you developed for the restaurant project. Six major deliverables were identified. Drilling down in the business plan, the deliverables identify six major groupings of activities. If you drill down in the operations plan, three specific activities have been identified: design procedures, create training guide, and write job descriptions.

A work breakdown structure is created for setting up a new restaurant. This partial WBS provides details of the three activities required for the operations plan section of the business plan deliverable. There are six major deliverables procurement systems and procedures, menus, front of house, kitchen, and business plan. Further into the business plan, the deliverables identify six major activities groupings. Drill down to the Operations Plan, three specific activities have been identified: design procedures, create training guide, and write job descriptions

Focus on developing a duration estimate for the "create training guide" activity. You can assume that the training guide activity is closely related to the "design procedures" activity in that the training guide will provide written instructions explaining how each procedure is to be completed.

You have completed the first step in creating good duration estimates by working at the activity level. Next, you need to engage the person who will do the work in providing the estimate. Thankfully, Carol is on the project team, and she has more than 10 years of experience opening and operating restaurants.

Carol estimates that there are 20 specific procedures that will need to be documented. You should coordinate that estimate with the "design procedures" activity because the procedures will first be designed and then handed off to Carol for the creation of the training guide.

## Single-Point Estimates

Assuming that you believe 20 is a reasonable estimate, you ask Carol to provide a single point estimate of how long it will take to create a training guide. Carol suggests that based on her experience, each procedure would require approximately four hours. The single-point estimate would be 80 hours (20 procedures × 4 hours each). She also notes that since this is a totally new restaurant and the experience of the staff is unknown, it could take longer. Based on that knowledge, the team can include additional time for the overall activity or use an estimation technique that recognizes variation in estimating.

## Three-Point Estimates

You realize that there is some uncertainty in the estimate, so you ask Carol to provide a three- point estimate that would include the best-case (optimistic), worst-case (pessimistic), and most- likely estimates. Carol gives this some thought and determines that it cannot take less than three hours per procedure (optimistic) but that sometimes it has taken as much as seven hours per procedure (pessimistic); overall her initial estimate of four hours is most likely.

## Hours per Procedure Number of Procedures Total Hours

## Optimistic Estimate (O) 3 20 60

## Most Likely Estimate (M) 4 20 80

## Pessimistic Estimate (P) 7 20 140

Based on those three estimates, the optimistic estimate for the activity duration is 60 hours, the most-likely estimate is 80 hours, and the pessimistic estimate is 140 hours.

The table shows a three-point estimate for creating a training guide. The optimistic estimate shows an estimated 3 hours per procedure for a total of 60 hours; the most-likely estimate is 4 hours per procedure for a total of 80 hours; and the pessimistic estimate is 7 hours per procedure for a total of 140 hours.

The beta distribution allows you to combine the three estimates and emphasize the most-likely estimate.

By substituting the total hours from each of the three estimates into the equation, you arrive at a weighted average estimate of 86.67 or 87 hours.

Notice that Carol’s initial single point estimate of 80 hours is not significantly different from the beta distribution estimate of 87 hours. However, when there is uncertainty in the activity, the project manager is wise to use the beta distribution estimate that recognizes durations can vary depending on the conditions.

The activity estimate standard deviation would provide additional insights into the estimate.

This suggests that the actual activity duration will be between 74 and 100 hours (87 +/– 13).
