# S1 1.2 Project Management Principles

## Learning Objectives

Define projects, including their purpose.

## PMBOK 7

As you move forward on your project management journey, it is important to understand the principles, ethics, and professional conduct of project management practitioners. The Standard for Project Management, which is also known as PMBOK 7, presents 12 principles that anyone leading projects should be able to live by. They are not specific to methodologies and will work regardless of what delivery approach you choose to use. A principle is a fundamental truth, norm, or guide. Principles used in a profession serve as guidelines for strategy, decision-making, and problem-solving. Professional standards are based on principles, and in some professions, principles are based on laws or rules, making them prescriptive. Unlike principles within some professions, the 12 project management principles are not prescriptive. They are intended to guide the behaviors and actions expected to be present in the project manager, team, and project stakeholders.

12 Principles of Project Management Stewardship: Be a diligent, respectful and caring steward. Team: Create a collaborative team environment.

Stakeholders: Effectively engage with stakeholders. Value: Focus on value. Systems thinking: Recognize, evaluate, and respond to the system interactions. Leadership: Demonstrate leadership behaviors to support individual and team needs. Tailoring: Tailor based on context. Quality: Build quality into processes and products (deliverables). Complexity: Navigate complexity. Risk: Optimize risk response. Adaptability and Resiliency: Embrace adaptability and resiliency. Change: Enable change to achieve the envisioned future state.

## PMI Code of Ethics and Professional

## Conduct

Unlike the project principles that guide project management, the code of ethics and professional conduct defines the expectations for project management practitioners. According to the PMBOK 6th and 7th editions, this includes both aspirational ideas and mandatory behaviors. As a project management professional and practitioner, the four tenets of the code of ethics and professional conduct are responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty: 1. Responsibility is the duty to take ownership of the decisions made or failed to make as a practitioner, the actions you take or fail to take, and the consequences that result. 2. Respect is the duty to show a high regard for yourself, others, and the resources entrusted to you. Resources entrusted to you may include people, money, reputation, the safety of others, and natural and environmental resources. An environment of respect engenders trust, confidence, and performance excellence by fostering cooperation and an environment where diverse perspectives and views are encouraged and valued. 3. Fairness is your duty to make decisions and act impartially and objectively. Your conduct must be free from competing self-interest, prejudice, and favoritism.

4. Honesty is your duty to understand the truth and act in a truthful manner both in your communications and in your conduct.
