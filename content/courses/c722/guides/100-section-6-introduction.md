# Section 6: Introduction

## Section 6: Introduction

The image is a circular-shaped infographic illustrating six interconnected concepts that define the Project Scheduling within the broader scope of Project Management. At the center of the circular design is the title “Project Management,” surrounded by six evenly spaced nodes arranged in a closed loop, visually emphasizing the cyclical and continuous nature of project management processes. Starting at the top and moving clockwise, the first node is Project Selection Criteria, represented by a clipboard icon to indicate the evaluation and selection of projects based on strategic alignment. Next is the Project Life Cycle node, highlighted in green to signify its central role in tying all phases together. Following that is Project Management Methods, illustrated with a pencil and ruler icon, symbolizing structured frameworks like Agile or Waterfall. Continuing around the circle, the Project Planning node appears with a checklist icon, representing the organization of goals, timelines, and resources. This is followed by Project Scheduling, marked with a clock and calendar icon to reflect time coordination and milestone tracking. The final node is Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing Project Work, displayed with a gear and document icon, capturing the execution, supervision, and formal closing of the project. The circular shape, flowing structure, and consistent navy and green color scheme reinforce the continuous, interconnected nature of managing projects from initiation to completion. This section will build on the mechanics of project management that were established in the prior section. Specifically the work breakdown structure (WBS) and budget developed previously will be further enhanced by the addition of a schedule and network diagram. The work activities from the WBS that were previously budgeted will now be scheduled through a process of estimating the time required to complete the work and the sequence in which the work must occur. You will learn how the schedule is used to develop a visual depiction of the work called a network diagram. The network diagram is an important tool used to communicate how the work will flow during project execution. Additionally, you will see how to identify the critical path of activities whose schedule must be maintained in order to complete the project on time. Together with the planning tools presented in the prior section, this section will complete the development of the project plan. The approved scope, budget, and schedule will become the baseline used to measure project performance through project execution.
