# S7 1.3 Team Development- Colocated Versus Virtual

## Learning Objectives

Describe the characteristics of an effective project team.

## Team Development: Colocated Versus Virtual

The reality of today’s business world is that there are more and more project activities that require team effort. Further, the expertise required may not all be located at the same geographic location. Travel can be very expensive and disruptive to other work activities. Fortunately, you have technology that can lessen the need for travel. This gives rise to two types of team members:

- colocated
- virtual

It is possible that even with virtual teams, some team members may be colocated. However, for discussion purposes, we will consider a virtual team to be widely dispersed.

## Colocated Teams

Colocation involves team members physically working at the same location or holding project meetings together in a common setup. This tends to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of working relationships and improves communication and productivity. The colocated team is the more traditional—and in many ways easier to manage—format. Since team members are located in or near the same location, a team face-to-face meeting may only require a walk down a hallway to a conference room. Members are able to easily meet in person with one another at almost any time. On the positive side, a colocated team may allow the five team-building stages to progress more quickly and smoothly since team members have frequent interaction. On the negative side, human nature being what it is, more frequent interaction may also exacerbate team member conflicts.

## Virtual Teams

Virtual teams are teams whose members interact primarily through electronic communications. Members of a virtual team may be within the same building or across continents. There are a variety of venues and platforms designed for virtual teams to meet. These can run from relatively simple and inexpensive to complex and expensive to set up and operate. On the simple side are conference calls. Regular conference calls and online meetings help to clarify information and create the human interaction needed to create a team. Software is available to facilitate online meetings with voice and information sharing (e.g., Microsoft Teams, Skype, GoTo Meeting, Webex, etc.). The core to all of this is understanding everyone’s experience, ideas, and contributions. Always remember that you are dealing with people and that the needs of those people differ, but the project’s goals are specific and shared. With current technology, meetings may involve face-to-face or audio-only conversations or platforms for sharing presentations and documents in real time.

Regardless of which team meeting method is utilized, there is one very important issue that must be addressed. This issue is time zones. If, for instance, a virtual meeting is called for 10:00 a.m. Eastern time, it is 7:00 a.m. for a team member calling in from the Pacific time zone. This may seem minor, but it can lead to hard feelings and conflict if some group members feel that meeting times are determined only for the convenience of one group and not others. Another issue when dealing with team members from other geographies involves cultural differences. A team must recognize, acknowledge, and integrate people who have different value systems or behavioral norms based on their regions or cultures. Human behavior will affect the team’s ability to perform. While the platforms noted above can help virtual teams norm and storm, once the team has reached that point, they will probably not help getting to the perform stage. Virtual teams have their own set of challenges. The nature of virtual teams can make some team-building issues even more daunting than normal. Cultural differences can be an issue for any group. For virtual teams, it is even more so. Colocated team members have the advantage of being in close proximity to each other. Cultural issues may be less difficult to work through when people are in daily contact and can get to know each other. Virtual teams are not able to do this. Similarly, you must address communication issues. There are several techniques that work with virtual teams. What will work for you will depend on a number of factors, including the nature and makeup of the team, the time they will be working together, budget and schedule constraints, and factors that may be peculiar to your situation. One method to connect dispersed team members is a team newsletter or blog. This can introduce team members to each other and provide a way to keep each other up to date on key events. This has to be handled with great care because if there is any perception that a particular location is receiving a disproportionate amount of attention, the newsletter will be detrimental to team growth. If the project manager uses this type of communication method, it may be useful to have individuals contribute and talk about themselves and their cultures. Sometimes all that may be needed is for the team leader to take the time to visit each and every team location to assure everyone that their contributions are valued and needed. This will be very valuable if there are some team members who are far removed, geographically, from the rest of the team. It is easy for these people to feel neglected or ignored. A personal visit from the team leader can go a long way in making the team member feel valued. Another way is to schedule periodic team gatherings. This is time-consuming and may be costly and not always practical or appropriate. Due to geographically diverse locations, communication between virtual team members is critically important. Every effort must be made to assure and encourage open lines of communication. Communications must be open both horizontally and vertically. Vertical communication must be open both top-down and bottom-up and recognize time differences and specific special needs. One note of caution with virtual teams: It is possible that some members may be colocated. In this situation, it is possible for an "us versus them" attitude to develop. It is natural for colocated team members to form closer working bonds than with those team members who are remote. It is the team leader's responsibility to find methods to foster good working relationships among all team members, especially during the early stages of team development.
