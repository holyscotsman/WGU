# S5 1.3 The Purpose of the Project Scope Statement

## Learning Objectives

Describe how the scope of a project is developed.

## Project Scope Statement

The project scope statement represents a mutual understanding between the customer and the project team. Remember that during the defining and planning phases, the customer and the project team interact in order to ensure that the planned work will result in the customer’s expected project outcomes. During execution, the team will be busy doing the work to create the outcomes, so they must be confident that the planned work will result in the expected deliverable. If the plan is wrong or the deliverable was not described properly in the project scope statement, then the customer will probably not be happy with the result. A successful project begins with a clear understanding between the customer, or sponsor, and the project team regarding what the project will accomplish. The SOW can be used to create a high-level scope that will support the project proposal. Enough information is needed for the organization’s leadership to approve the project. The planning process will require a more extensive project scope statement so that the team is very clear on the work that needs to be included in the plan. The resulting plan will include the complete budget and schedule for the project. In the restaurant example, conceptual items are sufficient for project initiation. Information should be provided such as the type of food that will be served, the style of food, the decor, the approximate size of the kitchen, the size of the dining area, how many customers can be seated, whether a bar or dance floor will be included, and the number of restrooms. During the planning phase, much more detail will be needed to determine the real cost and time required and how you will meet customer expectations or government requirements. The team will need to know exactly what kitchen equipment will be required, which lighting fixtures, carpet, wall coverings, etc. If anything is omitted from the project scope statement, then it will not be included in the project plan. If it is not in the project plan, then it is not in the budget or the schedule, so it will not be done. If, for example, your new restaurant was intended to have a family restroom in addition to separate men’s and women’s restrooms, but it was not included in the scope, then it will not be in the finished restaurant.

In many instances, omitted items are identified during project execution. At that point, the scope, budget, and schedule will have been approved. The customer will need to determine if the oversight warrants a change to the plan through the change control process for scope, and the impacts to budget and schedule. It is in everyone’s best interest to carefully develop the scope during the planning phase.

## Collecting Requirements

A project requirement is a characteristic, function, or capability that must be present in the project's final outcome. The requirements are collected by the project team during the planning phase. Depending on the complexity of the project, the requirements can be extensive. Requirements come from the project stakeholders and will be used to create the project scope statement. The project team can glean high-level requirements from information included in the project charter. Developing an understanding of the project overview, business case (project justification), general objectives, risks, and constraints prepares the project team to meet with stakeholders to identify specific requirements. From this foundational understanding, the team then meets with project stakeholders to further define the requirements. Recall that stakeholders are individuals or organizations that are affected by the project or perceive that they will be affected by the project. That can be a wide range of individuals or organizations, so the project team needs to decide which stakeholders will provide requirements and how that information will be gathered, analyzed, and accepted or rejected. There are many tools for gathering information. Depending on the situation, surveys, interviews, focus groups, workshops, and group brainstorming techniques may be used. It is often incumbent on the project team to listen carefully and interact extensively with the stakeholders in order to develop specific and measurable requirements.

Once the requirements are gathered, they are documented in the requirements traceability matrix. The matrix not only includes the requirement, but it also identifies the business need that will be satisfied and the specific stakeholder who provided the requirement. The matrix can be expanded to include the name of a project team member who will be responsible for meeting the requirement, how the required outcome will be verified, and other pertinent information.

## Project Deliverables

Project deliverables are the features and functions of the project outcome that form the product scope. Recall that these describe what the project will create. Depending on the complexity of the project, the outcome may include several distinct and verifiable deliverables. The term is also used to describe the verifiable outcomes of a specific work activity (intermediate deliverables), which will contribute to the overall project outcomes. When you do your work breakdown, these deliverables will be broken into activities and tasks for assigning to the team members. This will be discussed further in the work breakdown structure section. In the restaurant example, one of the activities may be for the electrician to install high-efficiency lighting that can have its intensity adjusted using dimmers. When she has finished that work, you can verify that the lighting functions as required and has the desired effect by returning to your requirement document for the right measurement or stated expectation of the stakeholder. Deliverables are the outcomes that satisfy the requirements grouped together and identified by the stakeholders.

## Project Scope Statement

As was mentioned earlier, the project scope statement is a detailed description of the project outcomes. For small or routine projects, the project scope statement may be brief. Regardless of project size or complexity, the project scope statement must clearly identify the project deliverables and how their acceptance will be determined. The project deliverables come from the stakeholder requirements identified in the requirements traceability matrix. If some deliverables are created prior to delivery of the final project, their acceptance criteria must also be noted. The project scope statement should also identify the limitations or boundaries of the project. These would be listed as project exclusions. The listing of exclusions will help mitigate any assumptions on the part of the project team or the customer about what is or is not included in the scope. Too often, the customer assumes that the team will complete associated work that was not explicitly identified in the project scope statement. To protect the team and ensure that a clear and mutual understanding exists, exceptions should be noted.

In the restaurant example, the project scope statement may include the design, purchase, and installation of a lighted sign for the front of the building, but other marketing efforts may not be included. This should be noted in the project scope statement so that there is no assumption that other marketing work will be completed as part of the project team’s work. Creating the project scope statement is an iterative process between the project team and the customer. It can take several rounds of revisions to develop a document that satisfies both parties. The project scope statement will guide the team as they complete the detailed planning of the project work.

During the detailed planning, situations may be identified that require the project scope statement to be modified. However, at the conclusion of the planning phase, the project scope statement and project plan are approved and will not be changed without the agreement of both the customer and the project manager.
