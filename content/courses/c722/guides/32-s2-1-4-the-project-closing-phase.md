# S2 1.4 The Project Closing Phase

## Learning Objectives

Identify each phase of a project.

The phases occurring in the project are explained in the flowchart. The four phases are defining, planning, executing, and closing. In the closing phase, the project outcomes are delivered to the customer or sponsor. The project closing phase is when the project outcomes are delivered to the customer or sponsor. The customer or sponsor evaluates them to determine if they are acceptable: the deliverables are ready, the budget and time requirements are met, quality is achieved, and lessons learned are documented. When the customer or sponsor accepts the project outcome(s), the project manager must ensure that all the loose ends are tied up.

The project manager will need to do the following:

- reassign any project resources back to the company
- document the project results and lessons learned
- close out any procurement activities

verify that the completed project is transitioned over to the customer Remember that when projects officially end, the project manager and project team will transition to other assignments, so there will not be anyone around to complete documentation or approve invoices for payment. The team often gains experience during the project that can improve the success of future projects. These are documented in the lessons learned so that the host organization can learn from the positive and negative experiences of the team. During the closure phase, the customer may identify items that need to be corrected, which can require the team to replan parts of the project and then execute the revised activities to satisfy the expectations of the customer.

The customer’s acceptance of the project outcome and the host organization’s acceptance of all project documentation signals the completion of the project closing phase. Completing the project closing phase brings you to the end of the project life cycle.
