## Stakeholder Engagement

Stakeholders are included within the project management principles and the performance domain, focusing specifically on stakeholder engagement. Stewardship: Be a diligent, respectful, and caring steward. In this context, stewardship refers to being entrusted with the project's care, directing project management efforts toward practical planning and resource management. Stewardship encompasses responsibilities both internal and external to the organization, embodying integrity, diligence, trustworthiness, and adherence to regulations. It also involves maintaining ethical standards and values. Stakeholders: Stakeholders have the potential to impact a project in both positive and negative ways, which often hinges on their level of power and authority. Effective stakeholder engagement requires consistent communication, which is crucial for proactively identifying and mitigating risks that could compromise project commitments. Communication with project stakeholders, whether they are internal or external, is absolutely essential to any project. A stakeholder is anyone who has an impact on or is impacted by the project. Keeping stakeholders informed ensures the best support for the project. Stakeholders can include some or all of the following:

- senior management of the company and organization
- management and workers of all levels who will use the system

management and workers of all levels who will be on the project developing its deliverables suppliers and support groups in the organization or from the outside who will work on the project and create the resulting deliverables stockholders of the business any other partners or organizations that will be involved with the end project results It is a responsibility of the project manager (PM) to manage the interests of the stakeholders effectively. Some best practices for ensuring stakeholder engagement include the following: understanding each stakeholder’s level of engagement and how it might affect the project knowing how to communicate effectively with all levels of stakeholders Keep it simple, and explain needs and consequences thoroughly. Tell them clearly what you need them to know and do. Give them clear statuses of the project throughout its life. Develop communication plans with each stakeholder’s involvement and preferences in mind. Always write or speak with the listener or receiver of the information in mind. keeping stakeholders informed regularly by giving them the status and asking for decisions or resources as needed If they do not feel connected to the "flow" of the project, they will lose interest. managing stakeholder expectations and satisfaction

Stakeholders come in a variety of flavors and backgrounds. Beyond managing the team (who are stakeholders), managing the other people with a stake in the project is a very demanding part of the project leader’s job. Each person has a specific agenda, need for communication, and requirements from the PM. Project managers engage everyone by understanding their stake and obligations to the project. If a PM is starting a new business such as a restaurant, they set up a matrix of the stakeholders as soon as they begin.
