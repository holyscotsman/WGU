# S4 1.8 Extreme Project Management Methods

## Learning Objectives

Describe extreme project management methods.

## Extreme Project Management Methods

Extreme project management methods are approaches to address issues of extraordinary uncertainty or complexity. The traditional project management methods discussed in this lesson have a strong bias toward advance planning. Agile projects still have a plan but with more flexibility than traditional projects. In extreme projects, detailed planning is not as important since the team faces such uncertainty or complexity that they could not reasonably plan for every possible situation. The project team must complete the project without a comprehensive plan in place at the beginning of the project. Often the goal of an extreme project may be unclear. There must be a high-level goal for it to be considered a project, but it may be more of a general vision, which gradually becomes more specific and clearer as the project progresses. Consider the Deepwater Horizon oil spill, which poured oil into the Gulf of Mexico for several months. The vision or overarching goal was to stop the flow of oil, but there was no specific plan or actionable goal in the project. The team tried many approaches before finding one that worked. Extreme project management is often used in technology projects. This is due to the fast pace at which technology changes and the ease with which software can be modified. On a construction project, it can be difficult to make a major change to a building’s design halfway through it, but in a software project, new features or database tables can generally be added at any time. An example of an extreme project would be the controversial application of hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling to extract natural gas from shale over one mile below the earth’s surface. Imagine the complexity of drilling one mile down and then turning horizontally to drill multiple spokes out into the shale bed. The early projects to use this technology would not have known what to expect. That is where extreme project management methods come into play.

Fracking zone, oil pump with tank extracting petroleum from subsoil with pipes. jemastock/iStock / Getty Images Plus via Getty Images./Text removed In extreme project management, the focus is on human resources rather than planning. In order for these project teams to apply rapidly evolving technologies and quickly produce results, the team members must be able to collaboratively problem solve and apply expertise from their own respective fields. One of the most important players in an extreme project is the sponsor. The sponsor has an important role in helping to define and guide any project, no matter the methodology, but in a traditional project, they are involved more at the beginning and end while establishing the vision and plan for the project and verifying the completed scope of the final product. In extreme project management, however, the sponsor needs to be hands-on the entire time, as project goals and requirements are continuously refined throughout the entire life of the project. Doug DeCarlo, an expert in extreme project management, lists questions the project manager and sponsor should regularly revisit as they refine project goals:

- Who needs what and why?
- What will it take to do it?

Can we get what it takes? Is it worth it? The following are possible criteria by which to know that an extreme methodology would be appropriate: High stakes—failure is not an option. Deadlines are short. Innovation is paramount. Success is to be measured in bottom-line results. Bureaucracy cannot be tolerated. Quality of life is important.
