# S6 1 Introduction

## Learning Objectives

Consider the following learning objectives as you move through the lesson:

- Describe common techniques used to estimate activity duration.
- Construct an activity duration estimate.

As described in a previous section, the work breakdown structure (WBS) identifies the specific work activities necessary to complete the project. These activities are the starting point for developing the project schedule. Like much of project planning, developing the schedule is an iterative process. The team works from the WBS, risk register, and budget, but additional information will be identified during the development of the schedule that will require changes to those initial documents. This lesson will showcase the PMBOK 7 performance domains of Team, Development Approach, Planning, Delivery, Measurement, and Uncertainty. It will also focus on the processes used to estimate the time required to complete the work activities. A major component of the time estimates will be labor hours, which will feed back to the budgeting process to ensure that appropriate labor dollars have been allocated. Other time considerations include the following:

- time for the delivery of products (ordering/vendor lead time)

time for products to cure (drying time for paint and concrete) time to get necessary equipment put in place The activity durations will be a major input to the project schedule. Because the time component or schedule is part of the triple constraint, accuracy of estimates will also be addressed. The second major input to the project schedule is the sequence in which the activities will be completed. Activity sequencing will be discussed in an upcoming lesson. Why not just "play it safe" and overestimate the time required? In project management, you refer to that strategy as padding the estimates, and it is regarded as a bad practice. Padding was discussed in the budgeting lesson, and the same is true here. If the estimates are not accurate, then the plan will not be believable, and it will not be followed. If padding is done frequently, the credibility of the project manager is at risk. Furthermore, if padded estimates are accepted, then the team may allow the work to fill the allotted budget. On the other hand, if the culture of the organization is one of beatings if deadlines are not met, padding practices often become institutionalized.

## PMBOK 7 Performance Domains

Before moving on, let’s establish the PMBOK 7 performance domains.

## Team Domain

A project schedule is developed with a collaborative effort from the project team. Individual team members possess knowledge about resources, the time required to complete a task, and the task's interdependencies with other tasks. Every project team member must work with other team members to decide resource allocation, sequence tasks, and optimize the project duration. Experience and knowledge about similar projects executed in the past are of great help.

## Development Approach

Developing a project schedule differs significantly based on the project type, resource availability, and uncertainties associated with requirements and resources. In the predictive approach, the scope can be defined with less ambiguity at the start of the project, so a comprehensive and accurate schedule development is feasible through detailed scope planning and WBS creation. In turn, the WBS would also help determine the resources required and costs associated with each activity.

## Planning Domain

Deliverables influence schedule, so a predictive planning approach that creates a WBS broken down to the lowest-level deliverables could be relatively comprehensive and complete. In adaptive methods, high-level themes or epics are used to develop features that are further decomposed into stories and backlog items. A schedule is developed for each story, one at a time, to reduce waste and eliminate uncertainty. An adaptive approach uses incremental planning and often uses time boxes for scheduling. Work in each time box is based on a prioritized backlog.

## Delivery Domain

The WBS is used in a predictive plan approach to develop a comprehensive schedule so a realistic timeline for project deliverables can be developed. However, while using an adaptive approach wherein a full-blown schedule is not opted for, a checklist of all the criteria required to be met is developed so that a deliverable can be considered ready for customer use when the "definition of done" is fulfilled.

## Measurement Domain

A project schedule includes milestones along with a timeline and success criteria for project deliverables in a predictive approach. This schedule and timeline are used to measure progress. However, in an adaptive approach, you follow a different measurement of work progress based on a schedule developed for a story and a measurement of promised business value. In this approach, schedules prepared for a sprint are used for the measurement of progress.

## Uncertainty Domain

An effective schedule is not always possible when uncertainty exists in defining requirements. Ambiguity, volatility, complexity, and risk are some of the factors associated with requirements that may compel the project team to employ an adaptive approach of developing a schedule one story or sprint at a time.
