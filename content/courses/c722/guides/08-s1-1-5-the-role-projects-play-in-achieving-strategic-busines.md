# S1 1.5 The Role Projects Play in Achieving Strategic Business Initiatives

## Learning Objectives

Describe the role projects play in achieving strategic business initiatives.

## Business Strategy

Projects selected by an organization must be directly related to the organization’s strategy. Project alignment occurs when the planned and executed project agrees with the organization's broader strategic objectives. The project should consistently yield business outcomes that align with organizational goals, enhancing value and competitive edge in the market.

The commitment of resources to a project can be substantial, and the results of the project must support the organization's goals to ensure the organization's survival.

A business’s strategy consists of the activities, methods, and tactics that will be employed to meet the business's goals. These may include

increased market share and profitability through entry into a new market segment,

customer retention by providing the best customer services in the industry,

compliance with new and revised government requirements, and

reduced costs by providing the best tools and systems to operational personnel.

Strategy is determined by senior management in the organization to improve the value of the company to its stakeholders.

## Project Success and Its Determinants

It is important to recognize that a project must adhere to specific requirements regarding schedule, cost, and scope. These constraints are interdependent, necessitating trade-offs between them.

For example, adding resources to reduce the completion time of a project might lead to an increase in its overall cost. Time, cost, and performance constraints are known as the triple constraints. More specifically, the factors that limit a project's work options and actions include the predetermined completion schedule (time), the budget (cost), and the scope (performance).

Considering performance as work performance can be helpful. A very important factor to keep in mind is that projects carry out interrelated tasks, whereas each task within a project depends on or influences the completion of other tasks to produce the outcomes or deliverables that meet the customer's expectations and realize benefits.

In the most simplistic of definitions, meeting the triple constraints of scope, schedule, and budget is one way to measure the success of a project. That is, the project delivered what was specified in the project scope (product, service, result, etc.), within the allotted amount of time, and for the specified funding.

This is based on the premise that the scope, schedule, and budget were properly formulated, that they targeted a product or service that helps the organization meet its strategic business objectives, and that the product or service met a real-world need that customers would pay for. However, value is the ultimate measure of project success.

In addition, any activity that employs the skills and talents of SMEs (subject matter experts) should help to increase those skills and talents to make those SMEs a more valuable asset to the organization. Proper documentation of the project will also increase the "corporate memory" derived from lessons learned. In fact, even if a project is cancelled before completion, it may be successful if the SMEs learned and the organization's corporate knowledge level increased.

The groundwork for project success begins before the project is even approved. Some criteria for a successful project include the following:

The scope was met, but did it sufficiently support the desired outcome?

How well does the project meet the company's strategic objectives?

Does the company have experience with similar projects?

Will the company need to hire or contract with new SMEs or project management experts?

Will the company have the resources, money, people, facilities, and equipment to develop the project?

What is the competition for the product or service to be developed?

Is there a sufficient market for the company to recuperate its investment and make a profit?

What is the opportunity cost of this project?

Is this the best way for the company to expend its resources?

Does the company have all the needed resources: people, facilities, and equipment?

Does the company have all the needed resources to comply with new and revised legal and regulatory requirements?
