# Section 7: Introduction

## Section 7: Introduction

The image is a circular-shaped infographic illustrating six interconnected concepts that define the Executing, Monitoring, and Controlling, Closing Project Work within the broader scope of Project Management. At the center of the circular design is the title “Project Management,” surrounded by six evenly spaced nodes arranged in a closed loop, visually emphasizing the cyclical and continuous nature of project management processes. Starting at the top and moving clockwise, the first node is Project Selection Criteria, represented by a clipboard icon to indicate the evaluation and selection of projects based on strategic alignment. Next is the Project Life Cycle node, highlighted in green to signify its central role in tying all phases together. Following that is Project Management Methods, illustrated with a pencil and ruler icon, symbolizing structured frameworks like Agile or Waterfall. Continuing around the circle, the Project Planning node appears with a checklist icon, representing the organization of goals, timelines, and resources. This is followed by Project Scheduling, marked with a clock and calendar icon to reflect time coordination and milestone tracking. The final node is Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing Project Work, displayed with a gear and document icon, capturing the execution, supervision, and formal closing of the project. The circular shape, flowing structure, and consistent navy and green color scheme reinforce the continuous, interconnected nature of managing projects from initiation to completion. This section begins by addressing the important dynamics of team development. This section will discuss how some project teams work in the same physical location, while others will need to use technology to keep remote team members connected.

Two lessons are dedicated to the processes and tools used to monitor project performance. The first lesson introduces the iterative processes of monitoring and controlling that occur during project execution. The second lesson introduces the earned value management tools. Monitoring and controlling work together to ensure that the project is on track to meet its goals of time, cost, and scope. You will also learn the change management processes used to evaluate changes and communicate changes to stakeholders.

Earned value management analysis is a specific set of tools used by project managers to compare the work completed to the baseline schedule and budget. The analysis identifies variances that may prompt the project manager to revise the project plan or take other actions. The analysis also allows the project manager to predict how the project will end with regard to its budget target.

The section concludes with a lesson covering the processes necessary to close the project. Project closure includes turning over the project outcomes to the customer and completing all documentation.
