# S7 2 Summary

## Summary

This lesson explained the iterative processes related to monitoring and controlling project work during project execution. Monitoring relates to comparing actual performance to the baseline schedule, budget, and scope. If substantial differences are identified, then the project manager will control the project work by revising the project plan, taking corrective action, or negotiating changes to the baselines. Risk and quality must also be monitored during project execution. The risk register is reviewed to determine if the risk event has already passed or if an action must be taken to respond to a risk that has come to fruition. Quality is monitored to ensure that project outcomes will meet customer expectations. Change management processes ensure that potential changes to the project plan are identified and properly evaluated before being implemented. If the team arbitrarily changes the project plan without proper evaluation and approval, scope creep occurs. Change management also drives the communication and implementation of approved changes. Integrated change control integrates the change control process within the project to utilize established elements within the project plan, such as the original scope statement, established baseline, identified risks, and predetermined contingency plans. A structured change management approach, consisting of four essential phases—prepare for the change, plan, manage, and reinforce and sustain the change—linked with the project processes, is necessary for successful project management. The lesson concluded with a rigorous discussion of the three aspects of quality management: quality assurance, quality control, and quality management and the project performance domains integrated within these quality elements. The next lesson will introduce the tools of earned value

management, which is a method of measuring and assessing project schedule and cost performance.
