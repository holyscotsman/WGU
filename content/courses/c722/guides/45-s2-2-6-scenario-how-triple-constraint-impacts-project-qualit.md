# S2 2.6 Scenario: How Triple Constraint Impacts Project Quality

## Learning Objectives

Describe how triple constraint impacts project quality.

An equilateral triangle with the word Quality inside. The sides are labeled scope, cost, and schedule to indicate their equal importance to quality. The scope is labeled on the left side, cost on the right side, and the schedule as the baseline. Here is the triple constraint triangle. Being an equilateral triangle, all sides are equal in length, symbolizing that scope, cost, and schedule are balanced. The area inside the triangle represents the level of quality. And, with the scope, cost, and schedule as shown, you get the quality desired. What if the schedule decreases? This is shown by shortening the schedule line.

The length of the schedule line is too short and doesn't connect with scope or cost. Due to the schedule change, the contents and quality can be lost. We no longer have an equilateral triangle. The length of the schedule line is too short and does not connect with scope or cost. What will happen? The contents—quality—can be lost. The same thing happens in your project. If you shorten the schedule, you need to look at its impact on the project and possibly the impact on quality of the project. If you have less time due to a schedule change, you look to see if it is a shorter calendar time or fewer work hours. To meet a sooner due date, you need to reduce scope or functions or increase resources, thus increasing costs. The concept around triple constraint is that no decision can be made without looking at the impact on the project and its quality. Interrelationships are vital when making a decision. Now, you will look at increase in the scope (scope creep).

The line representing the scope extends beyond where it connects with cost and with schedule. This is a visualization of the fact that unless cost or schedule are increased also, not all of the scope increase can be included or quality might shrink. As shown in the following visualization, quality no longer fits in the triangle. This is meant as a reminder that when you make decisions, quality standards must be reviewed and the impact of the change understood.

The cost side and the schedule side are reduced in the triangle. Therefore, there is inequality. Cost and schedule are not sufficient to create an equilateral triangle needed for quality.

If you simply reduce the cost and the schedule lines so they now both connect with the ends of the scope line but not each other, you see that there is an opening in the triangle. Quality can "leak out" because of the disruption in the balance of the triangle. These are only a sample of a number of scenarios that could occur. But this provides a good visualization that if one of the three constraints is changed, then one—or both—of the other two constraints must be changed to maintain the same quality level or quality can be compromised.

## Scenario: The Impact to Project Scope

Let's revisit the vacation home scenario to examine the triple constraints further. For the sake of simplicity and better understanding, each of these three conditions will be considered separately. In the real world, these do not happen independently. You have given further consideration to your vacation home. You decide that because so much time, money, and effort are going into it, that you will build it such that it could also serve as a retirement home. This changes the project scope. You consider the changes desired or required: It is undetermined when, or if, there will be a natural gas line run to provide heat. You consider adding a secondary liquified natural gas (LNG) storage. It is also undetermined when or if power lines will be run. And, if they are run above ground, they may be subject to storm damage. You add a gas-powered generator and a fuel tank large enough to hold a winter's worth of fuel. You add a wood-burning stove, as a backup heat source. And you increase the size of the woodshed to hold a winter’s worth of wood. You also have other unexpected issues, including the delay in water and sewer lines and the environmental impact of the asphalt road. How will these scope changes affect cost and schedule? Because you expect to keep this for a retirement home, you do not want to compromise quality, and you must meet government health and safety regulations. If you attempt to get everything completed this year, costs will skyrocket. There is a limit to your ready cash and a limit on how much you are willing to borrow. So you look at your schedule. What can be postponed?

First, what must be done this year? the house itself health and sanitation concerns environmental and safety regulations the access road You decide to use lake water and install a residential water purification system. You also go with an underground septic system for wastewater. Both are designed with the capability to connect to a main if it is even run to your property. You postpone the boat dock. You only install a single LNG tank at this time. You also decide on a primary and secondary fuel storage system and only do the primary at this time. Because you are postponing secondary LNG and fuel storage, you investigate alternate energy sources. You decide to install electric heating and use wind power. The gas heat and the generator will be backups. The wind-powered generator can be built next season, as long as the house is already wired for it. You research the attributes of the three road surfaces you are considering and discover that if a graded packed dirt road is put in now, it can be done so that either gravel or asphalt can be put over the dirt at a later date, thus reducing your immediate cost and postponing additional cost till some future date. You initially planned on area rugs in all the rooms. You decide that it can be postponed until a later date. You also determine you do not need to fully furnish the entire house immediately. You do build the three-car garage but delay the snowmobile shed. You can temporarily store the snowmobiles in the garage. So what have you seen here? The scope was increased due to a decision to expand the purpose of the vacation home to also be a retirement home. To keep quality high, some adjustments had to be made to control schedule and cost. Cost could not increase uncontrollably. However, some immediate cost increase was accepted, as well as postponing some costs until later. The postponement of some costs was accomplished by rescheduling some work to a future date. This postponed work was determined by prioritizing what had to be completed immediately and what could, comfortably and safely, be delayed. In addition, some work, such as road surfacing, would be done in stages over several years.
