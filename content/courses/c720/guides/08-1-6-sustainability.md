# 1.6 Sustainability

### Essential Question

What role does sustainability play in an organization's efforts toward economic profitability, social responsibility, and environmental responsibility?

## Sustainability

Sustainability reflects the efforts organizations are expected to make to balance their interconnected obligations to economic viability, the societies in which they operate, and the natural environment. It may manifest in a variety of initiatives, including minimizing or mitigating environmental pollution, and may range to such efforts as employee wellness programs. Sustainability is a key ethical issue in operations and supply chain management impacting organizations and operations. Sustainability can also relate to the so-called "triple bottom line" of economic profitability, social responsibility, and environmental viability. Sustainability should be contrasted with vulnerability and, as such, should be perceived as a risk management strategy.

## Profitability

Sustainability considers the long-term benefits of actions over any short-term cost savings. The sustainability of operations involves improving efficiency to reduce costs, utilize available capacity, and achieve business objectives. Efficient use of resources means getting as much positive return from them as possible. Effective management of equipment and infrastructure is vital to long-term sustainability. Equipment and premises must be maintained to increase their useful working life, capability, and operational capacity. Although maintenance can be expensive in the short term, it will pay off over the life of the asset. Managing resources, equipment, and infrastructure allows lower prices to consumers while maintaining profit margins, providing a sustainable source of revenue.

## Environmental Sustainability

Effective management results in the maximum potential from the skills, knowledge, and experience of human resources. Recruiting the right people for the right jobs and providing them with the necessary support and responsibility to work efficiently is important to long-term sustainability. Transparency and fairness of policies and equal treatment for all employees reduce the drama of labor negotiations. Mutual trust from labor and management eliminates the need for third-party representation. Above all, a safe workplace helps ensure that employees remain healthy from day to day. The result is a long- term resource of viable employees.

Environmental sustainability is another important part of operations management. The public expects responsible operations from organizations in their communities. Governments are increasingly implementing regulations that force businesses to comply with environmental policies. Any business that operates without considering how to reduce its environmental impact will struggle to remain sustainable.

Sustainability and operations management are related by integrating the three elements into the organization's strategy. Sustainable operations are measured using a different cost-benefit ratio compared to non-sustainable operations, including environmental sustainability. Profitability and financial longevity can be enhanced using total quality management techniques. Labor resource viability can be achieved through a culture of workforce development and process safety— recognizing through corporate policies and actions that employees are the most important resource. Environmental stewardship is the third element and is operationalized through recycling programs, renewable resources, minimizing energy consumption, minimizing the use of toxins, reducing the supply chain, and prolonging product life.

Effective operations strategies consider how business objectives will impact the viability and success of the organization. The new challenge for operations and supply chain management is how to "operationalize" the long-term objectives that will leverage the organization toward sustainability. Managers must balance making decisions that offer a short-term gain with long-term implications.
