# 3.1 Inventory Management

### Essential Question

What is the role of inventory management in operations systems, and what are some of the primary types of inventories to consider?

## Purpose of Inventory

Inventory can help businesses meet demand and work more efficiently. For many items, it does not make sense to produce them only when there is demand. When a customer walks into a retail store to buy groceries or cosmetics, they expect to walk out of the store with the items. When customers walk into a dealer's showroom to buy a car, they do not want to wait until the car is produced; they want to drive it away. Managing inventory properly involves analyzing proper demand patterns. Demand patterns can help to manage risk, predict future demand, and identify opportunities. For example, a lawn and garden center manager can use demand patterns to have inventory for the lawn and garden season. This manager can have the proper amount of lawn supplies for the spring planting season, have organic lawn supplies to accommodate a potential trend, and limit purchases of certain types of mulch to align with the cyclic demand that shows a move toward rubber mulch. Demand patterns are regular fluctuations in demand for a product or service, often caused by seasons, cycles, and trends. The proper analysis of demand patterns enables a manager to reduce costs, increase profits, and enhance customer service. In short, the manager who properly analyzes demand patterns, seasonal demands, cyclical demands, and trends can gain a huge competitive advantage in their industry. Furthermore, this responsible predictive action for these three predictable patterns helps provide a hedge against the fourth demand pattern—random demand. Consider the following primary demand patterns: 1. Random demand is unpredictable demand that does not follow a pattern; for example, demand for goods generated by a pandemic. 2. Cyclical demand is a demand that occurs over a number of years and is generally predictable when a bigger picture of the market is taken into account. The housing market is an example of cyclic demand. 3. Seasonal demand occurs when shoppers adjust their purchase velocity in line with holidays, especially Christmas. In preparation for such events, companies may need to carry additional inventory. Other holidays or seasons can also create seasonal demand for certain kinds of merchandise, for example the increased sales of snow shovels in the winter. 4. Trend demand is a general regular uptick or downward flow trend over time. Organic food companies and other organic products show an upward trend in movement as the drive for healthy alternatives has become more popular.

A summary of the of 4 types of demand patterns: trend, cycle, seasonal, and trend with seasonal.

Many firms keep a cushion or safety stock of inventory to protect against unexpected demand. Keeping a safety stock of inventory is also useful when shipments from suppliers are delayed. This allows the firm to meet customer demand even if a supplier cannot meet its lead-time commitment. Safety stock is also a guard against random demand in the case of a rush on the company's product line. Situations may arise where companies may choose to retain surplus inventory. For example, quantity discounts or transportation discounts may entice a company to purchase in bulk. A firm may need a certain number of parts, but it may decide to buy more because a supplier is offering a discount for purchasing a larger amount. This is like buying a larger box of laundry detergent because it costs less per unit than a smaller box. Shipping in a certain amount may substantially reduce transportation costs, as well. A company may try to hedge against possible price increases. When an increase in the purchase price is anticipated by the company or announced by the supplier, the company may order additional materials prior to the increase. This is similar to the quantity discount. Other times, firms may want to hedge against uncertainty in supply. A supplier may announce downtime for its operations, or a supplier may have an upcoming contract negotiation with its labor union. Maintaining extra inventory on hand may be prudent.

Inventory Types "Inventory is the accounting of items, component parts, and raw materials that a company either uses in production or sells. As a business leader, you practice inventory management in order to ensure that you have enough stock on hand and to identify when there are shortages" (Jenkins, 2023). There are some who list as many as 13 different types of inventories; however, others list different numbers of types of inventories. Raw materials: These parts and materials are obtained from suppliers and are used in the production process. Work-in-process (WIP): These are partly finished parts, components, subassemblies, or modules. Finished goods: These items are ready to ship to the customer. No more work is required. Maintenance, repair, and operations (MRO) supplies: MRO inventory consists of items necessary to support day-to-day operations but does not directly contribute to the final product. These items are maintained to replace other parts of machinery or equipment, as well as other items like lubricant and cutting tools that are consumed in the production process. "Emphasis on

maintenance, repair, and operations inventory (MRO inventory) can lead to improvements in inventory control and productivity while reducing operational costs" (Siponen et al., 2019). Transit (pipeline) inventory: This is the portion of inventory in transit from a manufacturer to a distribution center or customer.
