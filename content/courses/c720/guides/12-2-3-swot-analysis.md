# 2.3 SWOT Analysis

### Essential Question

How is a SWOT analysis beneficial for organizations at all levels and of all sizes?

## SWOT Analysis

SWOT analysis is a simple but useful technique for analyzing an organization's strengths and weaknesses and the opportunities and threats that it faces. It helps a company focus on its strengths, minimize threats, and take the greatest possible advantage of opportunities available. "SWOT analysis has been extended beyond companies to countries and industries and is used in virtually every published business case positioned for business student analysis" (Helms & Nixon, 2010). SWOT analysis is a useful framework for understanding the strengths and weaknesses internal to an organization and for identifying both the opportunities and the threats related to external factors. Identification of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats is important because SWOT analysis can assist in future planning to achieve objectives.

The SWOT concept with an overlay of internal and external parts.

SWOT analysis includes the following four components: strengths: the internal characteristics of a business or project that lend an advantage within the scope of the study weaknesses: internal characteristics of a business or project result in a disadvantage relative to others opportunities: external elements that the business or project could exploit to its advantage threats: external elements in the competitive environment that could create trouble for the business or project

## Determining SWOT

## Determining Strengths

Consider strengths from both an internal perspective and from the point of view of customers or people in the industry or market. Strengths should be perceived in relation to other competitors. For example, if all competitors provide high-quality products, then a high-quality production process is not a strength but a necessity. The characteristic that makes the business or product unique among competitors is a strength. Ask the following questions when determining strengths:

- What internal advantages does the organization have?
- What are the core competencies of the business?
- What unique or lowest-cost resources are controlled?

What do people in the marketplace perceive as the organization's strengths or reputation? What factors make the organization unique?

## Determining Weaknesses

Consider weaknesses from an internal basis: Do other people perceive unknown weaknesses? Are other competitors doing any better than expected? When developing candidates for weaknesses, it is best to be realistic and accept any unpleasant truths. Ask the following questions when determining weaknesses:

- What internal elements could be improved?
- What should be avoided?
- What are people in the market likely to perceive as weaknesses?
- What factors result in lost sales?

## Determining Opportunities

Useful opportunities are external and can come from changes in social patterns, population profiles, lifestyle changes, changes in government policy, or changes in technology or markets. Ask the following questions when determining opportunities:

- What do good external opportunities look like?
- Which demographics are underserved in the competitive landscape?

What interesting trends might influence an organization's products or services? What are the potential positive effects of government measures or regulations?

## Determining Threats

Threats are external. Sometimes they can be managed or eliminated. Threats can bring harm to an organization and should be taken seriously. Ask the following questions when determining threats:

- What are the external obstacles to fair competition?
- What are other competitors doing?

What are the potential negative effects of government measures or regulations? Are quality standards or specifications for products or services changing? Is technology changing the way customers consume products or services?

## Utilizing SWOT Analysis

To determine an organization's competitive advantage using SWOT analysis, participants need to generate meaningful information for each strength, weakness, opportunity, and threat and make the analysis discernible and effective. SWOT analysis can be used as a simple tool to begin strategy formulation or as a more sophisticated strategy tool. What makes SWOT analysis particularly powerful is that, as a simple tool, it can uncover opportunities to exploit within the competitive landscape.
