# Lesson 4 Introduction

For manufacturing companies, wholesale and retail organizations, and food service providers, inventory represents a significant investment in working capital and a substantial cost to store and manage. When firms hold large amounts of inventory, they incur increased costs associated with holding inventory. Conversely, when firms hold small amounts of inventory, they must make frequent orders and accept the risk of being unable to satisfy customer demand. Making small and frequent orders tends to increase transaction, transportation, and equipment set-up costs. This is a fundamental trade-off in managing inventory.

Factors that impact this trade-off are discussed in this lesson. Selecting the appropriate inventory management model is important to help companies determine how much inventory should be ordered to minimize costs and when to order inventory so that the desired level of customer service can be provided. Determining how much inventory to order and when to order is key to successful inventory management for operations and supply chain managers.

## Learning Objective

Consider the following learning objective as you move through the lesson:

Describe how inventory management models are used in designing, planning, and managing operations to support organizational goals.
